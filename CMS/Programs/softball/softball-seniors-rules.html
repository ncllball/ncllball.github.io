Senior League Softball is a real, national Little League division for league ages 13–16. District 8 doesn't run a Senior softball regular season or district tournament, so NCLL forms a team with nearby leagues: registration is a waitlist, a team runs only if enough players sign up, and the season starts in June, after high school softball. An All-Star tournament team, if one forms, is separate (see G10). This page covers what's confirmed about the division nationally, and what NCLL still needs to settle.

Where these rules come from

  • Little League The 2026 Little League Softball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies, plus the 2026 Tournament Rules for Softball. Published by Little League (littleleague.org).
  • NCLL North Central Little League's own local rules.
  • Note There's no District 8 content on this page — District 8 softball stops at Juniors.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League League age 13–16 is the Senior League band, fixed nationally — the same way baseball's is. A local league may allow an assessed 12-year-old to participate in or dual-roster into Senior League. Junior League is 12–14, with a local option to allow an assessed 15-year-old to play Juniors for the regular season only (a local board may bar them from pitching) (Regulation I(a)).
  • NCLL League ages 13–16, the same as the national Senior League band.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL Senior League softball most closely mirrors high-school-style play under the Little League structure, while retaining Little League's community, inclusion, and development intent.

B. Field & equipment

What to buy and what the league provides: Equipment Hub.

B1–B2. Base paths & pitching distance

  • Little League 60-foot base paths and a 43-foot pitching distance — the same as Juniors (Rules 1.04 and 1.07).

B3–B4. Ball & bat standard

  • Little League A 12-inch ball, the same as Majors and Juniors (Rule 1.09). One softball bat standard applies at every level, including Senior League — non-wood bats must carry the current certification mark, maximum 34 in. length and 2¼ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Softball has no separate bat standard at the top of its ladder the way baseball's Senior division does.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • Little League 7 innings is regulation, the same as Juniors (Rule 4.10(a)).

D4. Mercy rule

  • Little League A full 3-tier structure, the same as Juniors: 15 runs after 4 innings (3½ if home ahead), 10 runs after 5 innings (4½ if home ahead), or 8 runs after 6 innings (5½ if home ahead). A local league may opt not to use this rule at all (Rule 4.10(e)).
  • NCLL The recovered source page's "10-run rule after 5 innings, 4½ if home ahead" is the middle tier of the Little League structure above — correct, not a conflict.

D6. Ties

  • Little League — regular season A tie after 7 completed innings doesn't trigger a special tie-breaker — play simply continues until the visiting team leads at the end of a complete inning, or the home team scores the winning run (Rule 4.10(b)).
  • Little League — tournament The international tie-breaker rule — starting the inning after regulation with a runner placed on second base — is a tournament rule, not a regular-season one. It applies to All-Star tournament play, not the June season (Tournament Rule 14).

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League A runner may not leave a base until the pitch is released, the same timing as Majors and Juniors (Rule 7.13).

D14. Sliding

  • Little League Headfirst sliding while advancing is permitted, same as Juniors — this flip stays on above Juniors rather than reverting (Rule 7.08(a)(4)).

E. Roster & lineup

E1. DP/Flex

  • Little League Senior League tournament play is the one level in softball where a Designated Player/Flex arrangement is actually available (Rule 3.03), rather than a continuous batting order. Softball-wide, DP/Flex can't be combined with a continuous batting order — District 8 mandates a continuous batting order everywhere it runs interleague, but no District 8 Senior softball structure exists to impose that mandate here. Whether an NCLL Senior Softball team would run a continuous batting order, like every other NCLL softball division, or a traditional lineup with DP/Flex available, is genuinely undecided — no source found answers this. It's the softball equivalent of the same open question on NCLL's baseball Senior team.

G. Season structure

G1. How the division runs

  • NCLL Registration is a waitlist, and a team runs only if enough players sign up. NCLL partners with nearby leagues to form a team. The season starts in June, after high school softball, and fields are set by the partner league.
  • District 8 No Senior softball regular season or district tournament.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League Senior League Softball is a real, chartered national division with its own World Series and tournament-eligibility rules, entirely separate from District 8. The specific bracket path (State, then Western Region, then the Senior League Softball World Series in Lower Sussex, Delaware) isn't stated in the rulebook itself — tournament bracket assignments live in a separate Little League document — so treat that path as likely but not yet independently confirmed.
  • NCLL If NCLL fields an All-Star team, it's listed as a possible "co-league" combination on the All-Star eligibility list — it depends on a coach and enough players, so it isn't a guaranteed team every year. Whether a fielded team gets a direct bye to State (the way NCLL's baseball Seniors team does) or follows some other qualifying path isn't documented anywhere found.

H. Conduct & safety

H1–H4. League-wide policies

  • NCLL If a team is fielded, the same league-wide policies apply as every other NCLL team: background checks and Abuse Awareness Training for volunteers, Positive Coaching Alliance standards for managers, the 30/30 weather policy, the concussion and injury protocol, and the Sport Parent Code of Conduct for spectators.

What NCLL still needs to settle

  • Which partner league NCLL teams up with, and whose local rules (batting order, time limits) apply in the June season.
  • If an All-Star team is fielded, exactly what tournament path it follows to get to State.
