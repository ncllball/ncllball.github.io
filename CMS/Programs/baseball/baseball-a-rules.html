Following are guidelines for the A Division level of play. We're a development league here — the goal is to prepare players to move up the Little League ladder and eventually reach the Majors.

Where these rules come from

  • Little League The 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book").
  • NCLL North Central Little League's own local rules.
  • Note A Division has no District 8 content on this page — District 8 interleague play doesn't start until AAA.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League Any player who turns 7 before September 1, 2026 and does not turn 13 before August 31, 2026 is eligible for Minor League (Regulation IV(a)) — subject to the local league's own alignment of that division; 5- and 6-year-olds may advance in after a year of Tee Ball. NCLL's A/AA split is exactly that alignment.
  • NCLL League ages 7–8 is the target band, with 6-year-olds who've completed a year of Tee Ball encouraged to register.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League Minor League exists to prepare children for eventual selection to a Major Division team — A Division is the entry point into that ladder.
  • NCLL A development-focused division whose primary goal is preparing players to move up the ladder and eventually reach the Majors.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 60-foot base paths — the standard Minor League diamond shared with AA and Tee Ball (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance & mound

  • Little League 46 feet — the Minor League mound distance shared by A, AA, and Tee Ball (Rule 1.07).
  • NCLL The pitching machine is set up just in front of the pitching rubber closest to home plate; exact placement may need adjusting depending on the specific machine.

B3. Ball standard

  • NCLL The Official RIF5 safety ball — a squishy, reduced-impact-force ball designed for young players, not a standard hardball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League Non-wood bats must carry the USABat mark; BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited; maximum 33 in. length and 2⅝ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not.
  • NCLL Look for a bat about 24–27 inches long and 13–17 oz. A batter should be able to hold it with only their top hand and move it across their body without dipping. Watch for oversized "cool-looking" bats that aren't actually sized for the player.

B5a. Gloves

  • NCLL Gloves should be 9–11 inches, with the player's name and phone number on them. Specialty gloves (first base, catcher) are usable but discouraged — usually too large for an A-level player.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League Same NOCSAE standard as every division (Rule 1.16).
  • NCLL Check helmets for cracks or missing pads. A player with lice concerns about shared helmets may bring their own, subject to the manager's final say on whether player-owned equipment meets safety standards.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League A local league may use adult pitch or machine pitch in Minor League games for players league ages 7–12; a pitching machine must be in good working order and operated only by an approved adult (Regulation VIII(d)) — the rulebook sets no velocity, distance, or model requirement beyond that.
  • NCLL Spring-arm mechanical pitching machines — NCLL's model is the Louisville Slugger UPM 45 "Blue Flame." A coach or parent from the batting team operates it via a foot pedal that releases the spring-loaded arm; only coaches or parents should operate it. Coaches adjust velocity and trajectory by player skill level, aiming for a slight arc landing just behind home plate. The home team's manager configures the machine before the game, typically at slower speeds with a moderate trajectory. If the machine is broken or unavailable, a coach or parent pitches to their own team from the same rubber position instead.

C2. Coach & machine mechanics

  • NCLL A batted ball that strikes the pitching machine stays in play.

C3. Catching

  • NCLL There's no pitch-count or catcher-restriction concept at this level — there's no player pitcher to protect. The defensive team provides a catcher; coaches make sure the catcher is in a squatting position facing the machine before each pitch.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • Little League A regulation game is six innings (Rule 4.10(a)).

D2. Time limit

  • NCLL 75 minutes — no new inning may start after that. Little League doesn't set a fixed time limit at this level; this is NCLL's own choice.

D5. Runs per inning

  • NCLL A 5-run cap per inning — once 5 runs score, the inning ends and the batting team returns to the field.

D6a. Strikeouts & courtesy strike

  • NCLL Three swinging strikes is an out; a batter must swing at the third strike — there's no called third strike, consistent with machine pitch having no true "balls." A foul tip doesn't count as a third strike; the batter stays alive at the plate.
  • Note A courtesy-strike option (not a rule — a suggestion): for players needing more development time, consider a 4-strike count for the whole team early in the season, transitioning to 3 strikes as the season progresses, discussed with the opposing manager before each game. Discontinue it once a team clearly outperforms its opponent, or builds a 6-or-more-run lead.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League No on-deck position below Majors (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1) — there's no warm-up circle, so the next batter waits in the dugout.
  • NCLL Bats stay on the fence outside the dugout.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL First offense: warning. Second offense: the player is removed from the game for safety, at the umpire's or managers' discretion.

D16. Advancing on a hit or error

  • NCLL Real base running is taught here, unlike Tee Ball — not every hit is a single; a legitimate double earns two bases, a triple earns three. Get out of the box quickly and look to the base coach. On an error, a runner is encouraged to take just one base even when more might be possible, as a courtesy to the opposing team while it develops.

E. Roster & lineup

E3. Mandatory play

  • NCLL Aim for equal innings in both the outfield and infield for every player — there's no need for position specialization at this level. On the batting side, coaches aim to get each player at least two at-bats, though that's acknowledged as harder to hit in pitching-duel or shorter 4–5-inning games.

E4. Number of fielders

  • NCLL A team may field 10 defensive players — one extra beyond the standard 9 — with the extra player restricted to the outfield (left, left-center, right, or right-center). It's fine for a 10-fielder team to play a 9-fielder opponent; this gives more playing time without meaningfully affecting competitiveness.

F. Game operations

F2. Umpiring

  • NCLL The defensive team provides a field umpire — a coach or parent — positioned around second base, with final say on field calls. There's no dedicated plate umpire at A Division, unlike every division above it. The coach who's pitching is responsible for tracking strikes during play, and should confer with the field umpire on any disputed calls.

F6. Score & standings

  • NCLL A Division doesn't keep score at all, and there are no standings — even though we don't keep score, there will always be individual and team moments worth celebrating.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • NCLL Each team gets 10 minutes to warm up before first pitch; the visiting team warms up first, then the home team. If 20 minutes isn't available before the scheduled start, the two teams agree on a shorter warm-up.

G. Season structure

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 2–3 events per week. Team formation lands in early March, practice starts mid-March, games start mid-April, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL Entirely in-house — no interleague and no District 8 involvement at this level.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • NCLL Same league-wide requirement as every division — anyone involved with players on a regular basis completes a background check and is cleared by NCLL. The same Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) standards apply league-wide.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
