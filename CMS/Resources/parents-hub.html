What Is a Club Team?

Exploring the “ifs” and “whos” of committing to a club/select/travel team can be daunting. You’re not alone; lots of us have been there.

This guide will help you demystify how club/select works alongside Little League, costs, travel, and fit for your player.

What Is It?

Select, club or travel: a team you try out for, built for players who want to put more into, and get more out of, baseball and softball.

What your player gets: more practice and coaching, tougher weekend tournaments, and a step toward high school ball.

What it takes: more time, travel and money.

How Is It Different?

Recreational Leagues (Little League, PONY)
  • Tee Ball from age 4, then coach pitch and player pitch.
  • No tryouts: neighborhood teams with volunteer coaches.
  • Everyone plays every game.
  • Fundamentals and fun, for far less than club.
  • Want more? Summer All-Stars is a first taste of tournament ball.

How Is It Different?

Club Programs
  • More competitive, with tryouts.
  • More practice and travel, so more cost in money and in time.
  • Most players start between 9 and 12, though more now start at 7 or 8.
  • The goal is often to develop players for high school and college ball.
  • Most clubs have several teams for different skill levels.

When Should We Start?

Most players join a club between 9 and 12. Some start at 7 or 8, but most of us at NCLL think that’s early, and young softball club teams are hard to find.

There’s no rush. The best thing you can do at that age is help your player love the game. That passion carries them through tough losses and the extra work of club ball.

How Do Ages Work?

  • Little League baseball: age on August 31, called league age.
  • Travel baseball (USSSA, Perfect Game, PONY): April 30.
  • Softball: travel mostly August 31; Little League, December 31 of the year before.

So a May–August birthday makes your player a year older in Little League than in travel baseball. See every organization’s age chart.

What Do the Names Mean?

Little League All-Stars teams go by league age: 10s, 11s, 12s.

Travel teams use a capital U, for “under”: a 10U team is players 10 and younger on the cutoff date.

So a June birthday could mean the 11s in All-Stars but 10U in travel ball.

Before You Commit

  • Weigh physical, emotional and social readiness.
  • Leave time for school and friends.
  • Check each club’s tryouts and costs, in money and in time.
  • Ask coaches and parents who know your player.

Where Do We Start?

NCLL often hosts clinics and events with local clubs: a low-pressure way to meet coaches, let them see your player, and find out if your child is ready (and wants it). Watch the Player Development Hub.

The clubs on the next panels are a starting point; NCLL doesn’t endorse any of them. Questions? Ask our Baseball VP or Softball VP.

All volunteers (coaches, team parents, board members, etc.) must complete a volunteer application annually. Choose the appropriate form based on your status:

  • New Volunteers : Complete the full Volunteer Application which includes a background check authorization
  • Returning Volunteers : If you volunteered last year, use the Basic Volunteer Application
