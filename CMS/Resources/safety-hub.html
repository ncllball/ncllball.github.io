Each season our elected Safety Officer submits NCLL’s ASAP (A Safety Awareness Program) plan to Little League International. Below is the annual letter to families plus quick links to the core resources you’ll need throughout the year.

Letter from the Safety Officer

Dear NCLL families,

Player safety is our first priority, from Tee through Teen. Our board, coaches, umpires, and volunteers follow Little League International’s A Safety Awareness Program (ASAP) and update our plan every year before Opening Day. That plan includes coach certifications, concussion and abuse-awareness training, heat/lightning protocols, field inspections, and rapid incident reporting.

Thank you for partnering with us: reviewing our safety links, completing required trainings, and helping us keep every practice and game welcoming and secure. If you have questions or see something we need to address, please reach out anytime.

Play hard, be safe, and see you at the Cloverleaf!

Michael Fishback
Safety Officer, North Central Little League
safety@ncllball.com

Incident Reporting

Someone hurt? Take care of them first, then report it.

  • Emergency: call 911
  • Then call: the Safety Officer, (206) 940-8463
  • Within 24 hours: email the form to safety@ncllball.com
Incident & Injury Form

When to report

  • First aid supplies used at a game or practice
  • Player removed or sat out due to injury (head injury? follow the Concussion Protocol)
  • Any injury that needs medical care
  • A near-miss or unsafe condition

The form asks who was hurt, when and where, what happened, and how to reach you.

If they needed a doctor

  • Insurance: a parent and a league official fill out Little League's Accident Notification Form. It has to reach Little League within 20 days, and treatment has to start within 30 days of the injury.
  • Back to play: the player needs a medical release with no restrictions before the next game or practice.
  • Follow-up: the Safety Officer checks in on recovery and helps with the insurance claim.

Concussion Protocol

When in doubt, sit them out.

Washington law requires these steps:

  • Remove: right away, no return that day
  • Danger signs: call 911
  • Evaluate: provider trained in concussions
  • Clear: written clearance to return
  • Report: family, Safety Officer, incident form

Danger Signs: Call 911

  • Seizure (shaking or twitching)
  • Getting drowsier, or can't be woken
  • Slurred speech, weakness, or numbness
  • Headache that gets worse and won't go away
  • Repeated vomiting
  • One pupil larger than the other, or double vision
  • Can't recognize people or places
  • Growing confusion or agitation

Signs you may see

  • Dazed, stunned, or confused
  • Knocked out, even briefly
  • Slow to answer or react
  • Clumsy or unsteady
  • Can't remember what happened
  • Mood or behavior changes
  • Vomits right after the hit
  • Says they have a headache, feel dizzy, sick or foggy, or are bothered by light or noise

Signs can take hours or days to show up. Tell families what to watch for at home.

Return to play

  • Written clearance from the provider first, sent to safety@ncllball.com and the coach
  • Then a step-by-step return, as the provider directs

Common causes in baseball & softball

  • Hit in the head by a pitch, throw, or batted ball
  • Collisions with another player
  • Falls, or hitting a fence, the ground, or equipment

Weather & Heat

When thunder roars, go indoors.

  • Forecast: check it before every game or practice
  • Lightning: off the field at the first thunder or flash; back 30 minutes after the last thunder
  • Heat: water breaks every inning
  • Who calls it: the umpire once play starts; head coaches before and at practice

Lightning

  • If you can hear thunder, you're close enough to be struck (about 10 miles)
  • It can strike before the rain arrives
  • Watch radar or a lightning app at the field

When to stop and restart

  • Stop: first thunder or lightning, everyone to shelter
  • Restart: 30 minutes after the last thunder; new thunder restarts the clock

Shelter

  • Safe: a sturdy building with wiring and plumbing, or a hard-top car with the windows up
  • Not safe: dugouts, rain shelters, pavilions, bleachers, sheds, or under trees

Know where the nearest safe shelter is at your field before the first pitch.

Heat Exhaustion

  • Heavy sweating
  • Cold, pale, clammy skin
  • Fast, weak pulse
  • Nausea, vomiting, or muscle cramps
  • Tired, weak, dizzy, headache, or fainting

What to do

  • Move them somewhere cool; loosen clothing
  • Cool, wet cloths; sips of water
  • Get medical help if they vomit, get worse, or aren't better within an hour

Heat Stroke: Call 911

  • Body temperature 103°F or higher
  • Hot, red, dry or damp skin
  • Fast, strong pulse
  • Headache, dizziness, nausea
  • Confusion
  • Passing out

While you wait

  • Call 911 right away
  • Move them somewhere cooler
  • Cool them with wet cloths or cool water
  • Nothing to drink

Staying Cool

  • Drink water before, during, and after
  • Light-colored, moisture-wicking clothes
  • Shade between innings
  • Sunscreen (sunburn makes it harder to cool down)
  • Wet towel on the neck and wrists
  • Water or sports drinks; no energy drinks
  • Pull players at the first signs of heat illness

Air Quality

Per Washington State DOH guidance, all children 18 and under are a sensitive group.

Kids breathe more air for their size, and exercise adds to it. Check the AQI before every game and practice.

  • Monitor: AirNow before and during play
  • Modify: lighter, shorter, or indoors
  • Protect: Asthma/respiratory plans on file

AQI Levels (WA DOH)

  • Good (0–50): play as planned
  • Moderate (51–100): play; watch kids with asthma or other conditions
  • Sensitive groups (101–150): consider canceling anything over 1 hour
  • Unhealthy (151–200): cancel, or move indoors with filtered air
  • 201+: cancel all outdoor activities

Modification Options

  • Lower intensity: switch to easier drills
  • Shorter: cut total time outside
  • Relocate: move indoors or somewhere with cleaner air

Symptoms to Watch For

  • Burning eyes
  • Coughing or throat or nose irritation
  • Wheezing or shortness of breath
  • Headache or fatigue

If a player has symptoms, move them indoors right away and tell a parent or guardian.

Rainouts & Reschedules

Families: wait to hear from your coach. A Parks email, the hotline or a “closed” sign doesn’t by itself mean your game is off.

  • Before first pitch: the two head coaches decide together; umpires advise.
  • After first pitch: the umpire crew chief decides.
  • Lightning or thunder: stop play at once (see Weather & Heat).
