Pitch counts follow each pitcher’s league age, not their division. Little League’s Regulation VI sets the limits, District 8 adds an early-season cap, and NCLL adds one rule at AA. If anything here conflicts with the rulebook, the rulebook wins.

At the limit mid-batter, the pitcher may finish that batter: until the batter reaches base or is put out, or the third out ends the inning. No one pitches three days in a row. (Little League Regulation VI)

Rest is counted in calendar days: 66 pitches on Monday means the next pitching day is Friday. (Little League Regulation VI)

Catching and pitching on the same day

  • Caught 4 or more innings: can’t pitch that day. One pitch to a batter in the 4th inning counts as catching 4 innings.
  • Caught 3 innings or fewer, then pitched 21 or more (31 or more at league age 15–16): can’t go back to catcher that day.
  • Pitched 41 or more: can’t catch the rest of the day (unless the 40th pitch came mid-batter). (Little League Regulation VI)
  • AA (NCLL rule): caught 3 or more innings, can’t pitch that day; pitched 21 or more, can’t catch that game.

Track it every game: managers record pitch counts (usually through the scorekeeper) for every pitcher all season and share them with the Board on request.

Softball

Softball counts innings, not pitches. One pitch thrown counts as a full inning.

  • A: coach pitch, so no limits.
  • AA, AAA and Majors: at most 12 innings in a day, and 7 or more innings in a day needs 1 rest day. (Little League Regulation VI(b)) Managers also watch for fatigue and use several pitchers.
  • AAA and Majors, through April 30: at most 3 innings a game. (District 8 interleague rules)
  • Juniors: league age 12 follows the AAA/Majors limit; league age 13–14 has no limit; league age 15 may not pitch at Juniors. (District 8)

Each division’s full rules, with sources, are on its rules page: AA, AAA, Majors, Intermediate, Juniors, Seniors baseball; AAA, Majors, Juniors softball.
