Gloves

The #1 thing your player's future coach will thank you for? A glove that actually fits. You don't have to spend a lot - just a few minutes making sure it's the right one.

The wall of options can feel intimidating - we'll walk you through sizing, types, and break-in tips, plus some coaches' favorites at the end.

Glove size limits by division are on each division's rules page. See Rules by Division below.

Glove Types

Here are the most common glove styles.

  • First Base: Larger mitts for scooping.
  • Catcher: Padded with deep pockets.
  • Infield: Smaller gloves for quick transfers.
  • Outfield: Larger gloves w/ deep pockets.

If you aren't sure if you need a catcher's or first baseman's mitt, that means you don't - you'll know when that time comes. This narrows things to an infield (IF) or outfield (OF) glove.

Understanding Parts of a BB/SB Glove

Click the image to enlarge

  • Webbing: Infield open webbing for quick transfers; outfield closed to hide the ball.
  • Pockets: Infield shallow for fast release; outfield deeper for fly balls.
  • Backs: Infield open back for flexibility; outfield closed back for support.
  • Wrists: Adjustable for a snug, secure fit.

Glove Sizing

Click the image to enlarge

Glove length is measured from heel to tip.

  • Skip the brand hype. The right glove fits your child's hand and how they play.
  • Look for a snug fit with room for a batting glove underneath.

Glove Sizing (cont.)

  • Choose quality materials - genuine leather lasts longer and breaks in better.
  • A good fit feels snug but lets the fingers fully extend.
  • Open and close the glove to ensure it flexes easily.
  • Break-In Period: New gloves can feel stiff at first, so encourage your player to wear them and practice catching regularly to break them in.

Breaking a Glove In

  • Wear it often - form the pocket with a ball.
  • Play catch to loosen the leather.
  • Oil sparingly - if you must, try this lighter alternative .
  • Store with a ball in the pocket.
What not to do
  • Don't drive over it.
  • Don't use baby oil.
  • Don't oven or microwave it.

Bats

Nothing beats the sound of a good crack off the barrel - let's find the bat that makes it happen.

Click the image to enlarge

From certifications to drop weights, we'll cut through the noise so your player steps up to the plate ready to swing.

Step 1: Decide necessary certification - USA Baseball

The following Little League divisions require a USA Baseball certified bat - look for the above stamp on the barrel or taper:

  • TB (4-6)
  • KCP (6-8)
  • A (7-9)
  • AA (8-10)
  • AAA (9-11)
  • MAJ (10-12)

T-Ball bats have an additional requirement: in addition to the USA Baseball stamp, the bat must include the text ONLY FOR USE WITH APPROVED TEE BALLS . Tee Ball bats may also only be 26 inches in length or shorter.

Step 1: Decide necessary certification - BBCOR

The following Little League divisions and all high school divisions require a BBCOR certified bat - look for the above stamp on the barrel or taper:

  • INT (11-13)
  • JR (12-14)
  • SR (13-16)
  • High School (14+)

Step 1: Decide necessary certification - USSSA

USSSA (aka U-dub) is used for travel, select, and club ball - look for the above stamp on the barrel or taper:

  • 8U
  • 9U
  • 10U
  • 11U
  • 12U
  • 13U
  • 14U+

Step 1: Decide necessary certification - USA Softball (ASA)

The following Little League Softball divisions require a USA Softball certified bat - look for the above stamp on the barrel or taper:

  • A (7-9)
  • AA (9-12)
  • AAA (9-12)
  • MAJ (9-12)
  • JR (12-14)
  • SR (13-16)

Step 2: Choose Bat Construction & Materials

Construction Method
  • One-Piece: Single piece - more balance, more vibration. Good for beginners.
  • Two-Piece: Barrel + handle fused - less vibration, more flex and whip.
Material Types
  • Alloy: Durable, no break-in, affordable - smaller sweet spot.
  • Wood: Ash, maple, or birch - classic feel, less power than alloy.
  • Composite: Carbon fiber, larger sweet spot - needs 150-200 break-in hits.
  • Hybrid: Alloy barrel + composite handle - reduced vibration, balanced feel.

Step 3: Size It Quickly

  • Length check: Bat next to player in cleats; around hip height is a good start.
  • Drop check: Younger players often start around -10 to -13.
  • Rule: If it feels heavy, go lighter before going longer.

Batting Helmets

Every batter and every base runner wears a batting helmet, in every game and practice.

The league has a few loaner helmets, but we recommend buying your own. It fits better, and it keeps kids from sharing helmets (lice spread that way).

Step 1: The NOCSAE Rule

  • Every batting helmet must carry the NOCSAE stamp. Look for "Meets NOCSAE Standard" on the shell or the inside label.
  • No paint, no tape, no changes to the shell. Even stickers can void its NOCSAE certification, so Little League recommends no changes at all.

Step 2: No Chrome

  • It must be non-glare. Matte or standard gloss is fine; mirrored or chrome is not (Rule 1.16).
  • An umpire who spots a chrome helmet will have it taken out of the game (Rule 4.19(a)). If an illegal helmet is used in a game, the player and the manager are ejected and both sit out the team's next game (Rule 3.01).

Step 3: Face Guard or Not?

  • Softball: a face guard (cage) on the batting helmet is strongly recommended. Most softball helmets come with one.
  • Baseball: not required, but recommended. See the next panel.
  • Juniors and Seniors: ERNESTO: anything different, e.g. two ear flaps vs. one?

Baseball Face Shield

A face shield (also called a jaw guard or C-flap) bolts onto the helmet and covers the cheek and jaw. Not required in baseball, but recommended. Buy the one made for your helmet model.

Softball Face Guards

A face guard (cage) on the batting helmet is strongly recommended; most softball helmets come with one. On defense, a fielder's mask is recommended for infielders, especially pitchers and third base.

Step 4: Measure

  • Soft tape, about an inch above the eyebrows, just above the ears. No hat.
  • Typical: Tee Ball 19-20½"; AAA/Majors 21½-22⅝" (junior); 50/70 and up 23"+ (senior).
  • Brands differ, so use that maker's chart.
  • Between sizes? Go smaller. Don't buy big "to grow into."

Step 5: Fit Check

  • Snug, with no wobble when your player shakes their head.
  • The front edge sits about one finger-width above the eyebrows.
  • The ear holes line up with the ears.
  • Your player can see clearly straight ahead and to the side.

Step 6: Check It Every Season

  • Look for cracks in the shell, loose or missing pads, and a loose chin strap or cage.
  • Damaged helmets get replaced, not repaired.
  • Helmets carry a manufacture date. ERNESTO: replace after X years? Makers say 5 or sooner.
  • The manager has the final say on whether a player's own helmet is safe to use.

Catcher's Gear

The league provides full catcher's gear for every team in every division. Your player doesn't need to buy any.

Some players who catch a lot like their own. That's optional. ERNESTO: sizing tips or picks?

What a Catcher Wears

  • Catcher's helmet or mask with a dangling throat protector
  • Chest protector
  • Leg guards (shin guards)
  • A catcher's mitt. An infielder's glove isn't allowed for catching.

Cups and Warm-ups

  • Boys: a hard protective cup. This is the one catcher item your player must bring.
  • Anyone catching, even during warm-ups or between innings, wears at least a mask, a chest protector and (boys) a cup.

Cleats & the Rest

  • Cleats: molded (rubber) cleats. Athletic shoes work for younger players. Metal spikes only in the older divisions.
  • Athletic supporter: every boy. Hard cup: boys who catch.
  • Water bottle: with your player's name on it, every practice and game.

Metal Spikes by Division

  • Not allowed: Majors and younger, baseball and softball. Molded cleats only.
  • Allowed: baseball 50/70, Juniors, Seniors; softball Juniors, Seniors.
  • Never for managers, coaches or umpires.

Removable spikes are OK in the younger divisions if no metal shows once they're off. Rules 1.11 and 9.06.

Cups

  • Every boy must wear an athletic supporter.
  • Boys who catch must wear a hard cup, including warm-ups and the bullpen.
  • For everyone else a cup isn't required, but it's recommended, especially for infielders.

Rule 1.17 and the Little League Safety Code (baseball and softball).

Optional Extras

  • Bat bag, batting gloves, sliding shorts, sunglasses: nice to have, not required.
  • Softball fielder's mask: see Softball Face Guards on the Batting Helmets card.
  • Put your player's name on everything. Gloves especially: name and phone number.
