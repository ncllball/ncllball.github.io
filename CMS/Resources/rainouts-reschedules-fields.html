Seattle rain is part of the game. This page covers who calls a rainout, how you find out, what coaches do when a game is off, how make-ups get scheduled, how we care for our fields, and where our field permits are.

Jump to: Families · Who decides · How to find out · Coaches · Protecting our fields · Fields & permits · Umpires

Is My Game On? (Families)

  • Your coach tells you, through your team’s usual channel (GameChanger, team email or text).
  • If Seattle Parks has closed the field, nobody plays on it. You can check the Parks rainout hotline yourself: (206) 233-0055.
  • Thunder or lightning stops play right away, at every level. See the lightning rule on the Safety Hub.

Who Decides

  • Seattle Parks decides whether a field is closed. A closed field is closed, permit or not.
  • Before first pitch, the two head coaches decide together whether an open field is playable. Umpires can advise and should be heard.
  • After first pitch, the umpire crew chief alone decides whether to continue or suspend play.
  • Lightning or thunder stops play immediately. Resume only after 30 minutes with no lightning.

How to Find Out

  • Parks rainout hotline: (206) 233-0055, updated weekdays around 3:30 PM.
  • Parks rainout emails: sign up on Seattle Parks’ Sports Field Reservations page. This is the single best way to stay current.
  • Weekends: Parks doesn’t update the hotline or emails, and a closed field usually has a posted sign. Otherwise the coaches decide, and word goes out in your division’s coaches Space in Google Chat.
  • Managers & umpires Google Group: coaches and umpires share field conditions in an informal Google Group. To join, email the umpire coordinators.

Coaches: When a Game Is Off

  1. Talk to the other coach. For an interleague game, your division head has their contact. There is nothing worse than kids showing up to no game.
  2. Tell your team and families right away, with the field, date, time and whether it’s canceled or delayed.
  3. Tell your division head, so the schedule gets updated and the umpires know.

Grey areas

On some days one Cloverleaf field is unplayable while yours is fine, or a short rake would fix it. Work it out with the other coach and be honest: is it safe, do both teams agree, and would 10 minutes of field work leave it the same or better than you found it? If you can leave it better than you found it, play ball. Otherwise, take the day off. Our relationship with Seattle Parks matters more than any one game.

Make-up games and extra field time

  • Make-ups and extra practice time go through your division head. Include your team, division and open dates.
  • Games come before practices when field time is short.
  • Interleague make-ups follow the District 8 schedule; your division head coordinates them.

Protecting Our Fields

NCLL’s Fields lead, Tyler Christofferson (fields@ncllball.com), gets fields ready and organizes work crews. Watch your email and your Chat Space for calls to action.

  • Friday nights before a wet weekend: rake or nail-drag the dirt, and remove standing water with buckets or a shop-vac.
  • Drying agent (Turface or Diamond Dry): only within about 45 minutes of game time, never the night before, and never on a standing puddle.
  • Tools: rakes, nails and string for chalking foul lines are in the field lock boxes.
  • Rainy Fridays: email Tyler a photo of your field so he can plan.
  • Never play on a closed field. Ruts in a closed field bring formal complaints from Parks and put our permits at risk.
  • Report damage or unsafe conditions (holes, broken fencing, debris) to fields@ncllball.com.

Fields & Permits

NCLL’s fields are permitted through Seattle Parks. Most games are at Lower Woodland (the Cloverleaf); we also use BF Day, Lower and Upper Ross, Robert Eagle Staff, Loyal Heights and Dahl. Maps and addresses are on the Field Locations page.

  • Permit schedule: the 2027 permits (fields, dates and times) will be in a read-only Google Sheet from our scheduler. The link goes here once it’s shared.
  • Your team’s slots: your division head assigns practice and game times from those permits.
  • Stay in your slot: use only your assigned field and time, arrive on time and leave on time so the next team can start.

Umpires

  • After first pitch, weather calls belong to the crew chief. Lightning or thunder stops play at once.
  • Before first pitch, advise the coaches; they make the call together.
  • Questions about postponed games or assignments go to the umpire coordinators. More on the Umpires Hub.
