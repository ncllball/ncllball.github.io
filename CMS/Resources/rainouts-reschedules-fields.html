Rain, Reschedules & Fields

Who Decides

Decision authority

Straight from the mouth of Jon Becker, NCLL UIC:

  • Before first pitch: Authority rests with the league (president). In practice, coaches decide together; umpires can advise and should be weighed in.
  • After first pitch: The umpire crew chief has sole authority to continue or suspend play due to weather, often based on infield safety.
Report a Field Issue

Who calls a rainout?

Seattle Parks closures carry real weight: playing on closed dirt and leaving ruts brings complaints and puts our permits at risk. But closures can be blanket, and one field may be playable when the rest aren’t, so coaches and league officials make the final call before play. Even if the City of Seattle does not close the fields, team managers and league officials may decide fields are unplayable in advance of play.

Once a game begins, the chief umpire is solely empowered to halt play if conditions are unsafe. Lightning in the area is cause for immediate suspension of play, regardless of field conditions.

Lightning safety

Play must stop immediately when lightning or thunder is detected. Everyone should shelter in a substantial building (electrical/plumbing preferred) or a hard-topped metal vehicle. Dugouts, sheds, and small outdoor structures are not safe. Resume only after 30 minutes with no lightning.

How to Find Out

How do I find out about rainouts?

For coaches. Families: your coach makes the call and tells you.

  • Rainout hotline: (206) 233-0055 (updated Mon-Fri at 3:30 PM).
  • Parks daily emails: sign up on the Seattle Parks field reservations page for weekday updates around 12:30 and 3:30 PM. Probably the number one thing you can do to stay updated.

City alerts & closures

Subscribe to the parks email alerts. Daily emails go out on weekdays around 12:30 and 3:30 PM. This is probably the number one thing you can do to stay updated.

If your field is listed, Parks has officially closed it and you should expect a closure sign at the field.

Weekend updates & group chat

Note: The Parks Department hotline is not updated on weekends. If fields are unsafe, the only indication may be a posted closure sign, which is unusual. On weekends, field playability is usually at the discretion of managers or league officials.

An informal Google Group of managers and umpires often shares updates. To join, email the umpire coordinators .

Coaches: When a Game Is Off

  1. Talk to the other coach. There’s nothing worse than kids showing up to no game.
  2. Tell your team and families right away.
  3. Tell your division head, so the schedule gets updated and the umpires know.

When in doubt, if you can leave it better than you found it, then play ball. Otherwise, take the week off.

When a game is canceled (no grey area)

  1. If bad weather is forecast, communicate with the opposing coach. For inter-league games, reach out to your division head for contact info.
  2. Make the call; if you’re on-site, involve the umpire when possible.
  3. Notify your team and umpires immediately (email/text/app).
  4. Tell your division head, CC’ing the other head coach, with the original date, start time and teams. For an interleague game (e.g., NCLL vs RUG), agree with the other league on which one reschedules.
  5. Get the game back on the schedule ASAP (your division head handles this) so parents and umpires can plan.
  6. Cross fingers.

Step 1 — Check status

  • Confirm field status (league text/email, city parks hotline, field coordinators).
  • Walk the infield/mound if on site; standing water or unsafe footing = no play.
  • Coordinate with opposing coach and umpire if assigned.

Step 2 — Notify immediately

  • Post in your team channel (GameChanger/Team Central) and email families.
  • Include: field, date/time, status (canceled, delayed, moved indoors), and next update time.
  • Alert umpires/field crew if applicable so they can stand down.
  • Update any volunteers (scorekeeper, team manager, snacks).

Sample: “Today’s 5:30 PM at Lower Woodland #2 is canceled due to rain. No reschedule yet; we’ll share a make-up slot once posted.”

Step 3 — Protect the field

  • Replace/tighten tarps, secure bases, and remove standing water with buckets (no digging ruts).
  • Do not drag heavy screens or rakes on soaked clay; avoid creating holes on the mound/batter’s box.
  • Lock sheds and return league gear to dry storage.

Grey areas to expect

  • Parks & Rec does not post official field closures on weekends.
  • Some Cloverleaf fields (4, 5, 6) might be unplayable while your scheduled field (e.g., 3) is playable.
  • Both teams may be able to improve conditions with a short pre-game field rake.

In grey areas, use good judgment and be honest. Work with the opposing coach and answer:

  1. Is it safe—and do both teams agree it is safe?
  2. If you spent 10 minutes on the field after the game, would it be the same or better than when you started?

When in doubt, if you can leave it better than you found it, then play ball. Otherwise, take the week off. Good relationships with Parks and Recreation are more important and something we work very hard at.

Policies & make-ups

  • League may close fields proactively; always honor posted closures.
  • Games take priority over practices for reschedules; use assigned slots for make-ups.
  • Make-up and extra field time requests go through your division head; include teams, division, and open dates.
  • Interleague make-ups follow the District 8 schedule; your division head coordinates them.

Quick checks

  • Watch league rainout alerts before leaving home.
  • Use the 30/30 lightning rule; stop play on unsafe surfaces.
  • Report rainouts to your division head and reply quickly about make-ups.

Closed Fields & Wet Practices

  • Closed by Parks? Stay off the dirt. Using “closed” dirt and leaving ruts is a surefire way for the league to get a formal complaint.
  • The grass is often fine for a workout if it isn’t actively raining.
  • Batting practice: swing away from the cages; if there’s room, hit from the walking paths to save the turf.
  • Warmups and drills: use the deep outfield along the fences or center field; the grass along the foul lines wears fastest.
  • Ground balls: on solid ground, away from puddles. Safety comes first.
  • A closed field can still be improved so your team, or another, can play Saturday.

Friday Night

  • Dry or damp field: scratch it up with a rake (think nail drag) to turn the dirt and let overnight rain soak in. Fill holes and bring dirt from the high spots back to the low spots; that’s the best time to level it.
  • Wet field, little rain expected overnight: remove every puddle (shop-vac, buckets, rake or broom), then scratch the surface up to ½ inch deep and leave the marks. The wind dries it overnight.
  • No drying agent the night before. It wastes our limited supply.
  • Check the hourly forecast and radar. Don’t do a lot of work if it’s going to pour all night.

Saturday Morning

  • Get there early. Tyler is often there by 7 AM when a field is in bad shape, and recruits coaches and parents to help.
  • Keep scratching the wet dirt and removing puddles. Scratching plus a little wind dries a field surprisingly fast.
  • About 45 minutes before your game, put drying agent on the wet spots: fielding spots, home plate, in front of the mound.
  • See a Seattle Parks worker? Tell them we have games all day; they’ll often bring more Turface for coaches who are out working the field.
  • Be realistic. Lower Woodland 3 drains best and is usually easy to save; 4 has problem spots at first base and home but often can be; 5 and 6 need extra love (and prayers).

Turface vs. Diamond Dry

  • Turface (granular, like kitty litter) is the main surfacing product and soaks up a lot of water. Use it first.
  • Diamond Dry (chalky powder) is strictly for wet fields. Fall back on it if the Turface runs out.
  • Never fill a puddle with either. Remove the water, scratch the dirt, then apply.
  • Spreading: open a 6-inch hole at one end of the bag and pour while you walk, swinging the bag side to side, then smooth with a rake.

Chalking Foul Lines

Dry field and no Parks crew? Put the lines down yourself with the nails and string in the lock box.

  • One nail in the outfield grass on the foul side of the paint line, one just past home plate, so the string runs through the back point of the plate and along the edge of first or third base.
  • Chalk on the fair (field) side of the string, so the line runs right under the base.
  • Rakes, nails and string are in the field lock boxes; bringing your own garden rake helps.

Fields & Permits

Our fields are permitted through Seattle Parks. Most games are at Lower Woodland; we also use BF Day, Lower and Upper Ross, Eagle Staff, Loyal Heights and Dahl. Maps and addresses: Field Locations.

  • Permit schedule: the 2027 permits (fields, dates and times) will be in a read-only Google Sheet from our scheduler. The link goes here once it’s shared.
  • Your team’s slots: your division head assigns practice and game times from those permits.
  • Stay in your slot: on time in, on time out.
