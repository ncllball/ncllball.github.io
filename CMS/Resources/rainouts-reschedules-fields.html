This page is for coaches and umpires. Parents: if a game or practice is canceled, your coach will tell you.

Seattle rain is part of the game. This page covers who calls a rainout and how you find out, what coaches do when a game is off, how make-ups get scheduled, how we care for our fields, and our field permits.

Rain, Reschedules & Fields

Is the Game On?

Who decides (Jon Becker, NCLL UIC)

  • Before first pitch: authority rests with the league. In practice, the coaches decide together, with the umpires advising.
  • After first pitch: the umpire crew chief alone decides, often based on infield safety.
  • Lightning or thunder: stop play at once. See the Safety Hub’s Weather & Heat card.

Weekdays: how to find out

  • Parks daily emails: sign up on the Seattle Parks field reservations page. They go out weekdays around 12:30 and 3:30 PM, and they’re probably the number one thing you can do to stay updated.
  • Rainout hotline: (206) 233-0055, updated Mon-Fri at 3:30 PM.
  • If your field is listed, Parks has closed it; expect a closure sign at the field.

When Parks closes a field

Parks closures carry all the weight. However:

  • Closures can be blanket; one field may be playable when the rest aren’t.
  • Even without a closure, coaches and league officials may still call a field unplayable ahead of time.
  • Weekends: the hotline isn’t updated and Parks rarely posts closures, so it’s up to coaches and league officials.

The key to all of it is judgment

  • Good: you have the people (say, four) to rake out the moisture, aerate, dry and chalk it, and leave it better than you found it.
  • Poor: playing on closed dirt and leaving ruts, or pouring Turface all over the field and leaving a mess for Parks and your fellow coaches.
  • The cost: Parks complaints, our permits at risk, and a league that looks bad.

Making the call

  • Talk early with the other coach and walk the field together, with the umpire if you can.
  • Ask: is it safe, do both teams agree, and can you leave it better?

When in doubt, if you can leave it better than you found it, then play ball. Otherwise, take the week off. Good relationships with Parks and Recreation are more important and something we work very hard at.

Once a Game Is Canceled

  1. Make sure the other coach knows. There’s nothing worse than kids showing up to no game.
  2. Tell your umpires if they weren’t part of the call, so they don’t show up. They’re on the umpire sign-up sheet (link coming).
  3. Tell your team and families right away.
  4. Home team: tell your division head in your division’s Chat Space, so the schedule gets updated.

Tell your team

  • Post in your team app and email families: the field, date and time, status (canceled or delayed) and when you’ll update next.
  • Tell your volunteers (scorekeeper, snacks) too.

Sample: “Today’s 5:30 PM game at Lower Woodland #2 is canceled due to rain. No make-up date yet; we’ll share it once it’s posted.”

Tell your division head

  • Home team posts it in your division’s coaches Chat Space (e.g., “2027 Majors BB Coaches”). Your division head is in that Space.
  • Include the original date, start time and teams.
  • That gets the schedule updated and the umpires stood down.

Make-ups

  • Your division head reschedules the game. Reply quickly in your Chat Space about open dates so parents and umpires can plan.
  • Games take priority over practices for make-up slots.
  • Need extra field time? Ask your division head with the teams, division and dates that work.

Interleague games

  • For a game against another league (e.g., NCLL vs RUG), agree with the other league on which one reschedules.
  • Make-ups follow the District 8 schedule, and your division head coordinates them.
  • Other leagues’ coaches: names and numbers are in the District 8 contact sheet (link coming).

Closed Fields & Wet Practices

  • Closed by Parks? Stay off the dirt. Ruts on closed dirt bring formal complaints.
  • The grass is often fine if it isn’t actively raining.
  • Batting practice: away from the cages, or hit from the walking paths.
  • Drills: deep outfield or center; the foul-line grass wears fastest.
  • Grounders: solid ground, away from puddles.

Friday Night

  • Check the radar first. If it’ll pour all night, save your energy.
  • Dry or damp: rake it up (think nail drag) so rain soaks in. Fill holes; move dirt from high spots to low spots.
  • Wet, little rain coming: remove every puddle, then scratch ½ inch deep and leave it. The wind dries it overnight.
  • No drying agent tonight. It wastes our limited supply.

Saturday Morning

  • Get there early. Tyler’s often there by 7 AM, recruiting coaches and parents.
  • Keep scratching and removing puddles.
  • 45 minutes before the game: drying agent on fielding spots, home plate and in front of the mound.
  • Parks worker around? Tell them we play all day; they often bring more Turface.
  • Be realistic: LW 3 drains best, 4 is usually savable, 5 and 6 need extra love.

Turface vs. Diamond Dry

  • Turface (granular, like kitty litter) soaks up the most water. Use it first.
  • Diamond Dry (chalky powder) is for wet fields, once the Turface runs out.
  • Never fill a puddle or pour it everywhere; that’s poor judgment. Remove the water, scratch, then apply.
  • Spreading: cut a 6-inch hole in the bag and pour as you walk, swinging it side to side; rake smooth.

Chalking Foul Lines

Dry field, no Parks crew? Nails, string and rakes are in the lock box.

  • Old line: you’ll most likely see a faint one; with a steady hand, just follow it.
  • No line? String it: one nail in the outfield grass (foul side of the paint line), one just past home; it runs through the back point of the plate and along the base.
  • Chalk on the fair side of the string, so the line runs under the base.

Field Permits

Another team on your field? Our permit list is the final word on which fields, dates and times are ours. Pull it up on your phone and show them our permit for that slot.

  • The permit list: the 2027 permits are in a read-only Google Sheet from our scheduler. The link goes here once it’s shared; bookmark it before the season.

Slots & fields

  • Your team’s slots: your division head assigns practice and game times from those permits.
  • Stay in your slot: on time in, on time out.
  • Where we play: Lower Woodland for most games, plus BF Day, Lower and Upper Ross, Eagle Staff, Loyal Heights and Dahl. Maps and addresses: Field Locations.

Umpires

  • Before first pitch, advise the coaches; they make the call together. After first pitch, it’s the crew chief’s call (see Is the Game On?).
  • Postponed games or assignments: ask the umpire coordinators.
  • Managers & umpires Google Group: an informal group that often shares weekend field updates. To join, email the umpire coordinators.
