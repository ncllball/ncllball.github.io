Welcome to the Volunteer Hub. This is the one place to raise your hand for the roles and quick-call crews that keep NCLL running.

No experience required. Most roles need little or no training — NCLL provides support and encouragement to help you succeed. There are four primary volunteer categories below: Team Level , League Level , Umpiring , and Pop-Up Support .

We’ve all got our place — come and find yours!

Volunteer Options and Help

How to Volunteer (process)

New to volunteering with NCLL? Welcome! Five steps and you’re on the field.

  1. Register with Sports Connect (< 5 min • government ID)
  2. Find Your Role (at your leisure)
  3. Finish Your Volunteer Form (< 5 min • waivers)
  4. Background Check (< 5 min • SSN + ID)
  5. Abuse Awareness Training (< 25 min • email)

Step 1 — Register with Sports Connect

  1. Go to ncllball.com and click Register
  2. Enter your email and click Continue
  3. Create a password (8+ characters, upper and lower case, one symbol)
  4. Verify your email (the link expires in 24 hours; check spam)
  5. Select Volunteer registration, choose your programs and check out (no cost)

Step 2 — Find Your Role

Registering a player? Add volunteering right in their registration. Otherwise:

  1. Log into Sports Connect and click MY ACCOUNT (top right)
  2. Click Volunteer in the left sidebar, then Find Volunteer Roles
  3. Scroll down and click Show More (the step most people miss): programs your player isn’t in stay hidden until you do

Step 2 — Sign Up for It

  1. Pick this season’s program (not last year’s) and click View Available Positions
  2. Click Select next to the role you want, then Continue
  3. Choose Sign up [Your Name] for yourself, or Sign up a New User for a family member not yet on the account

Step 3 — Finish Your Volunteer Form

  1. Answer the questions for your role, including the background check questionnaire
  2. Add any planned absences or availability notes if the form asks
  3. Click View / Accept on the Little League Volunteer Application, Privacy Policy, and Child Protection waivers
  4. Click Continue to submit. You’re in!

Step 4 — Background Check

  1. After registering, watch for an email from JDP with your link (check spam). It’s good for 14 days
  2. Complete the questionnaire using your legal name and the same email you registered with
  3. Submit (no fee to you). It renews every season before you work with players.
  4. Missing your invite? Email treasurer@ncllball.com

Step 5 — Abuse Awareness Training

  1. Go to the Abuse Awareness Training page at Little League University
  2. Log in (or create an account), search for Abuse Awareness Training and enroll
  3. Work through the modules and the final assessment, then submit
  4. Done — the league is notified automatically. No code or certificate needed.

Thank You!

Your support is invaluable to NCLL. We appreciate your dedication to creating a positive and fun experience for our young athletes.

Questions about signing up? We’re happy to help.

Abuse Awareness Training

Keeping kids safe is job one. Every volunteer should know how to spot, stop, and report concerns—physical, emotional, or online.

Twenty-five minutes a year — and even one takeaway could make a real difference for a kid on your team.

Details

  • Time: ~20–25 minutes
  • Frequency: Every calendar year
  • Deadline: Before your first practice or game
  • Who: Managers, coaches, umpires, on-field helpers, Board members, and any volunteer in contact with players
  • Proof: Logged automatically — no upload needed

How to Complete (1 of 4)

  1. Access the Platform
    • Go to Little League University – Abuse Awareness Training
    • Create an account (first-time) or log in (returning)

How to Complete (2 of 4)

  1. Enroll
    • Search for “Abuse Awareness Training” if not immediately visible
    • If asked, search for North Central Little League
    • Open the course and click Enroll / Register

How to Complete (3 of 4)

  1. Complete Modules
    • Work through each module’s video and reading segments
    • Answer any embedded questions or mini-quizzes

How to Complete (4 of 4)

  1. Final Assessment
    • Finish the end-of-course quiz and submit
    • Completion is logged automatically — no upload needed

Tips & Reminders

  • Technical issues: Use the LLU Help / Support link, or try a fresh browser tab or incognito mode
  • Deadline: Finish before your first scheduled practice or game
  • Returning volunteers: You must retake the course each calendar year

Tips & Reminders (continued)

  • Stay sharp: Revisit key indicators periodically during the season
  • Scope: Required for managers, coaches, umpires, on-field helpers, and board/key volunteers
  • Required by Little League International: Recognize and report abuse to keep every field safe

Background Check

Safety starts with screening. All volunteers complete an annual background check before stepping on the field or in the dugout.

Details

  • Time: 3–5 minutes online
  • Cost: Free; NCLL covers it
  • Who: Anyone who works with players
  • When: Before your first practice or game
  • Renewal: Every calendar year
  • Provider: JDP (secure screening)
  • Required by: Little League policy and the law

What to Have Ready

  • Full legal name, birth date, and current address for the JDP screening.
  • Driver's license or state ID to speed up verification.
  • Best email and mobile number so we can share scheduling updates.

How to Complete (1 of 2)

  1. Register as a volunteer
    • Follow the How to Volunteer steps on this page: sign in to Sports Connect and pick your volunteer role.
  2. Watch for the JDP email
    • Look for a secure link from JDP (check spam). The link is valid for 14 days .

How to Complete (2 of 2)

  1. Complete the questionnaire
    • Open the JDP link and fill out the required fields with accurate, up-to-date information.
    • Use your legal name and the same email you registered with NCLL.
  2. Submit and wait for confirmation
    • No additional upload needed — NCLL is notified automatically when you're cleared.

Little League Policy

Little League may turn away anyone whose background check shows a risk to young people or other volunteers.

Questions?

NCLL is notified automatically; don’t forward results. Need a new invite? Email the Treasurer.

Team Level Volunteers

Head Coach, Assistant Coach, and Team Manager keep every roster humming—setting culture, organizing practices, and keeping parents informed—and we’re always looking for more to join them.

Team-level volunteers work directly with players: coaching, mentoring and organizing so kids build skills, confidence and a love for the game.

Why Volunteer at the Team Level?

  • Watch kids grow in skill and confidence
  • Build lasting friendships with families
  • Make a visible difference for kids
  • Create lifetime memories

Explore Specific Team Roles

Dig into the Head Coach, Assistant Coach, or Team Manager roles to understand expectations, time required, and training support.

Head Coach
  • Leads the team and sets the tone.
  • Plans fast-paced practices for the roster.
  • Owns strategy and player development.
  • Sets lineups and talks with umpires.
  • Builds players with encouragement.
  • Keeps the dugout positive and inclusive.
  • Leads assistants and the team manager.
  • 6-8 hrs/week; training provided.
Assistant Coach
  • Run drills so the head coach can teach.
  • Hands-on in practices and games.
  • Lead stations and small groups.
  • Coach bases and the dugout.
  • Specialize: hitting, pitching, or catching.
  • Step in when the head coach is out.
  • Build trust with players and parents.
  • Time: 4-6 hrs/week (practices + games).
  • Great for new coaches and former players.
Team Manager
  • Off-field organizer (Team Mom/Dad).
  • Backup scorekeeping and pitch counts.
  • Dugout help during games.
  • Uniforms, paperwork and records.
  • Team emails, reminders and schedules.
  • Snacks, photos and the season-end party.
  • Link between families and coordinators.
  • Pitch in on fundraising or special events.

League Level Volunteers

League Level Volunteers keep NCLL running smoothly behind the scenes. Board and Key Volunteer roles take a bigger time commitment but let you shape programs, policies, and culture for all 800 families.

Have a passion to grow or a pain point to fix? Tell us and we'll find your role.

Why Volunteer at the League Level?

  • Shape the experience for 800+ families
  • Build leadership skills that last
  • Strengthen neighborhood connections
  • Work alongside dedicated volunteers
  • Leave a legacy for future players

Board Members

The NCLL Board runs the league. Elected yearly; 8-15 hrs/week in season, 3-5 off.

Board Roles

  • President - vision, planning
  • VPs (Baseball, Softball) - divisions, coaches
  • Secretary - minutes, records
  • Treasurer - budget, books
  • Registrar - registration, rosters
  • Player Agent - rosters, eligibility
  • Safety Officer - risk, safety
  • CIO - website, tech

Key Volunteers

Take a set role, or find a need and help the Board solve it. No election; many Board members started here.

  • Division Coordinators
  • Umpire Coordinator
  • Equipment & Fields
  • Sponsorships
  • Volunteer Coordinator
  • Time : usually 4-8 hrs/week

Skills Needed

  • Organization and attention to detail
  • Communication and people skills
  • Problem-solving and flexibility
  • Commitment to the league's mission
  • Willingness to learn (we provide training!)

Umpiring Volunteers

Often called the "third team on the field," the umpiring Junta —as they like to be called—keep games moving, fair, and safe.

The best crew in the district, and a fun one. Don't be afraid to reach out!

Overview

Umpires aren’t employed by Little League or NCLL. They’re independent volunteers who partner with us to keep games fair, safe, and fun, and they don’t work for coaches.

New or experienced, you get training, support, and mentorship behind the plate and on the bases.

Why Volunteer as an Umpire?

  • Best seat in the house for every game
  • Learn the game inside and out
  • Optional stipends for your time
  • Build confidence and decision-making skills
  • Join a supportive community of fellow umpires

Great fit for parents, former players, or anyone who wants to stay close to the game.

What Umpires Do

  • Call balls and strikes to keep the game fair
  • Rule on plays at bases and in the field
  • Maintain game flow and pace
  • Watch for safety issues and rule violations
  • Communicate calls and manage coach interactions

Time Commitment

  • Games typically last 1.5-2 hours
  • Most umpires work 1-3 games per week during the season (April-June)
  • Flexible scheduling to fit your week

Training & Support

  • Pre-season clinics (in-person and online)
  • Mentorship from experienced umpires
  • Rules clinics and ongoing development
  • Protective gear provided by the league

Umpire Roles

On-Field Umpire
  • Rotate between plate and base assignments
  • Manage game tempo, enforce rules, communicate with coaches
  • Apply clinic training and peer mentoring
  • Model calm, confident decision-making
  • Time commitment: 1-3 games per week (about 90 minutes each)
Umpire Coordinator(s)
  • Schedule coverage and manage swaps
  • Track availability and certifications
  • Communicate rainouts and time changes
  • Celebrate milestones (first plate job!)
  • Time: 3-4 hrs/week, more pre-season

Primary point of contact for questions, assignments, and training updates.

Umpire in Chief
  • Mentor the crew; keep mechanics sharp
  • Run preseason mechanics and rules clinics
  • Shadow new umpires and give real-time coaching
  • Standardize interpretations across fields
  • Time commitment: 4-6 hrs/week

Sets the standard for consistency and helps every umpire level up.

"POP UP" Volunteer Lists

It's baseball: Pop-Ups happen anytime. These lists are for one-shift, as-needed help.

Can't commit to a whole season? Join a list: the Ad Hoc Heroes or the Ground Gremlins.

1. Ad Hoc Heroes

Keep special events moving all season.

  • Assessments - setup, check-in
  • Photo Day - cat wrangling
  • Home Run Derby - pitch, track, safety
  • Skills Clinics - station help
  • Fundraisers - raffles, setup
  • Season-end celebrations
  • Time: 2-4 hours per event
  • We email sign-up links before each event

2. Ground Gremlins

The field-first crew keeping every diamond safe and game-ready.

  • Upkeep - weeding, raking, dugouts
  • Field prep - drag, line, set bases
  • Mounds - repair, pack clay
  • Sheds and field equipment
  • Safety checks - report hazards
  • Spring prep, season-end cleanup
  • Time: flexible (often early Saturday texts)

Sign Up

Drop-in help: Even 15 minutes of raking between games makes a difference. We’ll message the lists when help is needed.

"Super Volunteers" (Employer Matching)

Every volunteer is a hero, but Super Volunteers tap employer matching so each hour counts twice, with no extra time!

Two years ago, one volunteer's matched hours funded every All-Star uniform.

What is Employer Matching?

Many companies turn your volunteer hours into cash grants for nonprofits like NCLL, from Microsoft, Boeing, and Starbucks to plenty of smaller firms.

NCLL is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, so it qualifies for most corporate giving programs.

Get Started

  1. Search the matching-gift database for your company.
  2. Not listed? Ask HR about volunteer-time matching (“dollars for doers”).
  3. Log your hours. Some firms want a letter from NCLL; many approve within a week.
Search matching gift database

Why It Matters

  • Matching funds help pay for uniforms, player development, equipment, and more.
  • Time literally is money—your hours, doubled in impact.

Questions or stuck in an HR portal? We’ll walk you through it.
