Assessments: Expectations

Assessments are not tryouts. Every player makes a team. Assessments help NCLL place players in the right division and build competitive balance across teams so games are fun, safe, and development stays the priority.

Who attends

  • Baseball: AAA and Majors players.
  • Softball: AAA and Majors players.
  • Players asking to play up, or on the edge between two divisions. Assessments help us make safer, fairer decisions.
  • Returning and new players alike. Each season is a fresh look.

Why assessments matter

  • Competitive balance: NCLL drafts AAA and Majors teams to keep them even, and assessments help spread pitching, catching, hitting and defense across rosters.
  • Better development: Players learn faster when the game speed matches their current skills: challenging but not overwhelming.
  • Safety: Placement reduces mismatch risk and helps coaches plan roles responsibly.
  • Clear expectations: Coaches start the season with a shared baseline and can build practice plans accordingly.

Assessment flow

Assessments run like a fast-moving practice. Players check in, get a bib number, warm up, then rotate through stations in small groups while coaches score each skill.

Baseball: five tools

  • Hitting: swing, contact and power.
  • Fielding: infield and outfield, including throws and cutoffs.
  • Speed: timed runs from home to first.
  • Pitching: mechanics, velocity and accuracy, for players who pitch.
  • Catching: receiving and throwing, for players who catch.

Each tool is scored 1 to 5, and the scores combine into one composite score used for the draft.

Softball stations

  • Throwing & catching at increasing distances.
  • Running: a timed sprint from home to first.
  • Fielding: ground balls with a throw to first.
  • Hitting.
  • Pitching and catching, for players who pitch or catch.

Staff work hard to create a consistent, supportive environment. The goal is quality reps, not pressure.

How results are used

  • To help NCLL volunteers, division leads and the Player Agent create balanced teams and to support parity-focused drafts when applicable. Read how the draft works to see how assessments feed team formation.
  • To guide safe placement decisions (including play up requests) alongside coach feedback and roster availability.
  • To help coaches plan early roles and practice priorities, not to label players.

How families should prepare

  • Bring: glove, water, weather-appropriate layers, and shoes appropriate to the surface; helmet is often recommended; bat optional.
  • Arrive early: plan for check-in and a short warm-up before your scheduled start.
  • Encourage effort: tell your player it's "show your current skills" day, not "make the team" day.
  • Help out: volunteering (even for a small role) keeps the day organized and gives kids more reps. New to NCLL? Visit the Volunteer Hub.
