Frequently asked questions about 2027 assessments: baseball AAA and Majors, softball AAA and Majors. For dates and sign-ups, see 2027 Assessments. For how the day runs, see What to Expect.

General

Q: Why do we run assessments?

A: To build balanced teams and place players where they will develop best. Every player makes a team.

Q: Who attends?

A: Baseball AAA and Majors players, and softball AAA and Majors players. Skilled baseball 9’s may attend for AAA consideration; younger divisions don’t assess. If you need a refresher on league eligibility rules, see Eligibility & Boundaries .

Q: Baseball: I think my 9-year-old is pretty good. Can they try out?

A: 10’s, 11’s and 12’s fill AAA/Majors first. After they are placed, skilled 9’s can earn spots if roster space remains and their assessment score places them in the draftable pool. If you think your 9-year-old is ready, have them attend; the draftable pool size depends on available roster space after 10’s and 11’s are placed and, ultimately, how many teams we field. Read how the draft works to see how teams are formed.

Q: Is there a make-up date, or what if we miss our window?

A: No. It isn’t a penalty: the day takes a lot of volunteers to put on, and it’s fairest when every player goes through it together. Email baseball@ncllball.com or softball@ncllball.com as soon as you know you’ll be late or must miss. It won’t change how your player is placed, but a dialogue is always better than a no-show. Players who miss are placed using prior-season evaluations and coach input.

Q: What if we arrive late but before the last group begins their stations?

A: Check in immediately. We will work you into the remaining stations during your sport’s window if space allows. The earlier you arrive, the more complete your evaluation.

Q: What if we arrive late and there are no more groups?

A: If the final group of the day has gone, you will not be able to participate. Email baseball@ncllball.com or softball@ncllball.com right away. Your player will be placed using prior-season evaluations and coach input, the same as anyone who misses the day.

Logistics

Q: When and where?

A: Estimated for baseball: Sat, Jan 23. Softball: Sat, Feb 6. Locations and times will be on the Assessments page.

Q: How do I sign up?

A: Sign-ups will open on the Assessments page, where you’ll pick an arrival time. Not registered yet? Start at 2027 Registration .

Q: What should we bring?

A: Come ready to play, like it’s a practice: glove, water, weather-appropriate clothing, and a helmet (loaners likely available); a bat is optional. Catcher’s gear is provided, and players are welcome to bring their own. Softball is indoors, so wear clean indoor shoes.

Q: Can parents stay?

A: Yes, and we encourage it, because timing on the day is fluid. Please give coaches and assessors space to run stations, and keep siblings clear of drill areas.

Q: How early should we arrive?

A: Arrive 15–20 minutes before your window for check-in, bibs, and a quick warm-up.

During assessments

Q: What skills are evaluated?

A: Baseball scores five tools: hitting, fielding, speed, pitching and catching (pitching and catching only for players who pitch or catch). Softball runs throwing and catching, a timed run, fielding, hitting and pitching. See What to Expect for details.

Q: How long will it take?

A: You sign up for an arrival time. Last year baseball used 15-minute arrival slots, with Majors-age players first and AAA after. Plan to stay until your group has finished every station.

Q: How are coaches involved?

A: Coaches run stations and assess players. If you plan to coach AAA or Majors, please sign up for a volunteer block on the assessments SignUpGenius when it opens. If you are a coach and cannot attend, email baseball@ncllball.com or softball@ncllball.com so coverage is arranged. New to volunteering? Visit the Volunteer Hub .

Q: Why do players wear bibs?

A: Bib numbers keep evaluations consistent and impartial. Assessors score the number, not the name, and the draft calculations stay number-based to reduce bias.

Q: How is scoring handled?

A: Coaches score each station from 1 to 5 using the same rubric. The scores combine into one composite score that forms the draft pool and informs placements. See how the draft works for how the pool feeds the draft and team assignments.

Q: What if my player is nervous?

A: Remind them it’s a practice-style workout. Effort, attitude, and listening to cues matter as much as results.

After assessments

Q: When are teams formed?

A: Drafts occur shortly after assessments; families are notified by team staff once rosters are set. See how the draft works for how teams are formed and assignments are finalized.

Q: What if my player is on the bubble?

A: Final placement depends on scores, roster sizes, and division needs. Younger players may be placed in the best-fit division for development.

Q: Can we appeal placement?

A: Placements are final. If you have a serious concern, email baseball@ncllball.com or softball@ncllball.com .
