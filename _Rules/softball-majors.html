Majors division softball rules

Majors is the highest standard softball division before Juniors. We balance competitive play with player development and a sustained love for the game — full player pitch, a faster tempo, district-level competition, and expanded strategy.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Softball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's City Interleague Rules for softball, which apply throughout — interleague play within District 8 is common at this level.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League A local league establishes the age structure for its divisions (Regulation V).
  • District 8 League ages 9 through 12 are eligible for Majors Softball.
  • NCLL League ages 10–12 is the registration target band.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL The highest standard softball division before Juniors — full player-pitch play with faster tempo, district-level competition, and expanded strategy, balanced against sustained development and love for the game.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 60-foot base paths, the same at every softball level (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance

  • Little League 40 feet, the Majors Softball standard — a step up from AAA's 35 feet (Rule 1.07 NOTE 3–4).

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8 A 12-inch Little League softball (yellow cover, raised seams).
  • NCLL The home team provides 2 balls at the plate meeting.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League One softball bat standard applies at every level. Non-wood bats must carry the current softball certification mark; maximum 34 in. length and 2¼ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Pine tar or a similar adhesive is now permitted on the handle or grip at every level (not on the barrel or taper, and not covering the certification mark). Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not.

B5. Home run line

  • District 8 200 feet — the first explicit home-run distance anywhere in the softball ladder (A, AA, and AAA don't use a fixed home-run line at all).

B6. Helmets & catcher's gear

  • Little League Batting helmets must be NOCSAE-approved and league-provided; the batter and all base runners must wear one (Rule 1.16). Anyone catching — including during warm-ups — needs full catcher's gear (Rule 1.17). Little League has no rule requiring a defensive/fielding face mask at any other position — that's a local or opponent-specific choice, not a Little League mandate, though some interleague opponents may require one.
  • NCLL Face masks on batting helmets are strongly encouraged, and often required in interleague play. A defensive mask is recommended for pitchers.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League The same underhand-windmill delivery rules apply as at every level (Rule 8.01).
  • NCLL Full kid pitch for the whole season. Rotate at least 3–4 pitchers across the season where possible. The core mechanical focus is command and changing speeds — introducing a change-up. Hold off on advanced spin pitches until a player shows stable core mechanics.

C2. Illegal pitch enforcement

  • NCLL Umpires enforce obvious replant or leaping violations. Early in the season, an instructional warning is used unless a real advantage was gained — the same standard as AAA.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League Little League Softball tracks innings pitched, not pitch counts. A pitcher may throw a maximum of 12 innings in a day; pitching 7 or more innings in a day requires 1 mandatory rest day; one pitch thrown counts as one full inning (Regulation VI(b)).
  • District 8 No more than 3 innings pitched per game through April 30 — the same District 8 workload cap as AAA.
  • NCLL No pitch counts — monitor fatigue and mechanics directly (a shortening arm circle, loss of control, posture breaking down).

C4. Catcher / pitcher overuse

  • NCLL Avoid a full game of catching plus a heavy pitching workload for the same player on the same day, where possible — guidance, not a numeric restriction.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League Little League never calls a balk in softball, at any level (Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter.
  • NCLL A standard 4-ball walk applies, with a 4/3 count and a foul ball with two strikes extending the at-bat. There's no walk-continuation restriction at Majors — that's specifically an AAA rule; a walked runner may advance normally here, subject only to the standard leadoff/steal timing (see Stealing & leadoffs, below).

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • NCLL 6 innings is regulation.

D2. Time limit

  • District 8 No new inning starts after 2 hours 15 minutes; no new batter after 2 hours 30 minutes.

D4. Mercy rule & run cap

  • NCLL Through April 30, there's no separate mercy threshold — just the 5-run cap per half-inning (a home run or ground-rule double still counts every run it drives in; the 6th inning and later has no cap, but is limited to one time through the batting order). After April 30, that cap is replaced by a tiered mercy rule: 15+ runs after 3 innings (2½ if the home team is ahead), 10+ after 4 (3½), or 8+ after 5 (4½). This before/after split doesn't appear at any lower softball division.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League In effect at Majors, unmodified — the batter becomes a runner on an uncaught third strike when first base is open, or occupied with two out (Rule 6.09(b)). This is new territory for a player moving up from AAA, where it's off.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League The softball-wide default at Majors and Juniors is that a runner may not leave a base until the pitch is released (Rule 7.13) — looser than the Minor-level "batted or reaches the batter" standard. Little League explicitly lets a league adopt the stricter Minor timing for Majors instead, if it chooses to — this is a real, open choice for NCLL to make, not settled by the rulebook alone.
  • NCLL Steals of any base are permitted once the ball is released. Interleague stealing-home allowances vary by opponent — confirm at the plate meeting.

D8. Bunting

  • NCLL Not restricted — situational hitting (runners in scoring position, advancing runners) is a live offensive skill goal at this level.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League Not permitted at Majors — the on-deck exception is Juniors-only (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1). A bat is in hand only when actually up to bat.

D11. Courtesy runner

  • Little League A courtesy runner is permitted for the pitcher and/or catcher of record with two outs; under a continuous batting order, the runner is the last player put out (Rule 7.14(b) + 3.04). A 2026 change permits courtesy-running for both the pitcher and catcher of record at once, if both are on base with two outs.

D12. Infield fly

  • Little League In effect at Majors, the full standard, unmodified by District 8 (Rule 2.00/6.05) — unlike A and AA, where it's off, and AAA, where District 8's lenient version applies.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL One team warning; a repeat may result in the batter being called out — the same structure as AAA.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out at Majors (Rule 7.08(a)(4)) — Juniors is the only softball division where this is permitted.
  • NCLL Feet-first on advances; headfirst only when returning to a base already occupied. Hook slides and pop-up slides for scoring plays are introduced later in the season.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • District 8 A continuous batting order.
  • NCLL A standard 4/3 count; a foul ball with two strikes extends the at-bat.

E2. Substitution

  • District 8 Free substitution on defense.

E3. Mandatory play

  • District 8 Every player must play at least 9 defensive outs, including at least 3 innings at an infield position — the same standard as AAA, with the same pre-game safety-exception process for one named player.

E4. Minimum players to start

  • District 8 An 8-player minimum — no game may be played with 7 or fewer.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • NCLL Added to the bottom of the batting order.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8 The home team uses the third-base dugout, the visiting team the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8 STACK Officials is used for umpire scheduling, the same threshold as AAA.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8 The scorekeeper is a home-team official, not a manager or coach.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8 A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.

F5. Pool players

  • District 8 Pool players are run by the league's Player Agent, and may include league-age-10/11 AAA players. A pool player may not pitch in the game they're used for, and still must meet the mandatory-play requirement.

G. Season structure

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL Required. All AAA and Majors players attend assessments in early February — especially important for 10-year-olds moving into Majors.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 3–4 events per week, Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays. Team formation lands mid-to-late February, practice starts in early March, games start in late March, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL Interleague play within District 8 is common at this level. Managers clarify any local rule adjustments at the plate meeting.

G9. End-of-season tournament

  • NCLL All teams are seeded, using a method communicated before the tournament; the bracket format (single or double elimination) is published before it starts.
  • District 8 A "World Cup"-style post-season — pool play, then the top two from each pool move to a single-elimination bracket. City and North End leagues are seeded separately.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League The same tournament-player eligibility baseline as baseball — participation in enough regular-season games, plus residency and birth-certificate verification.
  • NCLL League-age 10, 11, and 12 players feed the Majors/12's All-Star team. Tryouts for the 10's/11's/12's softball pool are held together, May 17 at Lower Woodland. The District 8 12's All-Star Tournament begins June 18 (2026) — the earliest-starting softball All-Star tournament of any age band.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • District 8 A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.
  • NCLL NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
