Senior division baseball rules

As a Double Goal League, NCLL balances two goals in everything below: player development and competitive success. Seniors plays on the same full 90-foot diamond as Juniors, but with rule flexibility that opens up only at this tier — most notably, a Senior team may run a traditional nine-player batting order with a Designated Hitter instead of the continuous batting order every other NCLL division uses. Seniors follows the Little League "green book" rules; what follows is where District 8 or NCLL adds to or clarifies those rules.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's Interleague Supplemental Rules for Senior Baseball, the version currently in effect for our interleague games. A separate District 7/District 8/District 9 Seniors Division agreement governs cross-district games, and overrides a handful of the D8-only rules below — those are called out explicitly where they apply.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League A local league establishes the age structure for this division (Regulation V).
  • District 8 League ages 12, 13, 14, 15, and 16 are eligible.
  • NCLL 15s and 16s are the core. Players as young as 13 are technically eligible.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League Senior League is Little League's oldest full-size-diamond division — the same 90-foot/60-foot-6-inch dimensions as Juniors, but with additional rule flexibility (batting nine, a Designated Hitter) that only becomes available at this tier.
  • NCLL The Senior Division is built for league-age 15-16 players who want Little League reps alongside high school baseball — maintaining varsity-level conditioning, keeping arms on safe workloads, and aligning with high school timelines. It's built to coexist with high school ball, not replace it.

A3. Participation in two divisions

  • Little League A local league may allow a player to dual-roster onto a second division, provided the player is Major-Division-eligible or above — trivially true at this age.
  • NCLL No NCLL source documents dual-rostering into or out of Seniors. Given the age gap from Majors/AAA, this isn't a practical overlap case the way it is for Majors and AAA.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 90 feet — the same full-size diamond as Juniors (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance & mound

  • District 8 60 feet 6 inches — the same full distance as Juniors.

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8 A Little League-approved Senior Division baseball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League The strictest bat standard in the whole rule set, and the only division where plain USABat isn't sufficient: bats must be BBCOR-certified, maximum 36 in. length, drop -3 (a 33-inch bat must weigh at least 30 oz). Wood bats are always fine. Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not. Any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must be a single solid color and may not be white or gray.
  • Note The most important equipment flag on this page — a bat that's perfectly legal at Intermediate or Juniors (plain USABat, no BBCOR needed) is illegal at Seniors. Families moving a player up need to be told this explicitly, more urgently than any other equipment change across the division-rules pages.

B5. Home run line

  • District 8 On a non-fenced field, the home-run line is marked by cones 300 feet from home plate — the same as Juniors.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League Same NOCSAE standard as every division (Rule 1.16).

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League / District 8 Full kid pitch, the entire game — unchanged from every division.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League Daily pitch-count maximum for league age 13–16 is 95 pitches. Pitchers league age 15–16 follow a separate, higher rest table than the standard table every other division doc in this project cites: 76+ pitches → 4 calendar days rest; 61–75 → 3 days; 46–60 → 2 days; 31–45 → 1 day; 1–30 → 0 days. A 14-year-old rostered on a Senior team still uses the standard ≤14 rest table (66+→4 / 51–65→3 / 36–50→2 / 21–35→1 / 1–20→0) — the table that applies is set by the individual pitcher's own league age, not their division. Under no circumstance may a player pitch on three consecutive days, at any age.
  • District 8 No player may throw more than 70 pitches in a game before May 1; the Regulation VI chart applies from May 1 on. A catcher who catches more than 3 innings may not pitch that day; a catcher who catches even 1 pitch in the 4th inning may not pitch that day either.
  • NCLL Local recording workflow (who the recorder is, GameChanger vs. paper, retention) is NCLL's own to set, same as every other division.

C4. Catcher / pitcher restriction

  • Little League / District 8 Same mechanic as every division — see Pitch counts, above.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League Balks are in effect at Seniors, same as Intermediate and Juniors. An illegal pitch with runners on base is a balk; runners advance one base.

C6. Mound visits

  • Little League New for 2026: the number of remaining mound visits does not prevent a pitcher who was removed from the mound from returning as pitcher later in the same game.
  • Note A 2026-specific change worth flagging for coaches this season — under the old understanding, a pitcher removed from the mound was done pitching for the game.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • District 8 A complete game is seven innings, same as Intermediate/Juniors.

D2. Time limit

  • District 8 No new inning starts after 2 hours 15 minutes; no new batter after 2 hours 30 minutes. A game past that reverts to the last completed inning; ties are allowed. For cross-district games, a different standard applies instead: no new inning within 15 minutes of the field's posted reservation end time, or within 30 minutes of the next scheduled game's start time.
  • NCLL Which standard applies depends on whether the specific game is D8-only or crosses district lines.

D3. Weekday curfew

  • District 8 Sunday–Thursday while school is in session, no new inning after 9:15 pm and no new batter after 9:30 pm — same as Intermediate/Juniors.

D4. Mercy rule

  • District 8 Shifted one inning later than Majors/AAA, since Seniors plays a 7-inning game: 15+ runs after 4 innings (3½ if home ahead), 10+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead), or 8+ after 6 (5½ if home ahead) — same tiers as Intermediate/Juniors.

D5. Runs per inning

  • Little League / District 8 No cap — same as Majors/Intermediate/Juniors, unlike AAA's 5-run half-inning limit.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League The standard dropped-third-strike rule is always in effect at Seniors, with no District 8 override — same as Majors/Intermediate/Juniors, unlike AAA, where it's turned off.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League Full leadoffs are allowed, same as Intermediate/Juniors. A runner may leave their base at any time.

D8. Bunting

  • Little League Bunting is permitted, unchanged from every division.

D9. Batter's-box rule

  • District 8 The standard batter's-box rules and penalty apply, identical to every other division.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League An on-deck batter is permitted, wearing a helmet — same as Intermediate/Juniors.
  • District 8 No bats in the dugout; practice swings only in the on-deck circle or batter's box, under a coach's direction. The on-deck batter stands on the same side as the current batter.

D11. Courtesy runner

  • District 8 A courtesy runner (the player who made the last out) is permitted for the catcher and/or pitcher of record with two outs — identical to every other division.

D12. Infield fly

  • Little League The standard infield fly rule applies, not restated or modified by District 8 — same as Majors/Intermediate/Juniors, not AAA's narrower local version.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • Note Not currently documented for Seniors specifically. Majors and AAA each have a warning-then-removal local rule for bat throwing — whether that extends to Seniors is a genuine open board decision, and in a co-league All-Star year, a partner league would need to agree to it too.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League Headfirst sliding while advancing is permitted, same as Intermediate/Juniors.

D15. Malicious contact

  • Little League A runner may not deliberately or maliciously crash into a fielder to jar the ball loose or cause injury; the runner must slide or attempt to avoid the fielder. Violation: the runner is called out, with ejection possible for egregious contact.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • Little League The only division where a traditional 9-player batting order is allowed at all — District 8's continuous-batting-order requirement carries an explicit Seniors exception. A Senior team may run the continuous batting order every other division uses, or a traditional bat-nine lineup with standard substitution and re-entry rules — the team or league picks. If a team bats nine, a Designated Hitter becomes available: the DH bats throughout for one player in the regular lineup, may re-enter once in the same batting slot, and only a player not already in the batting order can be the DH. Under a continuous batting order, there's no DH, since everyone already bats.
  • District 8 The default is free substitution under a continuous batting order, same as every other division. If a team elects to bat nine instead, the standard substitution/re-entry rules govern in place of the free-substitution default.
  • NCLL Not documented anywhere — the single most consequential open item on this page. Whether NCLL's Senior team runs a continuous batting order (matching every other NCLL division) or a traditional bat-nine lineup with a Designated Hitter available is a real, board-level choice with no default to fall back on.

E2. Substitution

  • Note Follows directly from whichever lineup format NCLL's Senior team uses (see Batting order, above) — not a separate decision.

E3. Mandatory play

  • Little League Mandatory Play Requirements do not apply for the Senior League Division — Little League itself drops the standard 6-defensive-outs-plus-1-at-bat floor entirely at this level.
  • District 8 District 8 adds a floor back in that Little League itself doesn't require: 9 defensive outs (not necessarily consecutive) plus one at-bat for each player — the only division where the District 8 supplement requires the at-bat explicitly, rather than relying on a continuous batting order to cover it automatically.
  • NCLL This requirement matters most if NCLL's Senior team bats nine (see Batting order, above) — under a continuous batting order everyone bats automatically, so the explicit at-bat requirement is redundant; under a bat-nine lineup, a manager benching players for part of the game needs to actively make sure everyone still gets their at-bat.

E4. Minimum players to start

  • District 8 A team may start and play with 8 present. The 8 bat; the 9th lineup slot is skipped with no penalty. For cross-district games, this differs: no borrowing a fielder from the opponent is allowed, and a team with only 8 present plays defense with 8.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • District 8 Under a continuous batting order, a late arrival goes to the bottom of the order; under a bat-nine lineup, standard substitution rules apply instead — subject to the open Batting order decision above.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8 The home team uses the third-base dugout; the visiting team uses the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8 Since 2024, the home team provides both umpires — plate and base — for Senior/Teen baseball games by default, not just the plate-umpire fallback other divisions use.
  • Note A real operational difference from Majors/AAA (home = plate only, and only as a fallback) worth flagging clearly for anyone scheduling Senior umpires.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8 Each team provides a scorekeeper who isn't a manager or coach; the home team's book is official. For cross-district games, the home team provides the official scorekeeper, with either team covering if the other can't.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8 At all times, offense and defense, at least one background-checked adult must be in the dugout.

F5. Game balls

  • Note District 8's Senior supplement states the ball type (see Ball standard, above) but doesn't say who provides them — genuinely NCLL's to set for the regular season. Little League Tournament Rules & Guidelines govern ball provision in a tournament context.

F6. Score & pitch-count reporting

  • Note No documented NCLL standard — a pure NCLL operations decision.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • Note No Senior-specific source found; District 8 doesn't address this at any tier.

G. Season structure

G1. Team formation

  • Little League Regulation V governs the draft.
  • NCLL NCLL runs its own Senior Division draft — tryouts pace players into a snake draft; managers evaluate hitting, defense, and pitching; every athlete lands on a roster with balanced pitching depth, the same structure as Juniors.

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL Required for Senior-division players, same as AAA, Majors, and Juniors.

G3. Playing up

  • District 8 League ages 12 through 16 are technically eligible for Seniors (see Age eligibility, above); NCLL's own practice narrows the core to 15s and 16s.
  • NCLL No formal approval gate beyond the standard draft/tryout process, same pattern as Juniors.

G4. Roster size

  • Little League 10–15 players — the same range as every division — with the same exception as Juniors: up to 20 players if NCLL fields only one regular-season Senior team.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 2–3 events per week (same as Juniors), specifically Monday/Wednesday games with Sunday practice. Fields: Greenlake or Lower Woodland #1 primarily, with occasional sessions at Bitter Lake or Magnuson. Registration stays open longer than other divisions to accommodate high-school baseball cut timelines. 90-foot field access is limited, the same constraint Juniors faces.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL NCLL fields its own Senior team via its own draft, playing a District 8 schedule against individual opponent towns — the identical opponent list to Juniors: Ballard, Queen Anne, Magnolia, Richmond, Northwest, and Northeast Seattle. Regular season feeds directly into District tournament play.

G7. Player pool & call-ups

  • Little League Mid-season replacement or movement of players is permitted under player-agent control at the rulebook level.
  • Note No Senior-specific call-up or pool-player program is documented. Would need the co-league partner's agreement in a co-league All-Star year — not the regular season, which NCLL runs independently.

G8. Standings

  • NCLL Standings and seeding appear to be tracked — the regular season feeds directly into District tournament play, which implies a seeding mechanism.

G9. End-of-season tournament

  • District 8 An end-of-year "World Cup"-style tournament: teams split into pools, the top 2 per pool advance to single-elimination, with seeding set about one month before the tournament. Not wood-bat — that requirement is specific to Juniors.
  • NCLL Regular-season standings feed District tournament seeding. No NCLL-internal Senior post-season tournament is documented — the same as Juniors, post-season runs entirely through the District 8/All-Star track.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League Minimum regular-season participation for Seniors specifically is 60% of the regular season, 6 games minimum — a different framing than the flat 8-game rule other divisions use. High school baseball players, and those with approved medical waivers, are exceptions to the minimum-play requirement.
  • NCLL League age 15–16 Baseball Seniors All-Star team, marked pending a coach and sufficient player interest, the same dual gating pattern as Juniors. NCLL's confirmed Senior co-league partners are RUG and Northwest Seattle — cited directly from the 2026 District 8 tournament brackets. Unlike every other division's tournament path, there is no District 8 Senior District tournament at all — the RUG/NCLL/Northwest co-op gets a direct bye to the Washington State Tournament. All three partners are fellow District 8 City-group leagues, not a suburban North-End partnership. Unlike Majors and Juniors, Seniors is not named among the 2026 divisions advancing to Regionals — so even with the direct bye to State, a Senior State win doesn't advance further in 2026.
  • Note It isn't confirmed whether NCLL's regular-season Senior team (see Team formation/Schedule type, above — NCLL's own independent draft and roster) is this same RUG+Northwest partnership year-round, or whether that partnership forms only for the All-Star tournament specifically, the same City-co-op-is-an-All-Star-thing pattern established for Juniors.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • Little League An umpire may warn, eject, and forfeit for unsportsmanlike conduct. An ejected manager, coach, or player is suspended for the team's next played game, on top of any further penalty the league imposes.
  • NCLL NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct. Beyond the automatic next-game suspension, NCLL reserves the right to require Board review of any ejection and to impose an escalating penalty for a manager's or coach's second ejection in a season.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
