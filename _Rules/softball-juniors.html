Juniors division softball rules

Juniors bridges Majors and higher-level competitive play, including future high school softball. The focus is refining fundamentals, expanding strategy, and continuing to grow character and team culture.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Softball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to the district-wide Juniors Interleague Rules for softball — a single doc covering the whole district, not split by City/North-End the way some other divisions are.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League: A local league establishes the age structure for its divisions (Regulation V).
  • District 8: League ages 12, 13, and 14 are eligible for Juniors Softball (15 with District Administrator approval).
  • NCLL: League ages 12–14 is the registration target band.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL: A bridge between Majors and higher-level competitive play, including future high school softball — refining fundamentals, expanding strategy, and continuing to grow character and team culture.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League: 60-foot base paths, the same at every softball level (Rule 1.04) — unlike baseball, where Juniors jumps to a full 90-foot diamond, softball's base paths never change.

B2. Pitching distance

  • Little League: 43 feet, the Juniors Softball standard — a step up from Majors' 40 feet (Rule 1.07 NOTE 3–4).

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8: A 12-inch Little League softball (raised seam).

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League: One softball bat standard applies at every level, including Juniors. Non-wood bats must carry the current softball certification mark; maximum 34 in. length and 2¼ in. barrel (Rule 1.10).

B5. Home run line

  • District 8: 200 feet, the same distance as Majors.

B6. Helmets & catcher's gear

  • Little League: Batting helmets must be NOCSAE-approved; the league provides 7 at Juniors (one more than at lower levels, since the on-deck batter needs one too). The batter, all base runners, and the on-deck batter must wear one (Rule 1.16). Anyone catching — including during warm-ups — needs full catcher's gear (Rule 1.17). Little League has no rule requiring a defensive/fielding face mask at any other position.
  • NCLL: A defensive mask is strongly recommended for infielders, especially the pitcher and third base. Full catcher's gear with a dangling throat guard is required.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League: The same underhand-windmill delivery rules apply as at every level (Rule 8.01).
  • NCLL: Full kid pitch. The 43-foot distance calls for deeper command and change-up sophistication. Encourage developing a change-up; hold off on rise, drop, and curve pitches until consistency is established.

C2. Illegal pitch enforcement

  • NCLL: Illegal pitches (replanting, illegal leaping) are enforced, with early-season corrective instruction encouraged — the same graduated approach as AAA and Majors.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League: Little League sets no pitching-workload restriction at Junior/Senior softball (unlike Minor/Majors' 12-innings-per-day-plus-rest-day rule).
  • District 8: A local, age-tiered structure fills that gap — the single most Juniors-specific rule in the softball ladder: a league-age-12 player follows the Minor/Majors-style limit (12 innings per day, 1 mandatory rest day after 7 or more), since a 12-year-old could be playing up into Juniors. A league-age-13 or 14 player has no restriction at all. A league-age-15 player may not pitch at Juniors. One pitch thrown counts as one full inning.
  • NCLL: Monitor pitching mechanics for overuse — posture collapsing, shoulder recoil, reduced hip drive.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League: Little League never calls a balk in softball, at any level (Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter.
  • NCLL: A standard 4-ball walk applies. There's no walk-continuation restriction — that's specifically an AAA rule.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • NCLL: 7 innings is regulation — the only softball division with a 7-inning game (A, AA, AAA, and Majors are all 6 or shorter).

D2. Time limit

  • District 8: No new inning starts after 2 hours 15 minutes; no new batter after 2 hours 30 minutes, the same precise figures as Majors.

D3. Weekday curfew

  • District 8: None — a real, stated divergence from baseball Juniors, which has an explicit weekday curfew.

D4. Mercy rule

  • NCLL: 15+ runs after 4 innings (3½ if the home team is ahead), 10+ after 5 (4½), or 8+ after 6 (5½) — shifted one inning later than Majors' tiers, to match the 7-inning game.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League: In effect at Juniors, the same as Majors — the batter becomes a runner on an uncaught third strike when first base is open, or occupied with two out (Rule 6.09(b)).

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League: A runner may not leave a base until the pitch is released — the same timing as Majors (Rule 7.13).
  • NCLL: Runners leave on release; aggressive reads on passed balls are encouraged, along with introducing delay-steal timing and pitcher-rhythm recognition. There's no District 8 restriction on stealing beyond the standard Little League rule — unlike AAA's walk-continuation restriction, which doesn't carry up to Juniors.

D8. Bunting

  • NCLL: Bunting, slapping, and power approaches can all be layered in as appropriate — the most tactically open framing of any softball division, with no restriction.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League: Permitted at Juniors — the one softball division where the on-deck circle is allowed at all (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1). A helmet is required for the on-deck batter, same as the batter.

D11. Courtesy runner

  • Little League: A courtesy runner is permitted for the pitcher and/or catcher of record with two outs; under a continuous batting order, the runner is the last player put out (Rule 7.14(b) + 3.04). A 2026 change permits courtesy-running for both the pitcher and catcher of record at once, if both are on base with two outs.

D12. Infield fly

  • Little League: In effect at Juniors, the full standard, unmodified by District 8 (Rule 2.00/6.05) — same as Majors.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League: Headfirst sliding while advancing is permitted at Juniors — the one softball division where this flips on; it's out at every level below (Rule 7.08(a)(4)).
  • NCLL: A variety of slides — hook, pop-up, backdoor — are taught progressively, now that headfirst sliding is a live, legal technique at this level.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • District 8: A continuous batting order, unless tournament rules specify otherwise.

E2. Substitution

  • District 8: Free substitution under the continuous batting order for the regular season.

E3. Mandatory play

  • District 8: Every player must play at least 9 defensive outs — Juniors drops the "at least 3 innings at an infield position" sub-requirement that A, AAA, and Majors all have.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • NCLL: Added to the bottom of the batting order.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8: The home team uses the third-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8: STACK Officials is used for umpire scheduling, the same threshold as AAA and Majors.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8: The scorekeeper is a home-team official, not a manager or coach.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8: A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.

F5. Pool players

  • District 8: Pool players are run by the league's Player Agent, and may include league-age-12 Majors softball players. A pool player may not pitch in the game they're used for, and still must meet the mandatory-play requirement.

G. Season structure

G1. Team formation

  • NCLL: Players are evaluated tryout-style before the season. Formation targets a balance of pitching and catching distribution across teams. Every registered player is placed on a team.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL: 3–4 events per week, spread across Fridays, Sundays, Mondays, and Tuesdays — the only softball division with a 4-day-of-week spread. Team formation lands mid-to-late February, practice starts in early March, games start in late March, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL: Real, district-wide interleague and District scheduling is common. Managers clarify local adjustments (run rules, time limits) at the plate meeting.

G8. Standings

  • NCLL: May be maintained, depending on the district.

G9. Post-season

  • NCLL: Post-season opportunities (District, All-Stars) are communicated mid-season.
  • District 8: A "World Cup"-style tournament — pool play, then the top two from each pool move to a single-elimination bracket.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League: The same tournament-player eligibility baseline as baseball, requiring participation in at least 60% of the regular season, plus residency and birth-certificate verification.
  • NCLL: League ages 13–14 feed the Juniors All-Star team, which may combine with another league's roster as a "co-league" team when needed — softball co-leagues are more common at the Juniors and Seniors levels than in baseball. The District 8 Softball Juniors All-Star Tournament begins June 29 (2026).

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • District 8: A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.
  • NCLL: NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL: The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL: When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL: Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
