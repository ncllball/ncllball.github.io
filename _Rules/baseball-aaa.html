AAA division baseball rules

As a Double Goal League, NCLL balances two goals in everything below: player development and competitive success. AAA follows the Little League "green book" rules; what follows is where District 8 or NCLL adds to or clarifies those rules.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's Interleague Supplemental Rules for AAA Baseball, the version currently in effect for our interleague games.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League Any player who turns 7 before September 1, 2026 and does not turn 13 before August 31, 2026 is eligible for Minor League (Regulation IV(a)) — subject to the local league's own alignment of that division; NCLL's AAA/AA/A split is exactly that alignment.
  • District 8 League ages 7 through 11 are eligible for AAA.
  • NCLL AAA is primarily a 10s-and-11s division. 9s fill roster spots by assessment when Majors doesn't need them first; 8s are rare but not impossible. League-age-12 players aren't eligible for AAA — they must be drafted to Majors.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL AAA is NCLL's full-kid-pitch division below Majors — full-rules, full-diamond baseball with a run cap and a shorter clock as the main concessions to age. Teams are formed by a full annual draft, and a meaningful share of AAA's own roster is simultaneously eligible for call-ups to Majors games (see Player pool & call-ups, below). It's the direct feeder into Majors.

A3. Playing in two divisions

  • Little League A local league may allow an age-eligible player to participate in two regular season divisions in the same season — but only for Major Division and above (Regulation I NOTE 2). Three conditions all apply: (1) the player must be age-appropriate for both divisions under Regulation IV(a)'s age bands; (2) the player must be otherwise eligible under all standard conditions; and (3) the local league's Board of Directors must affirmatively decide to allow it — the Board sets age structure and may instead limit a player to one division/team, even if they'd otherwise qualify for two (Regulation IV(a)). This allowance does not extend to Tee Ball or Minor League — Regulation I NOTE 2 states players there "shall not participate in more than one division" (AAA is a Minor League sub-level, so this exclusion is why NCLL's call-up mechanism below works differently, not off this provision). For tournament team membership specifically, a player may be on only one tournament team at a time — once eliminated, they can be added to a second team if eligible, there's roster space, and they're not replacing another eligible player (Tournament Condition 3). Separately, Regulation V(c) is the actual governing provision for the call-up program below: a league short of players for a game may pool players from one division below, assigned by the player agent on a rotating basis; a pool player may not pitch in that game, and must play at least nine consecutive defensive outs and bat once.
  • NCLL Roughly 15 AAA players per season are designated "Majors Call-Up" at draft time — NCLL's implementation of Regulation V(c)'s player pool for the AAA-to-Majors direction. Their roster stays solely AAA — they are not dual-rostered onto a Majors team. They're called up to individual Majors games as GameChanger availability and need dictate, then return to AAA; per Regulation V(c), a Call-Up player may not pitch in a Majors game and must play at least nine consecutive defensive outs and bat once when called up.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 60-foot base paths, the same as Majors (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance & mound

  • Little League 46 feet, the same as Majors (Rule 1.07).
  • District 8 A portable mound is fine, same as Majors.

B3. Ball standard

  • Little League A regulation hardball (Rule 1.09), same as Majors.
  • District 8 The home team supplies the plate umpire with at least 2 new game balls before the game.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League Non-wood bats must carry the USABat mark; BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited; maximum 33 in. length and 2⅝ in. barrel (Rule 1.10) — the same standard as Majors, not a separate AAA rule. BBCOR bats aren't permitted at this level (that starts at Intermediate). Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not. Any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must be a single solid color and may not be white or gray.

B5. Home run line

  • District 8 On a non-fenced field, the home-run line is marked by cones 180 feet from home plate — a shorter line than Majors' 200 feet.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League Same NOCSAE standard as every division (Rule 1.16).

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League / District 8 Full kid pitch, the entire game — same as Majors.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League Daily pitch-count maximum by league age (Regulation VI) — the same chart that applies at every division; a pitcher's limit is set by their own league age, not their division.
  • District 8 No player may throw more than 50 pitches in a game before May 1; the Regulation VI chart applies from May 1 on.
  • NCLL Refer to the official pitch-count and rest-day guidelines by league age. Every player who wants to pitch should get the opportunity to learn and develop the skill.

C4. Catcher / pitcher restriction

  • Little League A player who has caught 4 or more innings in a game may not pitch that day (Regulation VI). A player who caught 3 innings or fewer, then pitches 21 or more pitches, may not return to catcher that same day — unless they reached 20 pitches mid-batter, in which case they keep catcher eligibility until that at-bat ends.
  • NCLL Per rulebook — a pitcher who reaches 21 or more pitches may not catch for the remainder of the game.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League Walks and hit-by-pitch are standard. Balks are not called at AAA and below (Rule 2.00, Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter.

C5a. Intentional walks

  • NCLL An intentional walk must be completed with thrown pitches, and every pitch still counts toward the pitcher's pitch count — an intentional walk can't be used to bypass pitch-count limits.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • Little League / District 8 A complete game is six innings, same as Majors.

D2. Time limit

  • District 8 No new inning starts after 2 hours; no new batter after 2 hours 15 minutes — tighter than Majors' 2:15/2:30. A game past 2:15 reverts to the last completed inning; ties are allowed. Doubleheaders: no new batter after 1:50 (game 1) or 2:15 (game 2).
  • NCLL Applies to interleague and in-house games alike.

D3. Weekday curfew

  • District 8 Sunday–Thursday while school is in session, no new inning after 8:30 pm and no new batter after 8:45 pm — same as Majors.
  • NCLL Applied to in-house games too, for consistency with interleague.

D4. Mercy rule

  • Little League / District 8 A game is called if a team leads by 15+ runs after 3 innings (2½ if the home team is ahead), 10+ after 4 (3½ if home ahead), or 8+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead) — the same tiers as Majors (Rule 4.10(e)). A local league may adopt the option of not using this rule at all.

D5. Runs per inning

  • District 8 A half-inning ends after 3 outs, when 5 runs have scored, or when the entire batting order has batted, whichever comes first — a rule Majors doesn't have at all. A home run or ground-rule double still counts every run it drives in, even past the 5-run cap. In the 6th inning and later, there's no 5-run limit, but a team is capped at one time through its batting order.
  • NCLL The 5-run cap is in effect, with the home-run/ground-rule-double exception and the late-inning exception above.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League The dropped-third-strike mechanic is scoped to Majors, Intermediate, Junior, and Senior League only (Rule 6.09(b)).
  • District 8 Not in effect for AAA — a direct override confirming the rulebook's own Minor League scoping.
  • NCLL A dropped third strike is simply an out, whether caught or not — this flips at Majors, so a coach or player moving between AAA and Majors should know the rule changes. A foul tip doesn't count as a third strike and the batter stays alive; a caught foul tip does count as a third strike.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League No leadoffs (Rule 7.13) — a runner may not leave a base until the ball has reached the batter. Stealing is permitted once the ball reaches the batter.
  • District 8 AAA has a restriction Majors doesn't: delayed steals aren't allowed. A runner may steal only on a straight steal once the ball crosses the plate, on a wild pitch or passed ball, or on an overthrow back to the pitcher. Once the pitcher has the ball back, the play is dead and a runner in a secondary lead must return to their base.

D8. Bunting

  • Little League Bunting, including the drag bunt, is permitted — same as Majors.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League No on-deck batter in AAA and below (Rule 1.08).
  • District 8 No bats in the dugout or bench area; no swinging a bat outside the batter's box except practice swings under a coach's direction — same as Majors.
  • NCLL No warm-up circle — the on-deck batter should be in the dugout, with bats kept under control.

D11. Courtesy runner

  • Little League / District 8 A Local League Option: a courtesy runner (the player who made the last out) is permitted for the catcher and/or pitcher of record with two outs — identical to Majors.

D12. Infield fly

  • Little League The standard infield fly rule (Rule 6.05(d)) — a catchable fair fly with runners on first and second, or the bases loaded, and fewer than two out — is a national rule with only one exemption: Tee Ball. It isn't a Majors-only rule; AAA isn't exempt, D8 just replaces the standard trigger with its own narrower version below.
  • District 8 AAA gets its own, narrower and more lenient version: it applies only with runners on first and second (not bases-loaded), with 0 or 1 out, and umpires should call it only when the ball will "more likely than not" be caught in the infield with little effort.
  • NCLL Per D8 — the narrower trigger and the leniency instruction to umpires are both in effect at AAA.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL First offense: warning. Second offense: the player is removed from the game for safety, at the umpire's discretion.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out (Rule 7.08(a)(4)).
  • NCLL Sliding is allowed and should be encouraged — headfirst slides aren't, for safety.

D15. Malicious contact

  • Little League A runner is called out for failing to either slide or attempt to get around a fielder who has the ball and is waiting to make the tag. There's no "must slide" rule; going around the fielder without contact satisfies this just as well as sliding does. Separately, an umpire has discretionary authority to eject a player for unsportsmanlike conduct, including egregious or intentional contact — that's not an automatic consequence of this rule.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • Little League / District 8 Continuous batting order is required, same as Majors — a traditional 9-batter order isn't allowed. Late arrivals go to the bottom of the order.
  • NCLL All teams follow a straight batting lineup of every player, regardless of fielding status — no substitutions or pinch hitting. A player who's injured and can't bat is scratched from the lineup. The batting order itself is fixed once set; fielding positions are freely substitutable.

E2. Substitution

  • District 8 Free defensive substitution is allowed, same as Majors.

E3. Mandatory play

  • Little League Every rostered player present at the start must play at least 6 defensive outs and bat at least once (Regulation IV(i)). A league using 15–20 player rosters with 15 or more players present at a game may reduce this floor to 3 defensive outs and 1 at-bat.
  • District 8 District 8 raises the mandatory-play floor to 9 defensive outs (not necessarily consecutive), recommended with at least 3 at an infield position.
  • NCLL Every player must have at least one at-bat and play a minimum of 9 outs in the field during a six-inning game. If the game is shortened to 4 or 5 innings, that floor drops to a minimum of 6 outs. Coaches should still aim to get each player at least two at-bats, acknowledged as harder in pitching-duel or shorter games.

E4. Minimum players to start

  • District 8 A team may start and play with 8. The 8 bat; the 9th lineup slot is skipped with no penalty. On defense, the opposing team lends a fielder — their last batting out — same mechanic as Majors.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • District 8 A player arriving after the game starts is added to the bottom of the batting order.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8 The home team uses the third-base dugout; the visiting team uses the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8 If a District 8 umpire is unavailable, the home team supplies the plate umpire and the visiting team supplies the field umpire.
  • NCLL The league provides a plate umpire for regular-season games, adding a field umpire for playoffs. If no umpire is available at game time, the home team provides one — calling from behind the rubber if they lack gear. The visiting team may add a second field umpire with the home team's Chief Umpire's approval; the Chief Umpire has final say on all calls.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8 Each team provides a scorekeeper who isn't a manager or coach. The home team's book is official; if the home team can't field a non-coach scorekeeper, the visiting team's book becomes official.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8 At all times, offense and defense, at least one background-checked adult must be in the dugout.

F5. Game balls

  • Little League The league supplies the umpire with game balls, and must ensure additional balls are available if needed; the umpire keeps at least two alternates on hand throughout the game (Rule 3.01(c)-(e)).
  • District 8 The home team gives the plate umpire at least 2 new game balls before the game.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • NCLL Each team gets 15 minutes to warm up before first pitch. The visiting team warms up first, then the home team. If 30 minutes isn't available before the scheduled start, the two teams agree on a mutual shorter warm-up. The field belongs to the umpire at the scheduled start time — a request to warm up past that point needs the umpire's approval. A team not ready at its scheduled slot forfeits that warm-up time.

G. Season structure

G1. Team formation

  • Little League Regulation V governs the draft.
  • NCLL A full redraft happens every year, adopting the same process as Majors: draft order is drawn from a hat, in two linked stages — returning AAA players first, then players new to AAA. A coach's own child (or a confirmed assistant coach's child) is auto-slotted by their assessment rating. A short post-draft trade window is allowed, for roster fit, not talent acquisition. AAA drafted before COVID, moved away from it for a time, then returned to drafting in 2024 after a board review found NCLL was the only District 8-area league not drafting its AAA division.

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL AAA and Majors players are assessed together at the same event, evaluated on the same skills, with the same bib-number blind scoring. Every player makes a team.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL Same season cadence as Majors — 3–4 events per week, typically Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL AAA runs primarily an in-house regular season under NCLL's own rules, with interleague games against other District 8 leagues layered in — the same structural pattern as Majors. For interleague games, the two managers agree on ground rules, which may mean reverting to standard Little League rules.

G7. Player pool & call-ups

  • NCLL See Playing in two divisions, above — the Majors Call-Up program is the same program, described there from the AAA roster-of-record side.

G9. End-of-season tournament

  • District 8 An end-of-season Tournament of Champions is held for North-End teams; seasons for City Leagues and Shoreline culminate with internal league playoff tournaments — the same provision that governs Majors.
  • NCLL NCLL runs its own internal AAA Championship rather than the North-End tournament, the same structural non-participation as Majors (NCLL is a District 8 City league, not North-End). Every team competes, double-elimination — but unlike Majors, AAA's seeding is decided before the season begins, not by random draw at tournament time.

G10. All-Star team

  • NCLL Feeds into the same District 8 All-Star program as Majors — All-Star teams are age-banded, not division-banded, so an AAA-age player who makes a team joins the same tournament structure a Majors-age player would.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • Little League An umpire may warn and eject for unsportsmanlike conduct. An ejected manager, coach, or player is suspended for the team's next played game, on top of any further penalty the league imposes. Forfeiture is separate from ejection and only applies for one of six specific grounds — refusing to start or continue play, failing to resume within a minute of being ordered to, failing to remove a player when ordered, willfully and persistently violating rules after a warning, or delay tactics (Rule 4.15) — not for unsportsmanlike conduct generally.
  • NCLL NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct. Beyond the automatic next-game suspension, NCLL reserves the right to require Board review of any ejection and to impose an escalating penalty for a manager's or coach's second ejection in a season.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
