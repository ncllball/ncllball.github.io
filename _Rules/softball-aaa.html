AAA division softball rules

As a Double Goal League, we emphasize both player development and competitive success. AAA is often a player's first full season of player pitch — the goals are fundamentals, confidence, playing multiple positions, and building situational awareness and strategy.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Softball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's City Interleague Rules for softball — the first division in the softball ladder where that supplement is genuinely load-bearing for the regular season.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League A local league establishes the age structure for its divisions (Regulation V).
  • District 8 League ages 8 through 11 are eligible for AAA Softball.
  • NCLL League ages 9–11 is the registration target band, with 10-year-olds specifically directed to register here. A player with a fall or winter birthday may play down with their grade-level peers.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL Often a player's first full season of player pitch. The goals are fundamentals, confidence, playing multiple positions, and building situational awareness and strategy.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 60-foot base paths, the same at every softball level (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance

  • Little League 35 feet, the Minor Softball standard (Rule 1.07 NOTE 3–4).

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8 An official 11-inch Little League softball (yellow cover, raised seams).

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League One softball bat standard applies at every level. Non-wood bats must carry the current softball certification mark; maximum 34 in. length and 2¼ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Pine tar or a similar adhesive is now permitted on the handle or grip at every level (not on the barrel or taper, and not covering the certification mark). Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not.
  • NCLL Discourage a bat that's too heavy for the player to swing with good mechanics.

B6. Helmets & catcher's gear

  • Little League Batting helmets must be NOCSAE-approved and league-provided; the batter and all base runners must wear one (Rule 1.16). Anyone catching — including during warm-ups — needs a mask with a throat guard, a chest protector, leg guards, and a catcher's mitt (Rule 1.17). Little League has no rule requiring a defensive/fielding face mask at any other position — Rule 1.14 only requires a glove; that's an NCLL or District 8 local choice, not a Little League mandate.
  • NCLL Face masks on batting helmets are strongly recommended, and required in some interleague play. A fielding mask is strongly recommended for infielders, and mandatory for pitchers where a local policy has adopted it.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League The same underhand-windmill delivery rules apply as at every level (Rule 8.01).
  • NCLL Full kid pitch — any rostered player may pitch if she demonstrates safe mechanics in practice. Multiple pitchers are encouraged; development outweighs short-term game outcome at this level. Focus stays on command before movement — riseballs, drops, and curves aren't required yet.

C2. Illegal pitch enforcement

  • NCLL Umpires call obvious illegal pitches — crow-hopping, replanting the pivot foot. Early in the season, most violations are handled as instructional warnings unless they create a real advantage.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League Little League Softball tracks innings pitched, not pitch counts. A pitcher may throw a maximum of 12 innings in a day; pitching 7 or more innings in a day requires 1 mandatory rest day; one pitch thrown counts as one full inning (Regulation VI(b)).
  • District 8 No more than 3 innings pitched per game through April 30 — a District 8 workload cap on top of the Little League baseline.
  • NCLL Managers watch for fatigue directly — loss of control, a dropping elbow, a slowing arm circle, decreased velocity — and rotate pitchers early in the season so more players gain circle experience before playoffs.

C4. Catcher / pitcher overuse

  • NCLL To reduce overuse, avoid having the same player catch a full game and then pitch significant innings the same day where possible — guidance, not a numeric restriction the way baseball's catcher/pitcher rule works.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League Little League never calls a balk in softball, at any level (Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter.
  • NCLL A standard 4-ball walk applies. A hit batter is awarded first base; batters are instructed not to bail out early, and to turn away properly instead.

D. Game play

D2. Time limit

  • NCLL 6 innings or 2 hours, whichever comes first. No new inning should start once the time limit reaches 1 hour 50 minutes, unless both managers and the umpire agree and daylight allows. Aim for efficient, 2-minute changeovers to maximize innings played.

D4. Mercy rule

  • NCLL A 10-run rule after 4 innings (3½ if the home team is ahead). Managers may continue with an instructional scrimmage if both agree, but the official score ends at that point.

D5. Runs per inning

  • NCLL A 5-run limit per half-inning. The final declared inning (or 6th) is open — no cap — to allow a comeback opportunity; the umpire and managers confirm this before it starts.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League Off at AAA — the dropped-third-strike mechanic only applies at Majors and Juniors (Rule 6.09(b)); this is the Little League default at Minor Softball, not a local choice.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League No leadoffs at any softball level. AAA is Minor Softball, so a runner may not leave a base until the pitch is batted or reaches the batter (Rule 7.13) — the looser "on release" timing is a Little League option written only for the Major Division, with no equivalent at Minor.
  • NCLL Stealing 2nd and 3rd base is permitted once the ball is batted or reaches the batter. Advancing home on a passed ball, wild pitch, or overthrow is permitted unless a local pre-season notice says otherwise. A walked runner may NOT advance further while the pitcher has the ball in the circle — this "continuation" restriction is the single most AAA-specific rule in the softball ladder (advancing on a wild pitch for ball four is not continuation, and is still permitted). No delayed steal attempts before the pitcher releases the ball.

D8. Bunting

  • NCLL Bunting is optional — use it sparingly, focusing first on core swing development.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League No on-deck position at Minor Softball (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1).

D11. Courtesy runner

  • Little League A courtesy runner is permitted for the pitcher and/or catcher of record with two outs; under a continuous batting order, the runner is the last player put out (Rule 7.14(b) + 3.04). A 2026 change permits courtesy-running for both the pitcher and catcher of record at once, if both are on base with two outs.

D12. Infield fly

  • District 8 In effect, but lenient — umpires call it only when the ball will "more likely than not" be caught with little effort, the same judgment-call standard baseball's AAA division uses.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL One warning per team for an accidentally thrown bat; subsequent occurrences may result in the batter being declared out for safety.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out at Minor Softball (Rule 7.08(a)(4)) — there's no rule requiring a runner to slide at all.
  • NCLL Feet-first sliding is encouraged on close plays. A headfirst slide while advancing isn't allowed; a headfirst return to a base already occupied is permitted.

D16. Overthrows & live ball

  • NCLL The ball stays live on an overthrow unless it enters dead-ball territory, in which case standard base awards apply. Runners should listen to their base coaches and commit decisively to the next base.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • District 8 A continuous batting order.
  • NCLL A late-arriving player is added to the bottom of the order, without penalty. A player who can't continue due to injury or illness has their spot in the order skipped — not counted as an automatic out.

E2. Substitution

  • District 8 Free substitution on defense.

E3. Mandatory play

  • District 8 Every player must play at least 9 defensive outs, including at least 3 innings at an infield position — the same numeric standard baseball uses, and a step up from AA's softer "at least 1 infield inning when feasible." A pre-game safety exception process exists for one named player who needs to be excused.

E4. Minimum players to start

  • District 8 An 8-player minimum — no game may be played with 7 or fewer.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • NCLL Added to the bottom of the batting order (see Batting order, above).

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8 The home team uses the third-base dugout, the visiting team the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8 STACK Officials is used for umpire scheduling — the first division in the softball ladder to require this (A and AA have no such requirement). If an assigned plate umpire is unavailable at game time, the home team supplies a qualified adult; managers mutually agree on plate vs. behind-the-pitcher positioning. During playoffs or interleague play, a second (base) umpire may be provided. The plate umpire has final authority on rules interpretation.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8 The scorekeeper is a home-team official, not a manager or coach.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8 A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.

F5. Pool players

  • District 8 Pool players are run by the league's Player Agent, rotating through the pool one game at a time, and may include league-age-9 AA players — a real playing-up mechanism. A pool player may not pitch in the game they're used for, and still must meet the mandatory-play requirement (see Mandatory play, above).

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • NCLL About 15 minutes of shared infield/outfield time per team, if field conditions allow. If a team is late due to a prior game running long, managers agree on a condensed format — infield only, for example.

G. Season structure

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL Required. All AAA and Majors players attend assessments in early February — especially important for 9-year-olds in AAA and 10-year-olds in Majors.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 3 events per week, Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays. Team formation lands mid-to-late February, practice starts in early March, games start in late March, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL AAA softball teams may schedule games with neighboring District 8 leagues — the first division in the softball ladder with confirmed, unambiguous interleague play. Managers clarify and agree on any local rule variances (run limits, stealing home, courtesy runners) at the plate meeting.

G9. End-of-season tournament

  • NCLL All teams participate in a season-ending bracket for the division championship; the format (single or double elimination) and seeding details are communicated before tournament week.
  • District 8 A "World Cup"-style post-season — pool play, then the top two from each pool move to a single-elimination bracket. City and North End leagues are seeded separately.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League The same tournament-player eligibility baseline as baseball — participation in enough regular-season games, plus residency and birth-certificate verification.
  • NCLL League-age 8, 9, and 10 AAA-eligible players feed the softball "10's" All-Star pool. The District 8 Softball 10's All-Star Tournament begins July 11 (2026). Tryouts for the 10's/11's/12's softball All-Star pool are held May 17 at Lower Woodland.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • District 8 A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.
  • NCLL NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL All regular on-field participants must complete required volunteer clearances and background checks. Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team but should stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
