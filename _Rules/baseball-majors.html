Majors division baseball rules

As a Double Goal League, NCLL balances two goals in everything below: player development and competitive success. Majors follows the Little League "green book" rules; what follows is where District 8 or NCLL adds to or clarifies those rules.

Two terms come up throughout this page: Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's Interleague Supplemental Rules for Majors Baseball, the version currently in effect for our interleague games.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League: League ages 9–12 are eligible for Majors (Regulation I). Every league-age-12 player must be drafted to a Major Division team — a local league cannot hold a 12-year-old back.
  • District 8: All players league age 9, 10, 11, and 12 are eligible for Majors.
  • NCLL: 10s, 11s, and 12s fill AAA/Majors roster spots first. A skilled 9-year-old may earn a Majors or AAA spot if roster space remains after 10s and 11s are placed, based on their January assessment results.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League: Majors is Little League's full-rules, player-pitch division — the level Minor League play exists to prepare kids for.
  • NCLL: Majors is NCLL's top small-diamond division for league ages 9–12 — 60-foot bases, 46-foot mound, full Little League rules. Teams are formed by an annual draft and play a mix of in-house and District 8 interleague games, with the season ending in NCLL's own Majors Championship tournament. It's also the springboard into District 8 All-Star tournament play.

A3. Playing in two divisions

  • Little League: A local league may allow an age-eligible player to play in two divisions in the same season (Regulation I NOTE 2). A player may be on only one tournament team.
  • NCLL: NCLL runs a Majors Call-Up program: a group of AAA players are rostered on both a AAA team and a Majors team, practicing with both and called up to Majors games as needed based on availability (see Player pool & call-ups, below).

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League: 60-foot base paths (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance & mound

  • Little League: 46 feet from the front of the pitching rubber to the back point of home plate (Rule 1.07); the rubber sits 6 inches above home plate (Rule 1.04).
  • District 8: A portable mound set at 46 feet, front of rubber to back corner of home plate. The entire game is played kid-pitch.

B3. Ball standard

  • Little League: A regulation hardball meeting Little League specifications — 5 to 5¼ oz., 9 to 9¼ in. circumference (Rule 1.09).
  • District 8: The home team supplies the plate umpire with at least 2 new game balls before the game.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League: Bats must meet the USA Baseball Bat standard (USABat) and carry the USA Baseball stamp, with a maximum barrel diameter of 2⅝ in.; BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited (Rule 1.10). Solid one-piece wood bats are exempt from the stamp requirement. Pine tar and similar adhesives are permitted; any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must be a single solid color and may not be white or gray; jewelry is permitted.
  • Note: Check usabat.com's decertified-bat list before buying a new bat — one older model (the 30" Easton Ghost X (-10)) is currently decertified.

B5. Home run line

  • Little League: A recommended minimum outfield fence distance of 200 feet; a ball leaving the field less than 165 feet from home is a two-base hit, not a home run (Rule 1.04, Rule 6.09(d)).
  • District 8: On a non-fenced field, the home-run line is marked by cones 200 feet from home plate.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League: Batting helmets must be NOCSAE-certified, non-glare, unaltered by paint, and free of tape (Rule 1.16). Mandatory for the batter, all base runners, and player base coaches. Team decals and stickers are fine as long as they aren't excessive or offensive. A player who loses a helmet during play is warned, not called out.
  • NCLL: Team logo decals, player-name stickers, and achievement stickers are fine within that same limit (Rule 1.16) — no repainting, no tape.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League: Majors is a full player-pitch division.
  • District 8: Full kid pitch, the entire game.
  • NCLL: Per District 8 — full kid pitch.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League: Daily pitch-count maximum by league age — 11–12: 85 pitches, 9–10: 75 (Regulation VI). Required rest: 66+ pitches → 4 days, 51–65 → 3, 36–50 → 2, 21–35 → 1, 1–20 → 0. No pitcher may pitch on three consecutive days, and a Majors pitcher may not pitch in more than one game per day.
  • District 8: No player may throw more than 50 pitches in a game before May 1; the Regulation VI chart above applies from May 1 on.
  • NCLL: The home team is the official scorekeeper and pitch-count recorder for both teams (Regulation VI(e)); counts are compared at the end of each inning, and managers exchange pitcher-eligibility information at the pre-game plate meeting. Violations are charged to the offending manager: 1st offense = warning, 2nd = one-game suspension, 3rd = season suspension.

C4. Catcher / pitcher restriction

  • Little League: A player who has caught 4 or more innings in a game may not pitch that day (Regulation VI). A player who caught 3 innings or fewer, then pitches 21 or more pitches, may not return to catcher that same day.
  • District 8: A player who is the defensive catcher for more than three innings is not eligible to pitch that day.
  • NCLL: Per Regulation VI — a pitcher who throws 21 or more pitches may not catch that day.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League: Walks and hit-by-pitch are standard. Balks are not called in Majors and below (Rule 2.00, Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter, and runners advance only if forced.
  • Note: This one surprises coaches new to Majors — a pickoff attempt just can't be an illegal quick-return pitch.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • Little League: A regulation game is six innings (Rule 4.10(a)). A game called after 4 completed innings (3½ if the home team is ahead) is official.

D2. Time limit

  • District 8: No new inning starts after 2 hours 15 minutes; no new batter after 2 hours 30 minutes. A game past 2:30 reverts to the last completed inning. Ties are allowed. Doubleheaders: no new batter after 1:50 (game 1) or 2:15 (game 2).
  • NCLL: Applies to interleague and in-house games alike.

D3. Weekday curfew

  • District 8: Sunday–Thursday while school is in session, no new inning after 8:30 pm and no new batter after 8:45 pm.
  • NCLL: Applied to in-house games too, for consistency with interleague.

D4. Mercy rule

  • Little League: A game is called if a team leads by 15+ runs after 3 innings (2½ if the home team is ahead), 10+ after 4 (3½ if home ahead), or 8+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead) (Rule 4.10(e)).
  • District 8: Identical tiers — 15+ after 3 (2½ if home ahead), 10+ after 4 (3½ if home ahead), 8+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead).

D5. Runs per inning

  • NCLL: No limit on runs scored in an inning — the 5-run cap used in lower divisions doesn't apply at Majors.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League: In effect for Majors (Rules 5.09(b)/6.09(b)) — the batter becomes a runner on an uncaught third strike when first base is open, or occupied with two out.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League: No leadoffs (Rule 7.13) — a runner may not leave a base until the ball has reached the batter. Stealing is permitted once the ball reaches the batter.

D8. Bunting

  • Little League: Bunting, including the drag bunt, is permitted (Rule 2.00).

D9. Batter's-box rule

  • Little League: The batter must keep at least one foot in the box throughout the at-bat, with listed exceptions (a swing, being forced out by a pitch, a drag-bunt attempt, and others) (Rule 6.03). Penalty: a warning, then a called strike.
  • District 8: Same rule, restated verbatim.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League: No on-deck batter in Majors and below (Rule 1.08). Only the next half-inning's leadoff batter may be outside the dugout between innings, and may not pick up a bat until it's their turn to hit.
  • District 8: No bats in the dugout or bench area; no swinging a bat outside the batter's box except practice swings under a coach's direction.

D11. Courtesy runner

  • Little League: A courtesy runner is permitted for the catcher and/or pitcher of record with two outs (Rule 7.14).
  • District 8: Under a continuous batting order, the courtesy runner is the player who made the last out. During the regular season, any player wearing a catcher's mask (or a coach) may warm up a pitcher; in tournament play, it must be a mask-wearing player.

D12. Infield fly

  • Little League: In effect (Rule 2.00) — a fair fly ball catchable by an infielder with ordinary effort, with runners on first and second, or the bases loaded, and fewer than two out.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL: First offense: warning. Second offense: the player is removed from the game for safety — this is a safety removal, not a ruling that the batter is out; if the batter was already safe on the play, that result stands.

D14. Headfirst sliding

  • Little League: Headfirst slides are not permitted while advancing (Rule 7.08(a)(4)) — a runner may slide headfirst only when returning to a base already occupied. There's no rule requiring a runner to slide; a runner may also stand and try to avoid a tag, or go around a fielder, as long as they don't leave the baseline to do it.
  • NCLL: No headfirst slides (Rule 7.08(a)(4)) except when returning to a base.

D15. Malicious contact

  • Little League: A runner may not deliberately or maliciously crash into a fielder — most commonly the catcher at home plate — to jar the ball loose or cause injury; the runner must slide or attempt to avoid the fielder. Violation: the runner is called out, with ejection possible for egregious contact.
  • NCLL: Matches the rulebook — a runner may not simply run through a catcher standing in the base path.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • Little League: A continuous batting order is optional for Majors (Rule 4.04).
  • District 8: Continuous batting order is required for interleague play; a traditional 9-batter order is not allowed. Late arrivals go to the bottom of the order.
  • NCLL: All Majors teams use a continuous batting order (Rule 4.04), in-house and interleague alike — every rostered player present bats in order, whether or not they're in the field that inning. The batting order itself is fixed once set; fielding positions are freely substitutable (see Substitution, below).

E2. Substitution

  • District 8: Free defensive substitution is allowed.
  • NCLL: A player may move freely between defensive positions without affecting their spot in the batting order. Only pitching is restricted, by Little League's pitcher re-entry rules (Rule 3.03).

E3. Mandatory play

  • Little League: Every rostered player present at the start must play at least 6 defensive outs and bat at least once (Regulation IV(i)).
  • District 8: District 8 raises the mandatory-play floor to 9 defensive outs (not necessarily consecutive), recommended with at least 3 at an infield position.
  • NCLL: 9 defensive outs is the mandatory floor for every player present — in-house and interleague alike. A player who doesn't get their 9 outs starts the next game and completes the unmet requirement; manager penalties follow the same warning / one-game / season-suspension schedule as pitch-count violations (see Pitch counts, above).

E4. Minimum players to start

  • Little League: A game may not start with fewer than 8 players on a team (Rule 4.16).
  • District 8: A team may start and play with 8. The 8 bat; the 9th lineup slot is skipped with no penalty (Rule 6.05(n); Rule 4.04 NOTE 3). On defense, the opposing team lends a fielder — their last batting out — recommended at a corner outfield spot near their own dugout.
  • NCLL: Same mechanic for in-house and interleague Majors games.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • District 8: A player arriving after the game starts is added to the bottom of the batting order.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8: The home team uses the third-base dugout; the visiting team uses the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8: If a District 8 umpire is unavailable, the home team supplies the plate umpire and the visiting team supplies the field umpire.
  • NCLL: The league provides a plate umpire for regular-season games, adding a field umpire for playoffs. If no umpire is available at game time, the home team provides one — calling from behind the rubber if they lack gear. The visiting team may add a second field umpire with the home team's Chief Umpire's approval; the Chief Umpire has final say on all calls.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8: Each team provides a scorekeeper who is not a manager or coach. The home team's book is official; if the home team can't field a non-coach scorekeeper, the visiting team's book becomes official.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8: At all times, offense and defense, at least one background-checked adult must be in the dugout.

F5. Game balls

  • Little League: The home team furnishes the game balls to the umpire (Rule 1.09).
  • District 8: The home team gives the plate umpire at least 2 new game balls before the game.

F6. Score & pitch-count reporting

  • NCLL: Reporting stays team-to-team — see Pitch counts, above. There's no requirement to separately report scores or pitch counts up to the league.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • NCLL: Each team gets 15 minutes to warm up before first pitch (30 minutes total, pending field availability). The visiting team warms up first, then the home team, and the home team stays on the field to start the game. If less than 30 minutes is available before the scheduled start, the two teams agree on a mutual split. The field belongs to the umpire at the scheduled start time — a request to warm up past that point needs the umpire's approval. A team not ready at its scheduled slot forfeits that warm-up time.

G. Season structure

G1. Team formation

  • Little League: Regulation V governs the draft. Every league-age-12 candidate must be drafted to a Major Division team.
  • NCLL: A full redraft happens every year, run by the coaching staff as a group. Draft order is drawn from a hat, in two linked stages — returning Majors players and new 12-year-olds first, then under-12 players new to Majors. A coach's own child (or a confirmed assistant coach's child) is auto-slotted by their assessment rating, skipping the coach's pick that round. A short post-draft trade window is allowed, for roster fit, not talent acquisition.

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL: 2026 date: Saturday, January 24, at Robert Eagle Staff Middle School. Players are evaluated on throwing/receiving, infield/outfield reads, hitting, and baserunning (pitching/catching may be a separate station); bib numbers keep scoring blind to name. Every player makes a team. There's no official make-up date — a late or no-show family should email baseball@ncllball.com.

G3. Playing up

  • NCLL: A 9-year-old can earn a Majors or AAA roster spot based on assessment score and remaining roster space after 10s and 11s are placed. Placements are final; serious concerns can be emailed to baseball@ncllball.com.

G4. Roster size

  • Little League: 10–15 players per team (Regulation III).
  • NCLL: Target is 11–13 players, within that range.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL: 3–4 events (practices and games combined) per week during the season, typically Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday. Team assignments land in mid-to-late February, practices start in early March, games start in late March, and the regular season ends in mid-June. Exact dates shift year to year with field availability.

G6. Interleague play

  • NCLL: Majors runs primarily an in-house regular season under NCLL's own rules, with interleague games against other District 8 leagues layered in. For those games, the two managers agree on ground rules for that game, which may mean reverting to standard Little League rules — NCLL's continuous-batting-order and free-substitution rules may not apply. NCLL plays an equal number of interleague games against every other District 8 Majors team; District 8 sets the week-by-week schedule.

G7. Player pool & call-ups

  • Little League: A called-up pool player must play at least 9 consecutive defensive outs and bat once (Regulation IV).
  • NCLL: NCLL runs a standing Majors Call-Up pool: a group of AAA players are pre-designated and dual-rostered on both a AAA team and a Majors team at draft time. They practice with both; when a Majors team needs a player, the league reviews GameChanger availability and calls players up from that pool. A Majors Call-Up player may not play down for a lower-division team — call-ups only go up from AAA to Majors. A call-up requires sign-off from both the player's AAA coach and the Majors team's coach.

G8. Standings

  • NCLL: Yes — all Majors games are scored via GameChanger, and wins/losses feed the division's standings.

G9. End-of-season tournament

  • NCLL: NCLL runs its own internal end-of-season tournament — the Majors Championship — rather than District 8's North-End Tournament of Champions (that tournament is only for the district's North-End group of leagues). Every team competes, double-elimination, seeded by random draw rather than regular-season standings. The same rules as the regular season apply. The tournament runs in the window between the end of the regular season and the start of All-Stars.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League: A player must have played in at least 8 regular-season games (as an eligible player under mandatory play) by the start of District tournament play, and must meet Little League's residency and school-attendance requirements, including a verified birth certificate. The tournament team may carry a maximum of 14 players, one manager, and up to two coaches; a minimum of 12 eligible players is required for certification. Team names may not be released before May 15.
  • NCLL: Players submit an official Player Application by the deadline — selection isn't automatic — and must be available for the full District Tournament. A tryout (encouraged, not mandatory) is held May 15 at Lower Woodland, in a format similar to the January assessments. A committee of NCLL coaches collectively selects each team's roster, weighing attitude, coachability, season and tryout performance, regular-season division, skills, positional need, and availability. Rosters are announced May 22. Team managers are recruited separately by the All-Star Coordinator and approved by a board committee.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • Little League: An umpire may warn, eject, and forfeit for unsportsmanlike conduct (Rules 4.06–4.08, 9.01(d)). An ejected manager, coach, or player is suspended for the team's next played game, on top of any further penalty the league imposes (Rule 4.07).
  • NCLL: NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct. Beyond the automatic next-game suspension, NCLL reserves the right to require Board review of any ejection and to impose an escalating penalty for a manager's or coach's second ejection in a season. Complaints about a manager or coach can be investigated by NCLL's Managers Committee and reported to the Board.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL: The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is. Before first pitch, coaches decide together (umpires may advise); after first pitch, the umpire crew chief has sole authority to continue or suspend for weather.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL: When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL: Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field (arrange a separate time and place instead), respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol. A parent who violates this may be asked to leave and can be suspended from the next game; repeat violations draw a longer suspension.
