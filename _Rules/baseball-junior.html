Junior division baseball rules

As a Double Goal League, NCLL balances two goals in everything below: player development and competitive success. Juniors moves to a full 90-foot diamond — the same dimensions as high school baseball — and several rules that stay off at Majors and AAA switch on here for the first time: real leadoffs, balks, and an on-deck circle. Juniors follows the Little League "green book" rules; what follows is where District 8 or NCLL adds to or clarifies those rules.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's Interleague Supplemental Rules for Junior Baseball, the version currently in effect for our interleague games. A separate District 7/District 8 Teen Divisions agreement governs games that cross between the two districts, and overrides a handful of the D8-only rules below — those are called out explicitly where they apply.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League A local league establishes the age structure for this division (Regulation V).
  • District 8 League ages 12, 13, and 14 are eligible; league age 15 may participate with District Administrator approval.
  • NCLL 13s and 14s are the core. 12s can join Juniors, but it's not advised, since they'd forgo a year of Majors eligibility. No NCLL practice is documented for accepting a 15-year-old under the D8/DA-approval option.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League Junior League is Little League's full-size-diamond division — 90-foot bases, a 60-foot-6-inch mound, matching the standard baseball field every level above it uses. Balks, full leadoffs, and an on-deck circle are all in effect, same as high school ball.
  • NCLL The Junior Division is built for league-age 13-14 players, mirroring prep baseball dimensions and pace — the division where NCLL players transition to true high-school-scale baseball before Seniors, elevating varsity-level mechanics, strategy, and leadership while competing across District 8.

A3. Participation in two divisions

  • Little League A local league may allow a player to dual-roster onto a second division, provided the player is Major-Division-eligible or above.
  • NCLL No NCLL source documents dual-rostering into or out of Juniors. Since Juniors draws primarily 13s and 14s who've already aged out of Majors eligibility, the practical overlap with the Majors↔AAA Call-Up program is narrow.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 90 feet — the full-size diamond, matching high school dimensions (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance & mound

  • District 8 60 feet 6 inches — the full pitching distance.

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8 A Little League-approved Senior Division baseball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: bats meeting either the USABat standard or the BBCOR performance standard are permitted, up to 34 in. long (vs. 33 in. at Majors/AAA). Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not. Any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must be a single solid color and may not be white or gray.
  • Note A bat that's illegal at Majors or AAA — a plain USABat bat with no BBCOR certification — becomes legal here. Worth calling out explicitly the first year a player moves up.

B5. Home run line

  • District 8 On a non-fenced field, the home-run line is marked by cones 300 feet from home plate.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League Same NOCSAE standard as every division (Rule 1.16).

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League / District 8 Full kid pitch, the entire game — unchanged from Majors/AAA.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • Little League A pitcher's daily pitch-count maximum and required rest are set by their own league age (Regulation VI), not by their division — the same chart applies at every level. The league must designate an official pitch-count recorder, who must provide the current count to either manager on request and notify the umpire-in-chief when a pitcher reaches the max.
  • District 8 No player may throw more than 70 pitches in a game before May 1 (higher than Majors/AAA's 50); the Regulation VI chart applies from May 1 on. A catcher who catches more than 3 innings may not pitch that day; a catcher who catches even 1 pitch in the 4th inning may not pitch that day either.
  • NCLL Local recording workflow (who the recorder is, GameChanger vs. paper, retention) is NCLL's own to set. If the Junior team is co-leagued in a given year, the partner league would need to use a shared recording method too.

C4. Catcher / pitcher restriction

  • Little League / District 8 Same mechanic as every division — see Pitch counts, above.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: balks are in effect at Juniors. An illegal pitch with runners on base is a balk; runners advance one base.
  • Note Genuinely new territory for a player or coach moving up from Majors/AAA, since neither of those divisions has balks.

C6. Mound visits

  • Little League New for 2026: the number of remaining mound visits does not prevent a pitcher who was removed from the mound from returning as pitcher later in the same game.
  • Note A 2026-specific change worth flagging for coaches this season — under the old understanding, a pitcher removed from the mound was done pitching for the game.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • District 8 A complete game is seven innings (vs. six at Majors/AAA).

D2. Time limit

  • District 8 No new inning starts after 2 hours 15 minutes; no new batter after 2 hours 30 minutes. A game past that reverts to the last completed inning; ties are allowed. For games crossing between District 7 and District 8, a different standard applies instead: no new inning within 15 minutes of the field's posted reservation end time, or within 30 minutes of the next scheduled game's start time.
  • NCLL Which standard applies depends on whether the specific game is D8-only or crosses into District 7.

D3. Weekday curfew

  • District 8 Sunday–Thursday while school is in session, no new inning after 9:15 pm and no new batter after 9:30 pm — later than Majors/AAA's 8:30/8:45. The D7/D8 cross-district agreement uses the same curfew.

D4. Mercy rule

  • District 8 Shifted one inning later than Majors/AAA, since Juniors plays a 7-inning game: 15+ runs after 4 innings (3½ if home ahead), 10+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead), or 8+ after 6 (5½ if home ahead).

D5. Runs per inning

  • Little League / District 8 No cap — unlike AAA's 5-run half-inning limit, Juniors has no run cap, same as Majors.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League The standard dropped-third-strike rule is always in effect at Juniors, with no District 8 override — unlike AAA, where it's turned off.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: full leadoffs are allowed. A runner may leave their base at any time, not just once the pitch reaches the batter.

D8. Bunting

  • Little League Bunting is permitted, unchanged from Majors/AAA.

D9. Batter's-box rule

  • District 8 The standard batter's-box rules and penalty apply, identical to every other division.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: an on-deck batter is permitted, wearing a helmet.
  • District 8 No bats in the dugout; practice swings only in the on-deck circle or batter's box, under a coach's direction. The on-deck batter stands on the same side as the current batter.

D11. Courtesy runner

  • District 8 A courtesy runner (the player who made the last out) is permitted for the catcher and/or pitcher of record with two outs — identical to every other division.

D12. Infield fly

  • Little League The standard infield fly rule applies, not restated or modified by District 8 — same as Majors, not AAA's narrower local version.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • Note Not currently documented for Juniors specifically. Majors and AAA each have a warning-then-removal local rule for bat throwing — whether that extends to Juniors is a genuine open board decision, and since Juniors may be co-leagued in a given year, a co-league partner would need to agree to it too.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: headfirst sliding while advancing is permitted.

D15. Malicious contact

  • Little League A runner may not deliberately or maliciously crash into a fielder to jar the ball loose or cause injury; the runner must slide or attempt to avoid the fielder. Violation: the runner is called out, with ejection possible for egregious contact.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • District 8 Continuous batting order is required, same as every division — a traditional 9-batter order isn't allowed at Juniors (that option is Senior-only).

E2. Substitution

  • District 8 Free defensive substitution is allowed for the regular season, overriding Little League's traditional re-entry restrictions. For tournament play, those re-entry rules govern instead.

E3. Mandatory play

  • District 8 District 8 raises the mandatory-play floor to 9 defensive outs (not necessarily consecutive) — the same number as every division.

E4. Minimum players to start

  • District 8 A team may start and play with 8 present. The 8 bat; the 9th lineup slot is skipped with no penalty. On defense, the opposing team lends a fielder — their last batting out. For games crossing between District 7 and District 8, this differs: no borrowing a fielder from the opponent is allowed, and a team with only 8 present plays defense with 8.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • District 8 A player arriving after the game starts is added to the bottom of the batting order.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8 The home team uses the third-base dugout; the visiting team uses the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • District 8 If a District 8 umpire is unavailable, the home team supplies the plate umpire and the visiting team supplies the field umpire. For games crossing between District 7 and District 8, this differs: the home team provides both umpires.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8 Each team provides a scorekeeper who isn't a manager or coach; the home team's book is official. For games crossing between District 7 and District 8, the home team provides the official scorekeeper, with either team covering if the other can't.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8 At all times, offense and defense, at least one background-checked adult must be in the dugout.

F5. Game balls

  • Note District 8's Junior supplement states the ball type (see Ball standard, above) but doesn't say who provides them — genuinely NCLL's, or a co-league arrangement's, to set for the regular season. Little League Tournament Rules & Guidelines govern ball provision in a tournament context.

F6. Score & pitch-count reporting

  • Note No documented NCLL standard — a pure NCLL operations decision.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • Note No Junior-specific source found; presumably follows a similar warm-up window to Majors/AAA when field time allows, but Juniors' more limited 90-foot-field access may make this less consistent.

G. Season structure

G1. Team formation

  • Little League Regulation V governs the draft.
  • NCLL NCLL runs its own Junior Division draft — a snake draft, run so parity stays front and center. All athletes attend tryouts, which slot players so managers can balance pitching and key positions; every player who tries out lands on a roster.

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL Required for all Junior-division players, same as AAA and Majors. Distinct from the AAA/Majors 5-Tool Assessment event — Juniors' tryouts slot players for the draft rather than following that same format.

G3. Playing up

  • Little League A league-age-12 player may play Juniors instead of Majors, at NCLL's discretion.
  • NCLL A 12 can join Juniors, but NCLL advises against it, since it costs a year of Majors eligibility. No formal approval gate exists beyond the standard draft/tryout process.

G4. Roster size

  • Little League 10–15 players — the same range as every division — with a Junior-specific exception allowing up to 20 players if NCLL fields only one regular-season Junior team.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 2–3 events per week (fewer than AAA/Majors' 3–4), on 90-foot fields when available. Those fields have more limited availability than standard fields, so days of the week and start times vary more than other divisions.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL NCLL fields its own Junior team(s) via its own draft, playing a District 8 schedule against individual opponent towns — regular opponents include Ballard, Queen Anne, Magnolia, Richmond, Northwest, and Northeast Seattle. Regular season sets seeds for District and All-Star tournaments. Co-leaguing (combining rosters with a neighboring league) is a real, Little League-sanctioned option NCLL could use for the regular season too, but NCLL rarely does — it's used almost exclusively for All-Star team formation (see All-Star team, below), not the regular Junior season.

G7. Player pool & call-ups

  • Little League Mid-season replacement or movement of players is permitted under player-agent control at the rulebook level.
  • Note No Juniors-specific call-up or pool-player program is documented (unlike the Majors↔AAA Call-Up program). In a co-league year, this is something the co-league agreement with the partner league(s) would need to spell out explicitly.

G8. Standings

  • NCLL Standings and seeding are tracked — the regular season sets seeds for both District and All-Star tournaments.

G9. End-of-season tournament

  • District 8 An end-of-year, wood-bat "World Cup"-style tournament: teams split into pools, the top 2 per pool advance to single-elimination, with seeding set about one month before the tournament. The wood-bat requirement is specific to Juniors.
  • NCLL Regular-season standings feed District and All-Star tournament seeding. Beyond that, Juniors' post-season runs entirely through the District 8/All-Star track — there's no separate NCLL-internal Juniors championship the way Majors and AAA each run their own.

G10. All-Star team

  • Little League Same tournament-player eligibility baseline as every division — a player must have participated in at least 8 regular-season games by the start of Tournament Play, plus residency/school-attendance and birth-certificate verification. Roster maximum: 14 players.
  • NCLL League age 13–14 Baseball Juniors All-Star team, marked pending a coach and sufficient player interest — the same gating as Intermediate's All-Star team. District 8 Tournament begins June 29 at Shorewood HS; State advancement is listed TBD for 2026. If the Juniors team wins District and State, 2026 is a Regionals year for this division specifically — only the Washington State Majors (12's) and Juniors champions advance to Regionals, a distinction most other NCLL age divisions' 2026 champions don't share.
  • Note Co-leaguing means NCLL's Junior All-Star team may combine with one or two neighboring leagues to form a joint team when NCLL alone doesn't have enough players or a qualified coach to field a competitive independent team — up to three leagues may combine under Little League rules. A co-leagued player practices and competes under the joint banner, but the experience, commitment, and tournament path stay the same as an independent team. NCLL's co-league partners are always fellow District 8 City-group leagues (Ballard, Magnolia, Northwest Seattle, Queen Anne, RUG, Northeast Seattle, Shoreline — NCLL's own home field, Lower Woodland, sits in this group), never a suburban North-End league. The 2026 District 8 Junior baseball champion co-op was Queen Anne/RUG/Ballard — a real example of what these three-league co-ops look like, not necessarily NCLL's own specific partnership in a given year.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • Little League An umpire may warn, eject, and forfeit for unsportsmanlike conduct. An ejected manager, coach, or player is suspended for the team's next played game, on top of any further penalty the league imposes.
  • NCLL NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct. Beyond the automatic next-game suspension, NCLL reserves the right to require Board review of any ejection and to impose an escalating penalty for a manager's or coach's second ejection in a season.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
