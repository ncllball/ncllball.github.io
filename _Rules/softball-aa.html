AA division softball rules

AA is an early player-pitch transition division, with coach pitch available as relief when needed. Development stays the focus while player pitching, beginning steal concepts, and expanded defensive awareness get introduced.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Softball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's City Interleague Rules for softball.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League: A local league establishes the age structure for its divisions (Regulation V).
  • District 8: League ages 7, 8, and 9 are eligible for AA Softball (10 with District Administrator approval).
  • NCLL: League ages 7–9 is the registration target band, with 8-year-olds specifically directed to register here. A 10-year-old can join, but it isn't advised. A player with a fall or winter birthday may play down with their grade-level peers.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL: An early player-pitch transition division, with coach-pitch relief available as needed. Development stays the focus while player pitching, beginning steal concepts, and expanded defensive awareness get introduced.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League: 60-foot base paths, the same at every softball level (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance

  • Little League: 35 feet, the standard Minor Softball distance (Rule 1.07 NOTE 3–4).

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8: An 11-inch ball — a real step up from A Softball's 10-inch Level-1 safety ball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League: One softball bat standard applies at every level. Non-wood bats must carry the current softball certification mark; maximum 34 in. length and 2¼ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Pine tar or a similar adhesive is now permitted on the handle or grip at every level (not on the barrel or taper, and not covering the certification mark). Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not.
  • NCLL: A typical glove at this level is 10–12 inches — discourage an oversized trap-style pocket, especially on a pitcher's glove.

B6. Helmets & catcher's gear

  • Little League: Batting helmets must be NOCSAE-approved and league-provided; the batter and all base runners must wear one (Rule 1.16). Anyone catching — including during warm-ups — needs full catcher's gear (Rule 1.17). Little League has no rule requiring a defensive/fielding face mask at any other position — that's an NCLL/local choice, not a Little League mandate.
  • NCLL: Face masks are encouraged on all batting helmets. A defensive mask is recommended for infielders.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League: The full underhand-windmill delivery rules apply to the player-pitcher portion of the game specifically — underhand, hand below the hip, both feet in the pitching circle at the start, no crow-hop, leaping is legal, no more than two arm revolutions, one step forward at release (Rule 8.01).
  • NCLL: A real step up from A — games begin with player pitchers when possible. If three consecutive walks or hit-batters occur, a manager may optionally switch to coach pitch to restore pace. Coach pitch is delivered underhand, firm, to a belt-to-mid-thigh zone. There's no formal pitch count — managers watch for fatigue signs (control loss, wrist drag, an off-balance follow-through) instead. Using multiple pitchers is encouraged, rather than over-relying on one player. Spin pitches (drop, curve, rise) aren't taught at this stage.

C3. Pitch counts & required rest

  • NCLL: No formal pitch count — Little League Softball uses innings-pitched guidance rather than pitch counts, unlike baseball. Managers watch for fatigue directly, and multiple pitchers are encouraged early in the season so more players gain circle experience before playoffs.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League: Little League never calls a balk in softball, at any level (Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter.
  • NCLL: A standard 4-ball walk applies — a real change from A Softball, where there are no walks at all. A hit batter is awarded first base — instruct players to turn away rather than step into the pitch — another change from A, where a hit batter does not advance.

D. Game play

D2. Time limit

  • NCLL: A 90–105 minute target window, with no new inning starting roughly 10 minutes before the field's scheduled end.

D5. Runs per inning

  • NCLL: A 5-run cap per half-inning, uncapped once the game reaches its final inning — the same pattern baseball's AAA division uses.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League: Off at AA — the dropped-third-strike mechanic only applies at Majors and Juniors (Rule 6.09(b)); this is the Little League default at Minor Softball, not a local AA choice.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League: No leadoffs at any softball level. AA is Minor Softball, so a runner may not leave a base until the pitch is batted or reaches the batter (Rule 7.13) — the looser "on release" timing some leagues use is a Little League option written only for the Major Division, with no equivalent at Minor.
  • District 8: Goes stricter still — a runner may not leave a base at all until the ball crosses the plate.
  • NCLL: Early in the season, stealing is limited to 2nd and 3rd base, only once the ball has crossed the plate. Later in the season and in interleague play, whether stealing home is allowed varies by opponent — confirm at the plate meeting. Delayed steals during the windup aren't permitted.

D7a. Look-back rule

  • Little League: A runner off base after a pitch, with the pitcher holding the ball in the pitching circle and not making a play, must immediately commit to advancing or returning. Violation: the ball is dead and the runner is out (Rule 7.13 NOTE 1–2 + 7.08(a)(5)).

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League: No on-deck position at Minor Softball (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1).

D12. Infield fly

  • District 8: Off at AA (and A) — District 8 turns off the standard Little League infield fly rule at these two lower divisions.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL: A team warning on the first throw; a repeat means the batter is called out. A different penalty structure than A Softball's half-inning benching.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League: A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out at Minor Softball (Rule 7.08(a)(4)).
  • NCLL: Feet-first on advances; headfirst is only for returning to a base already occupied, matching the rulebook exactly.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • District 8: A continuous batting order.
  • NCLL: A late-arriving player is added to the bottom of the order.

E2. Substitution

  • District 8: Free substitution on defense.

E3. Mandatory play

  • NCLL: Every player gets at least one infield inning when feasible.

E4. Number of fielders

  • NCLL: A standard 9 defensive players, with an optional 10th (a 4th outfielder) in intra-league play to give more players extra reps.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8: The home team uses the third-base dugout, the visiting team the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • NCLL: There's no STACK Officials requirement at AA — that starts at AAA.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8: The scorekeeper is a home-team official, not a manager or coach.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8: A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.

G. Season structure

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL: Not required at AA — the assessment process is for players entering AAA and Majors.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL: 2–3 events per week, Wednesdays and Saturdays. Team formation lands in early March, practice starts mid-March, games start mid-April, and the season ends in mid-June.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • District 8: A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.
  • NCLL: NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL: The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL: When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL: Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
