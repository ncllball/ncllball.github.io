AA division baseball rules

As a developmental league, our primary goal at the AA level is to prepare players to move up the Little League ladder and eventually play in the Majors. Throughout this page, a Rule is non-negotiable — it comes from either the official Little League rulebook or NCLL board-approved policy — while a Guideline is a nuance within the game that managers and the league have agreed to.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"). AA has no District 8 content on this page — District 8 interleague play doesn't start until AAA.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League Any player who turns 7 before September 1, 2026 and does not turn 13 before August 31, 2026 is eligible for Minor League (Regulation IV(a)) — subject to the local league's own alignment of that division; NCLL's A/AA split is exactly that alignment.
  • NCLL League ages 8–9 is the target band — sometimes called the "8/9ers" division — with skilled 7-year-olds occasionally qualifying.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League Minor League exists to prepare children for eventual selection to a Major Division team.
  • NCLL A development-focused division whose primary goal is preparing players to move up the ladder and eventually reach the Majors.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 60-foot base paths — the standard Minor League diamond shared with A and Tee Ball (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance & mound

  • Little League 46 feet — the Minor League mound distance shared by AA, A, and Tee Ball (Rule 1.07).
  • NCLL All pitchers — players and coaches alike — throw from the middle rubber at Lower Woodland, 46 feet from home plate.

B3. Ball standard

  • NCLL An official hardball — a real step up from A Division's RIF5 safety ball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League Non-wood bats must carry the USABat mark; BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited; maximum 33 in. length and 2⅝ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not. Any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must be a single solid color and may not be white or gray.
  • NCLL As a rule of thumb, look for a bat about 26–30 inches long and 15–20 oz. A batter should be able to hold it with only their top hand and move it across their body without dipping. Watch for oversized "cool-looking" bats that aren't actually sized for the player.

B5a. Gloves

  • NCLL Gloves should be 9–11 inches, with the player's name and phone number on them. Specialty gloves (first base, catcher) are usable but discouraged — usually too large for a player at this level.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League Same NOCSAE standard as every division (Rule 1.16).
  • NCLL Check helmets for cracks or missing pads. A player with lice concerns about shared helmets may bring their own, subject to the manager's final say on whether player-owned equipment meets safety standards.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League A local league may use adult pitch or machine pitch in Minor League games for players league ages 7–12, applied here as coach pitch (Regulation VIII(d)).
  • NCLL Coach pitch is primary at AA — a real step up from A's machine pitch. A team's own player pitchers throw first. Coach your pitchers with the mindset that you want the other team to hit the ball — success is getting through the game without excessive walks. If a team pitcher issues three consecutive walks or hit-batters in an inning, a coach pitcher finishes that inning (the player pitcher may return once they've completed their required two innings). If no team pitchers are available at all, a coach pitches the whole game to their own team. A coach who's pitching has two jobs: throw a hittable pitch, and help direct the player "pitcher," who still stands at the position even when a coach is actually throwing. Every pitcher stands from the same rubber (see Pitching distance, above); players pitch standing, coaches may pitch from a knee.

C3. Pitch counts

  • NCLL Managers are responsible for recording pitch counts — usually via the scorekeeper — for every pitcher on the team throughout the season, and must be ready to share that data with the Board on request.

C3a. Catcher gear & safety

  • NCLL A male catcher must wear a hard protective cup. A catcher's helmet must have a dangling throat protector ("dangler"). Catchers must use a specialized catcher's mitt — an infielder's mitt isn't allowed. Anytime a player is in the crouched catching position — including practices, warm-ups, and sidelines — they need at minimum a mask, chest guard, and cup. A catcher may not block access to the plate until the ball is actually in play to them.

C4. Catcher / pitcher restriction

  • NCLL A player who has caught 3 or more innings in a game may not pitch that day. A pitcher who throws 21 or more pitches may not catch that same game.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • NCLL Standard balls-and-strikes rules apply. Balks are not called at AA — that starts at Intermediate.

D. Game play

D2. Time limit

  • NCLL 90 minutes or 6 innings, whichever comes first. Standard 3-out innings, not Tee Ball's 6-batter cycle.

D4. Mercy rule

  • NCLL There is no mercy rule in AA — play to the time limit.

D5. Runs per inning

  • NCLL A 5-run cap per inning — once 5 runs score, the inning ends and the batting team returns to the field.

D6. Dropped third strike & foul tips

  • Little League The dropped-third-strike mechanic (batter becomes a runner on an uncaught third strike) is scoped to Majors, Intermediate, Junior, and Senior League only (Rule 6.09(b)) — Minor League, including AA, doesn't get it at all.
  • NCLL Matches the rulebook default exactly — a dropped third strike is simply an out, no throw to first needed. A foul tip doesn't count as a third strike and the batter stays alive, but a caught foul tip does count as a third strike.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • NCLL No leadoffs — a runner may not leave the bag until the ball reaches the batter. Stealing is season-split: no stealing at all in the first half of the season, so catchers and pitchers can develop without runner pressure. In the second half, stealing is allowed only in specific situations — on a passed ball or wild pitch, or on a dropped return throw to the pitcher (1st to 2nd, 2nd to 3rd). Stealing home is never permitted, for safety. A "slow down" convention also applies: a team leading by 5 or more runs should have its runners advance only on a batted ball, not opportunistically.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League No on-deck position below Majors (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1) — no on-deck circle or sideline warm-up, period.
  • NCLL Bats are only in a player's hands when they're actually up to bat; the on-deck batter stays in the dugout.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL First offense: warning. Second offense: the player is removed from the game for safety, at the umpire's discretion.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out (Rule 7.08(a)(4)) — this rule reaches every level below Majors, including AA.
  • NCLL No headfirst slides are allowed, matching the rulebook exactly. Practice proper sliding technique to reduce injuries, and as a general rule, always slide into home plate.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • NCLL All teams bat a straight lineup of every player, regardless of fielding status — no substitutions or pinch hitting. A player who's injured and can't bat is scratched from the lineup.

E3. Mandatory play

  • NCLL Managers are encouraged to strive for even playing time in the field during each game — with larger rosters, inning parity may need to be spread across a few games rather than guaranteed every single one. On the batting side, coaches aim to get each player at least two at-bats, though that's acknowledged as harder in pitching-duel or shorter 4–5-inning games. Players should get the chance to play every position over the course of the season, except catcher and pitcher.

E4. Flexible team size

  • NCLL A team may field a defense of 8, borrowing a 9th player from the opponent for defense only — the borrowed player still represents their own team on offense. By mutual manager agreement, a 10th player may be added as a 4th outfielder.

E5. Late-arriving players

  • NCLL A player arriving after the game starts is added to the end of the batting lineup, regardless of the team's current position in the order.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • NCLL Same as every NCLL division — the home team takes the third-base dugout, the visiting team the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • NCLL AA provides its own umpires — a real step up from A Division's single field umpire. The home team provides the Chief Umpire, positioned behind home plate; the visiting team provides the Field Umpire. If an umpire isn't available at game time, the home team provides one, calling from behind the rubber if lacking gear. The Chief Umpire has final say on all calls.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • NCLL Pending field availability, each team gets 15 minutes to warm up before first pitch; the visiting team warms up first, then the home team, staying on the field to start. If 30 minutes isn't available before the scheduled start, the two teams agree on a shorter split. The field belongs to the umpire at the scheduled start time — a late warm-up request needs the umpire's approval. A team not ready at its scheduled slot forfeits that warm-up.

G. Season structure

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 3 events per week, on Fridays and Saturdays. Team formation lands in early March, practice starts mid-March, games start mid-April, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL Entirely in-house — no interleague and no District 8 involvement at this level.

G8. Standings & post-season

  • NCLL AA is non-competitive, but game scores are kept — there are no playoffs or standings.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • NCLL Same league-wide requirement as every division — anyone participating with players on a regular basis must complete a background check and be cleared by NCLL. The same Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) standards apply league-wide.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
