Intermediate division baseball rules

Intermediate (50/70) is a real Little League division nationally and across District 8 — a bridge step between the small Majors/AAA diamond and the full-size Junior/Senior diamond. But NCLL doesn't run an in-house, regular-season Intermediate team: league-age 13-14 players who aren't in Majors go straight to NCLL's Juniors program instead, on the full 90-foot diamond. At NCLL, Intermediate exists only as an ad hoc District 8 All-Star tournament team, fielded when there's a coach and enough interested 11-13s — not guaranteed every year. This page covers what's confirmed about the division nationally and at District 8, and what actually applies if NCLL fields that team.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies, plus the 2026 Tournament Rules and Guidelines; District 8 refers to District 8's Interleague Supplemental Rules for Intermediate Baseball, the version currently in effect.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • District 8 League ages 11, 12, and 13 are eligible.
  • NCLL No in-house eligibility to set. 11s and 12s in this age band are covered by AAA/Majors' own age eligibility; 13s who aren't in Majors go to Juniors instead. The only NCLL-side use of this age band is All-Star tournament-team selection.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League Intermediate (50/70) is Little League's bridge division between the small Major/AAA diamond and the full-size Junior/Senior diamond — 70-foot bases, a 50-foot mound, with full leadoffs and balks now in effect and an on-deck circle permitted. It's meant to be a step, not a skip.
  • NCLL NCLL doesn't currently use this step — the league's regular-season track goes straight from Majors (60 ft) to Juniors (90 ft, its own independent team). Intermediate only shows up when NCLL has a coach and enough interested 11-13s to field a District 8 All-Star tournament team, which isn't guaranteed year to year.

B. Field & equipment

B1-B2. Base paths & pitching distance

  • Little League 70-foot base paths — the division's namesake "50/70."
  • District 8 A fixed 50-foot pitching distance, not portable-mound territory the way Majors/AAA are.

B3-B4. Ball & bat standard

  • District 8 A Little League-approved Senior Division baseball — not the standard smaller-diamond ball Majors/AAA use.
  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: bats meeting either the USABat standard or the BBCOR performance standard are permitted, up to 34 in. long (vs. 33 in. at Majors/AAA). Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not. Any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must be a single solid color and may not be white or gray.
  • Note Worth flagging to families/coaches if the All-Star team is fielded — a bat illegal at Majors/AAA becomes legal here, and the max length is an inch longer.

B5. Home run line

  • District 8 On a non-fenced field, the home-run line is marked by cones 250 feet from home plate.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League / District 8 Full kid pitch, the entire game — unchanged from Majors/AAA.

C3-C4. Pitch counts, rest & catcher restriction

  • District 8 No player may throw more than 70 pitches in a game before May 1 (vs. 50 for Majors/AAA); the Regulation VI daily-max chart by league age applies from May 1 on. A catcher who catches more than 3 innings may not pitch that day; a catcher who catches even 1 pitch in the 4th inning may not pitch that day either.

C5. Walks, HBP & balks

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: balks are in effect at Intermediate — this is the tier where the rulebook's Major/Minor "no balk" exception ends. An illegal pitch with runners on base is a balk; runners advance one base.
  • Note One of the most important coach-facing flags if the All-Star team is ever fielded — coaches and parents used to Majors/AAA's "no balk" rule need to be told this explicitly changes here.

C6. Mound visits

  • Little League New for 2026: the number of remaining mound visits does not prevent a pitcher who was removed from the mound from returning as pitcher later in the same game.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • District 8 A complete game is seven innings (vs. six at Majors/AAA).

D2-D3. Time limit & weekday curfew

  • District 8 No new inning starts after 2 hours 15 minutes; no new batter after 2 hours 30 minutes. Sunday–Thursday while school is in session, no new inning after 9:15 pm and no new batter after 9:30 pm — later than Majors/AAA's 8:30/8:45. For games crossing between District 7 and District 8, a different time standard applies instead: no new inning within 15 minutes of the field's posted reservation end time, or within 30 minutes of the next scheduled game's start time.

D4. Mercy rule

  • District 8 Shifted one inning later than Majors/AAA, since Intermediate plays a 7-inning game: 15+ runs after 4 innings (3½ if home ahead), 10+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead), or 8+ after 6 (5½ if home ahead).

D5. Runs per inning

  • Little League / District 8 No cap — same as Majors, unlike AAA's 5-run half-inning limit.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League The standard dropped-third-strike rule is always in effect, with no District 8 override — unlike AAA, where it's turned off.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: full leadoffs are allowed. A runner may leave their base at any time.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League A genuine rules flip from Majors/AAA: an on-deck batter is permitted, wearing a helmet — the biggest single mechanical adjustment for any NCLL player or coach moving up to this tier.
  • District 8 No bats in the dugout; practice swings only in the on-deck circle or batter's box, under a coach's direction.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League Headfirst sliding while advancing is permitted — the ban that applies at Majors and below lifts starting at this tier.

E. Roster & lineup

E1-E2. Batting order & substitution

  • District 8 Continuous batting order and free substitution, same as Majors/AAA.

E3. Mandatory play

  • District 8 9 defensive outs (not necessarily consecutive) — same number as Majors/AAA.

E4. Minimum players to start

  • District 8 A team may start and play with 8 present, same as every division. For games crossing between District 7 and District 8, this differs: no borrowing a fielder from the opponent is allowed, and a team with only 8 present plays defense with 8.

G. Season structure

G9-G10. All-Star / tournament team — this division's entire reason for existing at NCLL

  • District 8 An end-of-year "World Cup"-style tournament: teams split into pools, the top 2 per pool advance to single-elimination, with seeding set about one month before the tournament.
  • Little League The standard tournament-player eligibility baseline applies — a player must have participated in at least 8 regular-season games by the start of Tournament Play, plus residency/school-attendance and birth-certificate verification, roster maximum 14. Those 8 games don't need to be in the tournament team's own division — a player's regular-season AAA or Majors games count toward the threshold.
  • NCLL If fielded: the District 8 Tournament begins June 23 at Northshore AF. A District-winning team advances to the Washington State Tournament (2026: Pasco, starting July 4). Fielding the team needs both a coach and at least 12 interested players — not guaranteed every year, the same gating pattern as Baseball Juniors' All-Star team. Selection follows the same coaches'-committee process used league-wide.

H. Conduct & safety

H1-H4. League-wide policies

  • NCLL These apply whenever NCLL people — coaches, players, parents — are involved, regardless of whether Intermediate has an in-house season. Same league-wide standards as every other NCLL team: background checks and Positive Coaching Alliance training for volunteers, the 30/30 weather policy, the concussion and injury protocol, and the Sport Parent Code of Conduct for spectators.

What NCLL still needs to settle

  • Whether NCLL wants to revisit running a real in-house Intermediate division at all — not a documentation gap, but a genuine strategic question about facility and coach availability, and whether skipping straight to Juniors' 90-foot diamond serves 13-year-olds as well as a 50/70 season would.
