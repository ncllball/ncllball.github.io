A division softball rules

A Softball welcomes league-age 6–7 players into a coach-pitch season built around confidence and fundamentals first. Each softball division matches a developmental stage, a skill focus, and a game format — A is the entry point.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Softball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"); District 8 refers to District 8's City Interleague Rules for softball. A Division plays entirely in-house, so District 8 content on this page is limited to a few shared equipment and eligibility facts.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League: A local league establishes the age structure for its divisions (Regulation V).
  • District 8: League ages 6, 7, and 8 are eligible for A Softball.
  • NCLL: League ages 6–7 is the registration target band. A player with a fall or winter birthday may play down with their grade-level peers.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL: A coach-pitch entry point focused on confidence and fundamentals — every rep is meant to build comfort with the game before competition becomes the focus.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League: 60-foot base paths — softball uses the same 60 feet at every level, unlike baseball, which grows the diamond as players move up (Rule 1.04).

B2. Pitching distance

  • Little League: 35 feet, the Minor Softball distance shared by A, AA, and AAA (Rule 1.07 NOTE 3–4).
  • NCLL: A machine or coach is positioned approximately 35 feet away, simulating the player-pitch distance players will see later.

B3. Ball standard

  • District 8: A 10-inch Level-1 safety ball — a step below AA and AAA's 11-inch ball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League: One softball bat standard applies at every level, unlike baseball's USABat/BBCOR split by division. Non-wood bats must carry the current softball certification mark; maximum 34 in. length and 2¼ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Pine tar or a similar adhesive is now permitted on the handle or grip at every level (not on the barrel or taper, and not covering the certification mark). Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not.

B6. Helmets & catcher's gear

  • Little League: Batting helmets must be NOCSAE-approved; the batter and all base runners must wear one (Rule 1.16). Anyone catching — including during warm-ups — needs a mask with a throat protector, a chest protector, shin guards, and (for a male catcher) a cup (Rule 1.17). Little League has no rule requiring a defensive/fielding face mask at any other position — Rule 1.14 only requires a glove; any infield or pitcher mask policy is an NCLL/local choice, not a Little League mandate.
  • NCLL: Face masks on batting helmets are strongly encouraged early on, to help normalize the habit. Infield defensive masks are optional but supported, especially at first base and pitcher. A typical glove for this level is 9–11 inches — discourage oversized "trap"-style gloves.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • Little League: The same underhand windmill motion rule that applies at every softball level — no more than two arm revolutions, delivered from within the 8-foot pitching circle (Rule 8.01). Little League never calls a balk in softball, at any level (Rule 8.05) — an illegal pitch is simply a ball on the batter.
  • NCLL: Coach pitch (or machine) only — there's no player pitcher at this level. The offensive team's coach or an approved volunteer operates the machine, if one is used, or delivers soft pitches — not aiming for strikeouts. A coach may pitch from within a foot of the circle rather than a fixed spot, and may wear a glove; a batted ball that hits the coach pitcher is incidental and stays live. The player standing at the pitcher position stays fully inside the circle until the ball is in play.

C2. At-bat structure

  • NCLL: A batter gets 7 live pitches, then is out. A foul ball on the 7th pitch (or later) buys another pitch, unless it's caught for an out — there's no tee fallback after the 7th pitch. There are no walks, and a coach pitch that hits the batter doesn't send them to first.

D. Game play

D1. Length of game

  • NCLL: Games target 3–4 innings inside the time window, rather than a fixed inning count.

D2. Time limit

  • NCLL: Typically 75–80 minutes.

D5. Runs per inning

  • NCLL: A 5-run cap, or the full lineup batting around — whichever comes first — as a guideline the two managers agree on before the game, softer than the hard caps used at AA, AAA, and Majors.

D6. Dropped third strike

  • Little League: The dropped-third-strike mechanic only applies at Majors and Juniors (Rule 6.09(b)) — Minor Softball, which A sits within, doesn't get it.
  • NCLL: Not a meaningful concept in practice at A — a batter is out after 7 pitches, not on a called or swinging third strike against a live catcher.

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • Little League: No leadoffs at any softball level. At Minor Softball, a runner may not leave a base until the pitch is batted or reaches the batter (Rule 7.13).
  • NCLL: No stealing or leading off — a developmental-focus choice that matches the Little League default exactly.

D7a. Look-back rule

  • Little League: A runner off base after a pitch, with the pitcher holding the ball in the pitching circle and not making a play, must immediately commit to advancing or returning — no stopping, no standing off the base. Violation: the ball is dead and the runner is out (Rule 7.13 NOTE 1–2 + 7.08(a)(5)). In practice this comes up less at A, since a runner generally can't leave a base at all until the ball is batted or reaches the batter (see Stealing & leadoffs, above).

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League: No on-deck position at Minor Softball or Majors — that starts at Juniors (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1).

D12. Infield fly

  • District 8: Off at A (and AA) — District 8 turns off the standard Little League infield fly rule at these two lower divisions.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL: One warning; a repeat means sitting out for the rest of that half-inning. This is a different, lighter penalty structure than baseball's warning-then-removal-from-the-game mechanic.

D14. Sliding

  • Little League: A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out at every level through Majors (Juniors is the only level where it's permitted) — sliding headfirst back into a base to return is always legal, and there's no rule requiring a runner to slide at all (Rule 7.08(a)(4)).

D16. Advancing on overthrows

  • NCLL: A runner advances one base on most overthrows, to keep the pace of play and the teaching rhythm going. Force-play awareness and a "freeze on a line drive" concept are introduced later in the season.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • District 8: A continuous batting order — the entire roster bats, regardless of fielding position.

E2. Substitution

  • District 8: Free substitution on defense.

E3. Mandatory play

  • NCLL: Rotate players through positions, with no position specialization at this level. Every player bats through the continuous batting order regardless of where — or whether — they're in the field.

E4. Number of fielders

  • NCLL: A team may field up to 10 defensive players, with a 4th outfielder.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • District 8: The home team uses the third-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • NCLL: The home team supplies the plate umpire, the visiting team supplies the field umpire. There's no STACK Officials requirement at A — that starts at AAA.

F3. Scorekeeping

  • District 8: The scorekeeper is a home-team official, not a manager or coach.
  • NCLL: No official score is kept at A — the focus is on celebrating development moments, not the outcome.

F4. Adult supervision

  • District 8: A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.

G. Season structure

G2. Assessments & tryouts

  • NCLL: Not required at A — the assessment process is for players entering AAA and Majors.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL: 2 events per week, Tuesdays and Saturdays. Team formation lands in early March, practice starts mid-March, games start mid-April, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL: Entirely in-house — there's no District 8 interleague play for A Softball. The emphasis is purely developmental, inside the league.

G8. Standings

  • NCLL: None, and no score is kept at all — the most non-competitive framing of any softball division.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • District 8: A background check and Abuse Awareness Training are required for every volunteer.
  • NCLL: NCLL is a Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) league — all managers attend a PCA seminar and are expected to uphold NCLL's and PCA's rules of conduct.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL: The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL: When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL: Parents are encouraged to support their team with positive reinforcement rather than outcome commentary, and should stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
