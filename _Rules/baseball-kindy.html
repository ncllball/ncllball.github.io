Kindy division baseball rules

Kindy (Kindergarten Coach Pitch, or "KCP") is a division we've piloted for a couple of seasons now. It gives league-age-6 kids a chance to play a slightly more challenging game — a moving ball and real outs — while staying adaptive for a range of skill levels. If you're considering Kindy for your player, think about whether they have the coordination, the attention span for hit balls, and the emotional maturity to handle outs.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"). Kindy isn't a chartered Little League division — Tee Ball is the youngest one Little League charters — and it has no District 8 content: District 8 interleague play doesn't start until AAA.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • NCLL: League age 6 is the standard entry point, using Little League's own age-eligibility framework even though Kindy itself isn't chartered. Kindy is a step above Tee Ball, not a replacement for it or a lead-in to it. A league-age-7 player defaults to Kindy unless clearly ready for Baseball A. A league-age-5 player's default division is Tee Ball; Kindy is only available to a 5-year-old who meets the readiness criteria under Team formation, below.

A2. What this division is

  • NCLL: A coordination/readiness bridge between Tee Ball and A Division — a slightly more challenging game with a moving ball and actual outs, adaptive for kids at varying skill levels.

B. Field & equipment

B3. Ball standard

  • NCLL: Coach pitch with a safety ball, with occasional tee use.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League: Non-wood bats must carry the USABat mark; BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited; maximum 33 in. length and 2⅝ in. barrel (Rule 1.10) — the same standard as every other division once a player is swinging a real bat.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League: Same NOCSAE standard as every division, once a helmet is worn (Rule 1.16).

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching format

  • NCLL: Coach pitch, with occasional tee use as a fallback — this is Kindy's defining mechanic, distinguishing it from both Tee Ball (tee only) and A/AA (pitching machine and/or full coach pitch at a fixed distance). Rules adapt through the season to keep play challenging but appropriate.

G. Season structure

G1. Team formation

  • NCLL: Teams are formed by coach/teammate requests, sibling placement, and school affiliation — a placement model, not a draft or assessment. Kindy is offered to every league-age-6 player as the standard placement.
  • NCLL — standard placement (league age 6):
    • League age 6, one year of Tee Ball: automatic Kindy placement, no further questions.
    • League age 6, coach vouch: in.
    • League age 7: defaults to Kindy unless clearly ready for Baseball A.
  • NCLL — the "2-of-3" framework (the only path in for a league-age-5 player): Tee Ball is the default division for a league-age-5 player; this framework is the only way into Kindy at that age. A player needs two of these three: maturity (can stay focused, handle outs without melting down), love of baseball (genuinely wants to be there), and athleticism (coordination, can engage with a pitched ball).
    • One year of Tee Ball — automatic 2 of 3, regardless of age.
    • Coach vouch — counts as 2 of 3 on its own.
    • Parent will assist the coach — covers maturity, since the parent chases their kid so the head coach doesn't have to.
    • Parent leads with "they love baseball" and will assist — love of baseball plus maturity is a pass.
    • Athleticism is taken on the parent's word rather than independently assessed — at this age, parents are considered unusually accurate about their kids' physical ability.

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL: Two practices a week during the pre-season; once the season begins, one weeknight practice and one Saturday game per week — a lighter cadence than every other division.

G5a. Location

  • NCLL: Upper Ross Park or Upper Woodland.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL: Entirely in-house — no interleague and no District 8 involvement at this level.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • NCLL: Same league-wide requirement as every division — anyone participating with players on a regular basis must complete a background check and be cleared by NCLL. The same Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) standards apply league-wide.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL: The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL: When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL: Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
