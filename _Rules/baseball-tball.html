Tee Ball division baseball rules

This page covers guidelines for conducting games at the Tee Ball level of play. As a developmental league, our primary goal is to prepare players to progress through the Little League system and eventually reach the Majors — Tee Ball is intentionally non-competitive, with no official scorekeeping.

Little League refers to the 2026 Little League Baseball Official Regulations, Playing Rules & Operating Policies (the "green book"). Tee Ball has no District 8 content on this page — District 8 interleague play doesn't start until AAA.

A. Division identity

A1. Age eligibility

  • Little League Nationally, Tee Ball is players league age 4–7; a local league establishes its own age structure within that band, and players league age 4 are eligible only for Tee Ball unless the Charter Committee approves otherwise (Regulation V).
  • NCLL NCLL's local minimum is league age 5 — stricter than the national floor of 4. A returning 6-year-old with a year of Tee Ball experience should consider moving up to Kindy (KCP) or Baseball A rather than repeating Tee Ball.

A2. What this division is

  • Little League Tee Ball's entire design brief is instructional flexibility — the rules of the game may be varied to accommodate the need for teaching.
  • NCLL A developmental division meant to prepare players to progress through the Little League system, framed explicitly as non-competitive rather than results-focused.

B. Field & equipment

B1. Base paths

  • Little League 60-foot base paths — the same Minor League diamond A, AA, and Tee Ball all share (Rule 1.04).

B3. Ball standard

  • NCLL The "Safe-T-Ball," a safety ball engineered specifically for Tee Ball and provided by the league — not a standard hardball.

B4. Bat standard

  • Little League Non-wood bats must carry the USABat mark; BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited; maximum 33 in. length and 2⅝ in. barrel (Rule 1.10). Tee Ball bats specifically carry a "ONLY FOR USE WITH APPROVED TEE BALLS" mark. Pine tar and similar adhesives are now permitted on the handle or grip; thumb protectors are permitted, choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not.
  • NCLL Look for a bat about 24–25 inches long and 11–16 oz. A batter should be able to hold it with only their top hand and move it slowly across their body on a level plane without dipping. Watch for oversized "cool-looking" bats that aren't actually sized for the player.

B5a. Gloves

  • NCLL Gloves should be 9–11 inches, with the player's name and phone number on them. Specialty gloves (first base, catcher) are usable but discouraged — usually too large for a Tee Ball player.

B6. Helmets

  • Little League Same NOCSAE standard as every division (Rule 1.16).
  • NCLL Inspect helmets for cracks or missing pads. A player with lice concerns about shared helmets may bring their own, subject to the manager's final say on whether player-owned equipment meets safety standards.

C. Pitching

C1. Pitching

  • Little League There's no pitching at Tee Ball. Strikeouts are locally optional under Tee Ball's "rules may be varied for teaching" authority.
  • NCLL No pitching, no walks, and no strikeouts — a batter keeps swinging until the ball is put in play. There's no catcher restriction to speak of, though catching gear is provided and either a player or the offensive team's own coach (the one setting the ball on the tee) may serve as a de facto catcher.

D. Game play

D1. Length of a half-inning

  • Little League Little League's own Tee Ball default ends a half-inning at 3 outs or when the entire lineup has batted, whichever comes first.
  • NCLL NCLL varies this default — see Batting order, below. A fixed 6-batter cycle replaces both halves of the Little League default; neither "3 outs" nor "full lineup" governs when a half-inning ends here.

D2. Time limit

  • NCLL 60 minutes or 3 innings, whichever comes first. Teams agree on a mutual warm-up length; the visiting team warms up first, the home team second (staying on the field to start the game); managers can waive warm-up time entirely for more game time.

D4. Run rule

  • NCLL There's no inning run-rule limit — every player who bats scores. Half-innings are capped structurally instead, by the 6-batter cycle (see Batting order, below).

D7. Stealing & leadoffs

  • NCLL There's no stealing or leading off in Tee Ball.

D10. On-deck & bat safety

  • Little League No on-deck position below Majors (Rule 1.08 NOTE 1) — there's no warm-up circle, so the next batter waits in the dugout.
  • NCLL Bats stay on the fence outside the dugout — no bats in a player's hands unless they're up to bat, no exceptions.

D12. Outs

  • NCLL No outs are recorded at Tee Ball at all. A hitter or base runner who's "put out" by the defense stays on base — the fielder making the play should be acknowledged for good defense, but nobody is removed from play.

D13. Throwing of bats

  • NCLL First offense: warning. Second offense: the player is removed from the game for safety, at the managers' discretion.

D16. Advancing on a hit or error

  • NCLL First half of the season, a hitter advances to first base only, regardless of how well the ball is hit. Second half of the season, by mutual agreement between managers, a standout hit to the outfield may be allowed an extra base. Errors aren't recognized at all — there's no advancing on an error.

E. Roster & lineup

E1. Batting order

  • NCLL Teams bat six players per half-inning rather than following a fixed roster order or a 3-out structure. The sixth batter "clears the bases" by running from home plate all the way back to home plate; fielders wait until the last base runner crosses home before clearing the field.

F. Game operations

F1. Dugout assignments

  • NCLL Same as every NCLL division — the home team takes the third-base dugout, the visiting team the first-base dugout.

F2. Umpiring

  • NCLL There are no umpires in Tee Ball.

F7. Pre-game warm-ups

  • NCLL Teams agree on a mutual warm-up length before the game; the visiting team warms up first, the home team second (staying on the field to start play); managers may waive warm-up entirely for more game time. After the game, clear the dugout promptly and hold any team meeting off the field, away from the dugout, out of consideration for the next teams.

G. Season structure

G5. Practices & start dates

  • NCLL 2 events per week. Team formation lands in mid-March, practice starts in late March, games start mid-to-late April, and the season ends in mid-June.

G6. Schedule type

  • NCLL Entirely in-house — no interleague and no District 8 involvement at this level.

G8. Standings

  • NCLL None. Tee Ball is a non-competitive division with no official scorekeeping.

H. Conduct & safety

H1. Manager & coach conduct

  • NCLL Same league-wide requirement as every division — anyone participating with players on a regular basis must complete a background check and be cleared by NCLL. The same Positive Coaching Alliance (PCA) standards apply league-wide.

H2. Weather & lightning

  • NCLL The 30/30 rule is in effect: everyone leaves the field at the first thunder or within 30 seconds of a lightning flash, and play doesn't resume until 30 minutes after the last strike or thunder. Dugouts, pavilions, bleachers, and sheds are not safe shelter — a sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with the windows up is.

H3. Injury & concussion

  • NCLL When in doubt, sit them out. A player suspected of a concussion is removed immediately — no same-day return — and must be evaluated and cleared in writing by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management before returning. Report an injury to the Safety Officer by the end of the game or practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours. Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.

H4. Spectators

  • NCLL Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands and stay out of the dugout during play. Per the Sport Parent Code of Conduct signed at registration: don't confront coaches at the game field, respect officials' authority, and keep the sports environment free of drugs, tobacco, and alcohol.
  • Note Tee Ball leans hard into a positive-coaching spirit — find the good in every play (a wild throw is still a "strong throw"), and remember every swing is a good attempt at this age.
