## A. Division identity

### A1. Age eligibility (league age)

- **LL Rulebook:** Reg I(a)(1): "The Little League (Major) Division is to accommodate participants league ages 9–12." A local league MAY choose to limit Majors to 10–12 or to 11–12 (intro to the Regulations). Reg IV(a) NOTE / draft rules: "Any player who is league age 12 must be permitted to play in the Major Division. The local league cannot force any player [to move up]." And: "All candidates who are league age twelve (12) must be drafted to a Major Division team" (or Intermediate (50-70)/Junior/Senior). 9s and 10s MAY be in Majors but are not required to be.
- **D8 Interleague:** "All players league age 12, 11, 10 and 9 are eligible for the Majors division."
- **NCLL:** 10s, 11s, and 12s fill AAA/Majors roster spots first. Skilled 9s may earn a spot in AAA or Majors if roster space remains after 10s/11s are placed and their assessment score puts them in the draftable pool — default is AAA, with Majors possible via the same draft/assessment process (see G1/G3). (12s: no decision — they must be drafted to Majors. 11s: effectively must, unless NCLL runs an Intermediate/50-70 division — see A3/G10.)
- **Notes:** Cite: _Events/assessments-faq.html ("10's, 11's and 12's fill AAA/Majors first... skilled 9's can earn spots if roster space remains and their assessment score places them in the draftable pool"). No hard numeric cutoff (e.g. "top N nine-year-olds") is published — placement is assessment-driven, not a fixed quota.

### A2. Purpose / what this division develops

- **LL Rulebook:** Reg I framing — Majors is the full-rules player-pitch division; the Minor League exists "to prepare children for eventual selection to a Major Division team."
- **NCLL:** Majors is NCLL's full-rules, full-kid-pitch competitive division for league ages 9–12 — 60-foot bases, 46-foot mound, standard LL rules with no training wheels (see B/C/D sections). Teams are formed by a full annual draft (G1) and play a mix of in-house games and D8 interleague games (G6), with the regular season culminating in NCLL's own double-elimination "Majors Championship" tournament (G9). It's the top rung of NCLL's small-diamond baseball before players age into the 50/70 (Intermediate) and full-size-diamond (Junior/Senior) divisions, and the springboard into District 8 All-Star tournament play for players who try out and are selected (G10).
- **Notes:** Written 2026-09-02 from facts already established elsewhere in this doc (G1, G6, G9, G10) rather than a single repo/site source — no new citation beyond what those rows already cite.

### A3. Participation in two divisions

- **LL Rulebook:** Reg I NOTE 2: "Major/Intermediate (50-70) Division/Junior/Senior League: A local league may allow players to participate in two divisions." (Tee Ball/Minor players may not.) This is the general "dual roster" mechanism (distinct from Reg V(c) pool play — see Notes), grounded in Regulation I and IV per LL's own guidance ("Little Leaguers Can 'Dual Roster,' Play on Multiple Little League Teams in the Same Season," littleleague.org/university). Key conditions: it's a **local-league option**, not automatic — the league must affirmatively allow it. A player must be age-eligible for the Major Division or above to dual-roster (so a Majors-eligible 12 can also play Intermediate/Junior; an AAA-only 9–10 generally cannot dual-roster upward). Residency and school-enrollment eligibility apply to both teams. A player may be on only **one tournament team**. Waiver requests go to the District Administrator.
- **NCLL:** NCLL runs a formal "Majors Call-Up" program: a set of AAA players (15 in the cited example) are simultaneously rostered on both a AAA and a Majors team. They practice with both, and are called up to play Majors games as needed based on GameChanger availability. This matches the LL "dual roster" mechanism above, not Reg V(c) pool play (see Notes) — but whether the board has ever formally adopted "dual rostering" as NCLL's chosen local option (vs. it just having grown into practice) isn't documented anywhere found.
- **Notes:** Cite: Communications/majorcallups.html. This is AAA-players-up-to-Majors, not a Majors player rostering down — but it confirms NCLL exercises the Reg I NOTE 2 "two divisions" option. See G7 (player pool/call-ups), which this same program answers. **Resolved 2026-09-03 (tower session):** the earlier flag questioning whether this matches Reg V(c) pool play was answered — it doesn't, and it isn't supposed to. Dual-rostering (Reg I/IV, the mechanism above) and pool play (Reg V(c), where a player stays on one roster and the player agent makes game-by-game call-ups) are two separate, real LL mechanisms; NCLL's program is genuinely dual-rostering, not a misapplication of pool play. » NCLL DECISION: confirm/pull NCLL's actual board-level position on dual-rostering from Outline or past board minutes (checked Outline 2026-09-03, found nothing) — the program clearly operates as dual-rostering in practice, but a citable board adoption hasn't been located. See C3 for the pitch-count-aggregation consequence this raises.

## B. Field & equipment

### B1. Base path distance

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 1.04: "The infield shall be a 60-foot square for Little League (Major) division and below." Base path = 60 feet.
- **D8 Interleague:** not specified (standard 60 ft assumed).
- **NCLL:** 60 feet.

### B2. Pitching distance & mound type

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 1.07: distance from the front of the pitcher's plate to the rear point of home plate = 46 feet. Rule 1.04: the pitcher's plate shall be 6 inches above the level of home plate. Rulebook assumes a permanent mound; a portable mound is not prohibited.
- **D8 Interleague:** "The entire game will be kid pitch from a portable mound set at a distance of 46 feet between the front of the pitching rubber and the back corner of home plate."
- **NCLL:** per D8 — 46 ft, portable mound, full kid pitch.
- **Notes:** Portable mound matters for shared/multi-division fields; confirm NCLL has portable mounds at all Majors venues.

### B3. Ball standard

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 1.09: the ball must meet Little League specifications — weight 5 to 5¼ oz, circumference 9 to 9¼ in, LL-approved.
- **D8 Interleague:** "played using a hardball. The home team will provide a minimum of 2 new game balls to the home plate umpire prior to the start of the game."
- **NCLL:** per D8.

### B4. Bat standard

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 1.10: "The bat must be a baseball bat which meets the USA Baseball Bat standard (USABat) as adopted by Little League… Non-wood and laminated bats used in the Little League (Majors) and below… shall bear the USA Baseball logo… All BPF – 1.15 bats are prohibited. The bat diameter shall not exceed 2-5/8 inches for these divisions of play." Minor/Major Divisions: max 33" length, max 2⅝" diameter; if wood, min 15/16" diameter (7/8" for bats under 30") at its smallest part. Solid one-piece wood bats are exempt from the USA Baseball logo requirement. The decertified-model list itself is maintained by USA Baseball, not Little League (LittleLeague.org/BatInfo links out to usabaseball.com/bats/decertified-bats). Checked 2026-09-02: the list has one entry — the 30" Easton Ghost X (-10), models YBB18GX10/LL18GHX (only that length/drop; other Ghost X lengths remain approved) — decertified since 2018, nothing added for 2026. **2026 rule change to flag:** Rule 1.10 Note 2 now PERMITS pine tar or any similar adhesive substance at all Little League levels, reversing the 2025 prohibition; A.R. 2 permits thumb protectors but still bars choke-knobs/choke-up assists. Confirmed 2026-09-02 against LL National's 2026 rule-changes page — no change needed to that conclusion. **Separately, a 2026 equipment change worth flagging here:** Rule 1.11(a)(3) (pitcher's undershirt/sleeve) now covers ALL sleeves, not just neoprene ones — any sleeve worn under the jersey by a pitcher must still be a single solid color, and may not be white or gray. **And a 2025 equipment change also worth flagging here:** Rule 1.11(j) — jewelry is now permitted; the old "must be removed before play" requirement was struck. Medical-alert jewelry has always been allowed regardless.
- **D8 Interleague:** not restated (defers to rulebook).
- **NCLL:** per rulebook.
- **Notes:** Put the "USA Baseball stamp required" line in the coach handout every year, plus the 2026 change: pine tar is now legal (previously it made the bat illegal) — worth calling out explicitly since it reverses last year's guidance. Also flag the Rule 1.11(a)(3) sleeve change and the Rule 1.11(j) jewelry change in the same handout — both reverse older "must remove/can't wear" guidance coaches may still be repeating from memory. Sources: Rule 1.10 + Rule 1.11(a)(3) + Rule 1.11(j) (2025 OCR) + LL's 2026 Significant Rule Changes list + usabat.com/usabaseball.com decertified list, checked 2026-09-02 (confirmed again via the tower session's rulebook-RAG check).

### B5. Home-run distance / outfield line

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 1.04: recommended distance from home to the nearest fence/obstruction in fair territory is 200 feet or more (for Majors). Rule 6.09(d): a fair fly that clears a fence 165 feet or more from home is a home run; if it leaves the field less than 165 feet from home, the batter gets two bases only.
- **D8 Interleague:** "If playing on a non-fenced field, the home run line will be marked by cones (if available) at a distance of 200 feet from home plate."
- **NCLL:** per D8 — 200 ft coned line on non-fenced fields.
- **Notes:** Fenced-field HR minimum in the rulebook is 165 ft; D8's coned line is 200 ft. If any NCLL Majors fence is between 165–200 ft, decide in advance whether over-that-fence is a HR or a ground-rule double. See D8 "HOME RUN BALLS" common rule.

### B6. Helmets / protective equipment

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 1.16 — batting helmets must meet NOCSAE specifications, bear the NOCSAE stamp and an exterior warning label, and have a non-glare (non-mirror-like) surface. Mandatory for the batter, all base runners, and player base coaches; optional for adult base coaches and defensive players. Helmets may not be re-painted and may not contain tape. Stickers and decals ARE permitted "provided that such usage is not excessive, is not offensive, and does not make inappropriate references, such as that to drugs or alcohol" — this was the 2025 change; before that, decals were effectively barred. Standing caution: altering a helmet by painting or adding decals, by anyone other than the manufacturer or an authorized dealer, may void the warranty and/or NOCSAE certification; LL recommends no alterations. A.R.: a player who removes or loses their helmet during play is NOT called out, but is warned; repeated intentional removal can lead to removal for unsportsmanlike conduct. Reg 1.16 also requires each league to provide 6 helmets (7 for Intermediate/Junior/Senior) in the offensive dugout. No 2026 change to Rule 1.16.
- **D8 Interleague:** not restated (defers to rulebook).
- **NCLL:** per rulebook. Team logo decals, player-name stickers, and achievement stickers are fine within the "not excessive / not offensive" limit; no repainting, no tape.
- **Notes:** Verified 2026-09-02 against the 2025 rulebook OCR + littleleague.org's 2026 rule-changes page (no 2026 change to Rule 1.16 itself). See B4 for the two related 2025/2026 equipment changes (Rule 1.11(a)(3) sleeves, Rule 1.11(j) jewelry) folded in there rather than as separate rows.

## C. Pitching

### C1. Pitching format

- **LL Rulebook:** Major division is player-pitch. (Coach Pitch / Machine Pitch are Minor League structures only — Reg I / Minor League section.)
- **D8 Interleague:** full kid pitch, entire game (see B2).
- **NCLL:** per D8 — full kid pitch.
- **Notes:** No decision here for Majors. This row is where the lower divisions diverge — see the comparable-league research in SOURCES.md before setting AA / A.

### C2. Coach/machine mechanics

- **All rows:** n/a for Majors (full kid pitch).

### C3. Pitch-count limits & required rest

- **LL Rulebook:** Reg VI(c) daily max by league age — 13–16: 95, 11–12: 85, 9–10: 75, 6–8: 50. Reg VI(d) rest: 66+ ⇒ 4 days; 51–65 ⇒ 3; 36–50 ⇒ 2; 21–35 ⇒ 1; 1–20 ⇒ 0. NOTE 1: no player may pitch on 3 consecutive days. The rest threshold is set by the FIRST pitch of an at-bat; a pitcher who reaches the daily max mid at-bat may finish that batter but may not start a new batter. Reg VI(k): Majors — a player may not pitch in more than one game in a day. Reg VI(e): each league designates an official pitch-count recorder.
- **D8 Interleague:** "A player may not throw more than 50 pitches in a game prior to May 1st." From May 1 on, the Reg VI chart applies. Where local rule and Reg VI differ, Reg VI controls — EXCEPT the pre-May-1 50-pitch cap.
- **NCLL:** per D8 (Reg VI + the pre-May-1 50 cap). Recorder role: the home team serves as official scorekeeper and maintains the pitch count for BOTH teams; scorekeepers/counters from both teams compare at the end of each inning. Managers exchange pitcher-eligibility info at the pre-game plate meeting (umpires may facilitate but aren't responsible for it). Violations are charged to the offending manager: 1st = warning, 2nd = one-game suspension, 3rd = season suspension.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/majors.html ("Scorekeeping Responsibility," "Pre-Game Meeting," "Violations and Penalties"). The pre-May-1 cap is a genuine D8 addition on top of the rulebook — coaches miss it, call it out at the coach meeting. Reporting channel to the league/VP Baseball (vs. just between the two teams) isn't documented — see Open board decisions. **Added 2026-09-03 (tower session):** Regulation VI pitch limits are per player, per day, across ALL teams a player is rostered to — not per team. Since A3 confirms NCLL dual-rosters Majors Call-Up players across a AAA and a Majors team, a Call-Up player's pitch count needs to be aggregated across both teams' games on the same day, not tracked independently per team. Neither the LL rulebook article nor D8 resolves the mechanism for this — it's a real gap in the AAA/Majors scorekeeping-coordination process this row otherwise documents, and it's specific to Call-Up players (a non-Call-Up player only ever appears on one roster).

### C4. Catcher → pitcher restriction

- **LL Rulebook:** Reg VI(a): a player who has caught 4+ innings in a game is not eligible to pitch that day. A player who caught ≤3 innings, moves to pitcher, and throws 21+ pitches may not return to catcher that day. A.R.: "The catcher receiving one pitch to a batter in the fourth inning constitutes having caught four (4) innings." (Live pitches only; warm-ups don't count.)
- **D8 Interleague:** "Any player that is the defensive catcher for more than three innings is not eligible to pitch that day." + the same "single pitch in a 4th inning" clarification.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook — 21+ pitches. Corrected from Programs/Baseball/majors.html's live "Pitcher-Catcher Relationship" bullet, which says "41 or more pitches"; that number was stale/wrong and should be fixed to 21+ when this page is rebuilt.
- **Notes:** D8's wording restates only the catcher→pitcher (3-inning) rule — no conflict there. "More than three innings" = "4 or more innings" = "one pitch in the 4th." The pitcher→catcher pitch-count number (21+, not 41+) is the one that needs fixing on the live page.

### C5. Walks / HBP / balks

- **LL Rulebook:** Walks and HBP are standard. BALKS ARE NOT CALLED in the Little League (Major) Division and below (Rule 2.00 "Balk", Rule 8.05). An illegal pitch with runners on base results in a "ball" awarded to the batter; runners do not advance unless forced. There is no balk warning system — there is simply no balk.
- **D8 Interleague:** not modified.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook — no balks at Majors; illegal pitch ⇒ ball.
- **Notes:** Correct a common misconception at coach meetings: Majors has NO balk rule (that starts at Intermediate 50-70). Pickoff throws, etc., just can't be illegal-pitch quick returns.

## D. Game play

### D1. Innings for a complete game

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 4.10(a): "A regulation game consists of six innings." Called-game = regulation after 4 completed innings (3½ if home leads) — Rule 4.10(c).
- **D8 Interleague:** "A complete game is defined as 6 innings."
- **NCLL:** per D8 — 6.
- **Notes:** D8/NCLL only restate the 6-inning headline; neither overrides the rulebook's called-game shortcut (Rule 4.10(c)) — a game stopped early by weather, the time limit, or the mercy rule is still official once 4 innings are complete (3½ if the home team is batting and already ahead). That threshold keeps applying by default even though this row doesn't repeat it.

### D2. Time limits

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 4.10(c)(2) Local League Option: a game is regulation once it has been played 1 hour 45 minutes; an inning started before the limit is completed, no new inning begins after it expires. (This is the rulebook's default local option — D8 overrides it with its own clock below.)
- **D8 Interleague:** No new inning after 2:15. No new batter after 2:30. Games past 2:30 revert to the last completed inning. Ties allowed. Doubleheaders: no new batter after 1:50 (game 1) / 2:15 (game 2). Clock starts at scheduled start time.
- **NCLL:** per D8 — no new inning after 2:15, no new batter after 2:30 (games past 2:30 revert to the last completed inning), for interleague and in-house games alike.
- **Notes:** D8's clock is a permitting rule, not a mandate — 2:15/2:30 is the outer ceiling a game is allowed to run to, not a required length. Programs/Baseball/majors.html's live "2 hours or 6 innings" line reads as a rounded, simplified paraphrase of that same ceiling rather than a stricter separate in-house rule; treat the D8 clock above as the authoritative number and tighten the site's wording to match when it's rebuilt.

### D3. Weekday curfew

- **LL Rulebook:** not addressed (local option).
- **D8 Interleague:** Sun–Thu while school is in session: no new inning after 8:30 pm, no new batter after 8:45 pm. Not "in session" during vacations/holidays.
- **NCLL:** per D8 — same curfew applied to in-house games too, for consistency with interleague.
- **Notes:** No NCLL source (majors.html, timeline.html, safety-hub.html) states this explicitly one way or the other; inheriting D8's curfew is the default absent a stated local override.

### D4. Mercy / run-ahead rule

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 4.10(e): game conceded if — 15+ runs after 3 innings (2½ if home ahead) · 10+ after 4 (3½ if home ahead) · 8+ after 5 (4½ if home ahead). NOTE: the local league may adopt the option of not using this rule; if the visiting team hits the margin, the home team still bats its half.
- **D8 Interleague:** Identical tiers: 15+ after 3 (2½ home) · 10+ after 4 (3½ home) · 8+ after 5 (4½ home).
- **NCLL:** per rulebook / D8 — identical, no conflict.
- **Notes:** As of the 2025 rulebook the 15/10/8 tiers are IN the rulebook (Rule 4.10(e)); D8 is not adding anything. NCLL just needs to decide it is NOT opting out.

### D5. Runs-per-half-inning cap

- **LL Rulebook:** NO cap at Majors. The 5-run rule ("Rule 2.00 – Inning") applies only to Minor League and Tee Ball.
- **D8 Interleague:** not specified for Majors (no cap).
- **NCLL:** No half-inning run cap — confirmed by omission. Programs/Baseball/majors.html is NCLL's live, comprehensive Majors rules page and it explicitly lists every other in-house modification (mercy rule, time/inning limit, CBO, free substitution); it includes no per-inning run cap, matching the rulebook/D8's silence.

### D6. Dropped third strike

- **LL Rulebook:** Rules 5.09(b) / 6.09(b): IN EFFECT for Majors — the batter becomes a runner on an uncaught third strike when (1) first base is unoccupied, or (2) first base is occupied with two out. Rule 6.09(b) also carries a Local League Option: a league MAY elect to apply the Minor/Tee Ball rule (batter is out on a third strike caught or not) for the Major division in the regular season.
- **D8 Interleague:** restates the standard rule (IN EFFECT for Majors).
- **NCLL:** Confirmed ON — per rulebook. Programs/Baseball/majors.html's live "Dropped Third Strike" section: "We follow Little League rule 6.05(b)(2) when a third strike is not caught by the catcher." NCLL has not invoked the local option to turn it off; matches D8 interleague default — no mixed-rules confusion.
- **Notes:** D8 turns this OFF for AAA via the same local option — that's the AAA/Majors dividing line, not a D8 invention.

### D7. Stealing / leadoffs

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 7.13 (Major & Minor): NO leadoffs. "base runners shall not leave their bases until the ball has been delivered and has reached the batter." Stealing IS permitted once the ball reaches the batter. Penalty for leaving early: the runner is returned (no out) if the batter doesn't hit; other outcomes per 7.13(a)–(d). No balk/pickoff-leadoff game — see C5.
- **D8 Interleague:** not modified.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook — no leadoff, steal only after the ball reaches the batter.
- **Notes:** (Corrected from an earlier draft that implied leadoffs are allowed — they are not at Majors. True leadoffs start at Intermediate (50-70).)

### D8. Bunting

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 2.00 "Bunt": permitted at Majors, including the drag bunt (drag bunt is named in the Rule 6.03 batter's-box exceptions). Bunts are prohibited only in Tee Ball.
- **D8 Interleague:** not modified.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook.

### D9. Batter's-box rule

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 6.03 — the batter must keep at least one foot in the box throughout the at-bat, with a listed set of exceptions (swing/slap/check-swing; forced out of the box by a pitch; drag-bunt attempt; catcher misses the pitch; a play is attempted; time is called; the pitcher leaves the dirt of the mound or goes 5+ ft from the rubber, or the catcher leaves the box; a 3-ball pitch the batter takes thinking it's ball four). Penalty: warning, then a strike called (any count), ball dead, no advance, no pitch needed.
- **D8 Interleague:** same rule, restated verbatim.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook (D8 restates it — no conflict).
- **Notes:** Not a D8 invention — this is Rule 6.03.

### D10. On-deck / bat safety

- **LL Rulebook:** ⚠ CORRECTED CITE — Rule 1.08 (benches), not 1.11/4.15. Rule 1.08 NOTE 1: "The on-deck position is not permitted in Little League (Major) Division and below." A.R.: "Fenced-in areas MAY NOT be used for an on-deck batter." Rule 1.08 NOTE 2: "Only the first batter of each half-inning will be permitted outside the dugout between half-innings in Little League (Major) Division and below." A.R.: "The next batter should be ready with a helmet on, but may not pick up a bat until it is his/her turn at bat." Reinforced by Regulation XIV(b) (Majors and below stay on the bench/dugout/bullpen when at bat; the on-deck EXCEPTION only starts at Intermediate 50-70/Junior/Senior) and by the tournament rules' separate statement that the on-deck position isn't permitted in the 8-10, 9-11, or Little League tournament divisions. No change to Rule 1.08 for 2026 (the only 1.1x change is Rule 1.11(a)(3), pitcher sleeves — unrelated), so this wording is current.
- **D8 Interleague:** "players are not allowed to hold a bat in the dugout / bench area or swing a bat outside of the batter's box. Practice swings allowed in the batter's box only under the coach's direction."
- **NCLL:** per rulebook + D8 — no on-deck batter, no bats in the dugout, only the half-inning's leadoff batter may be outside the dugout between innings.
- **Notes:** Rulebook already bans the on-deck circle for Majors; D8 restates and adds the "no bat in the dugout" emphasis. Citation corrected 2026-09-02 (was mis-cited as Rule 1.11/4.15) via a comparable-league rulebook-RAG check against the 2025 OCR + the 2026 Significant Rule Changes list.

### D11. Courtesy runner

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 7.14 Local League Option: a "courtesy runner" is permitted for the catcher and/or pitcher of record when there are two outs. With a traditional order, the courtesy runner may not be used more than once per game; with a continuous batting order, the courtesy runner is the player who made the last out.
- **D8 Interleague:** Courtesy runner (last out) permitted for the catcher and/or pitcher of record with two outs. Regular season: any player with a catcher's mask, or a coach, may warm up a pitcher; tournament play — mask-wearing player only.
- **NCLL:** per D8 (adopts the rulebook local option + the D8 warm-up detail).

### D12. Infield fly

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 2.00 "Infield Fly": in effect — a fair fly (not a line drive or bunt) catchable by an infielder with ordinary effort, with runners on 1st & 2nd or bases loaded and fewer than two out.
- **D8 Interleague:** not modified.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook — in effect.
- **Notes:** In effect at Majors; contrast the lower divisions.

### D13. Bat throwing

- **LL Rulebook:** Not separately codified as a numbered rule; governed by the umpire's general authority over player safety and unsportsmanlike conduct (Rules 4.06–4.08, 9.01(d) — see H1).
- **D8 Interleague:** not addressed.
- **NCLL:** 1st offense = warning. 2nd offense = the player is removed from the game for safety — this is a safety ejection, not a ruling that the batter is out on the play; if the batter was already safe when the bat was thrown, that result stands (a non-lineup sub runs the bases, or under CBO the player who made the last out runs). Umpire has discretion over both issuing the warning and enforcing removal.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/majors.html ("Throwing of Bats"). Corroborated by the archived 2024 Division Rules Cheat Sheet and the pre-2018 UIC FAQ — this rule has been stable across at least two eras of NCLL documentation. Formalized 2026-09-02 from the "Historical archive cross-check" section below (was previously undocumented as its own row despite being live on majors.html).

### D14. Headfirst sliding

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 7.08(a)(4): headfirst slides are not permitted in the Little League (Major) Division and below — "the runner slides head first while advancing." The exception for a runner returning to a previously-occupied base to avoid a tag is implied by that "while advancing" limitation (not a separate carve-out clause). Also relevant: the A.R. under Rule 7.08(a)(3) — there is no "must slide" rule; a runner may slide, stand up and try to avoid a tag, or go around a fielder, so long as the runner doesn't run out of the baseline to do so. No 2026 change to either.
- **D8 Interleague:** not addressed.
- **NCLL:** per rulebook — no headfirst slides, except returning to a base. Programs/Baseball/majors.html's live "Sliding" section: "Head-first slides are not allowed. Sliding into home plate is required" (the second clause ties into malicious contact — see D15).
- **Notes:** Corroborated by the archived pre-2018 UIC FAQ. Formalized 2026-09-02 from the "Historical archive cross-check" section below (was previously undocumented as its own row despite being live on majors.html). Rule 7.08(a)(4)/(a)(3) A.R. citation confirmed 2026-09-02 via the tower session's rulebook-RAG check against the current text — no 2026 change to Rule 7.08. **Flag for the live-page rebuild:** majors.html's own "Sliding into home plate is required" wording contradicts the rulebook's A.R. under 7.08(a)(3) ("There is no 'must slide' rule") — the page should be reworded to "slide OR avoid contact" rather than stating sliding is mandatory. See D15 for the same fix.

### D15. Malicious contact

- **LL Rulebook:** A runner may not deliberately or maliciously crash into a fielder (most commonly the catcher at home plate) to jar the ball loose or cause injury; the runner must slide or attempt to avoid the fielder. ## E. Roster & lineup

### E1. Batting order

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 4.04 Local League Option: a league may adopt a continuous batting order (all rostered players present bat in order). CBO is mandatory for Tee Ball and Minor League; it is OPTIONAL for Majors. Absent CBO, the traditional 9-batter order with substitution rules applies.
- **D8 Interleague:** Continuous Batting Order required. "Batting nine" not allowed (except Seniors). Late players go to the bottom of the order.
- **NCLL:** Confirmed adopted for in-house too. Programs/Baseball/majors.html's live "Batting Order" section: "All teams shall use a straight line-up (continuous batting) regardless of whether players are in the field or not. No substitutions or pinch hitting are allowed during the game." Consistent with D8's mandatory CBO for interleague — no split rules. **Clarification:** "no substitutions or pinch hitting" here means the *batting order itself* is fixed once set (CBO — nobody is skipped, benched from batting, or hit for) — it is not a ban on defensive substitution, which is separately addressed (and confirmed free) in E2. Batting order: fixed. Fielding positions: free.
- **Notes:** The E1/E2 pairing can read as contradictory at a glance ("no substitutions" here vs. "free substitution" in E2) — they're not in conflict, they're describing two different things (who bats, vs. who plays where on defense).

### E2. Substitution

- **LL Rulebook:** Rule 3.03: with a traditional order, a starter may be withdrawn and re-enter once, in the same batting slot; a substitute may not be removed before completing mandatory play. - **D8 Interleague:** "'Free' substitution is allowed."
- **NCLL:** per D8 — free defensive substitution (which follows automatically from CBO). This is about fielding position only — the batting order itself stays fixed under CBO (see E1); a player can move freely between defensive positions without affecting their spot in the batting lineup.
- **Notes:** With CBO + free sub, only pitching is restricted by Reg VI re-entry rules.

### H2. Weather & lightning

- **LL Rulebook:** not specified (local option); LL recommends a 30-30 lightning policy.
- **NCLL:** 30/30 rule, explicitly adopted: everyone off the field at first thunder (or within 30 sec of a lightning flash); wait 30 min after the last strike/thunder to resume. Safe shelter = sturdy building or hard-topped vehicle with windows up; dugouts/pavilions/bleachers/sheds are NOT safe. Authority: before first pitch, the league/president has authority (in practice coaches decide together, umpires can advise); after first pitch, the umpire crew chief has sole authority to continue or suspend for weather. Makeup: intra-league — one head coach emails weather@ncllball.com with original date/time/teams, CC both head coaches + division lead; inter-league — leagues agree who reschedules, same email process if it's NCLL's game to reschedule; games are re-slotted in GameChanger, with division-lead help if needed. Non-lightning field closures (city-closed, or a manager/official judgment call) follow a separate process — same page.
- **Notes:** Cite: _Hubs/safety-hub.html ("Weather & Heat" + "Weather & Rainouts" cards, the latter explicitly flagged WIP but already contains this content) + Coaches/weather-rainouts-coach-guide.html (draft, not yet folded into the hub).

### H3. Injury / concussion

- **LL Rulebook:** LL follows state concussion-management law; local return-to-play protocol required.
- **NCLL:** "When in doubt, sit them out." Under WA state law (Lystedt Law, though not named by that name on the page), a suspected-concussion player must be removed immediately (no same-day return), evaluated by a healthcare provider trained in concussion management, and cleared in writing by that provider before returning; families and the Safety Officer are notified right away. Incident reporting: call the Safety Officer by end of game/practice, then file the Incident & Injury Tracking Form within 24 hours (plus the Accident Claim Form if medical care was given). Safety Officer: Michael Fishback, safety@ncllball.com, (206) 940-8463.
- **Notes:** Cite: _Hubs/safety-hub.html ("Concussion Protocol" + "Incident Reporting" cards) — both forms are linked from that page as PDFs.

### H4. Spectators / sideline

- **NCLL:** Parents are encouraged to cheer for their team, but should avoid coaching from the stands or being in the dugout area — no on-field/dugout presence during play. Per the "Sport Parent Code of Conduct" (signed at registration): never confront coaches at the game field (speak with coaches at an agreed-upon time/place instead), respect officials and their authority, and demand a sports environment free from drugs, tobacco, and alcohol (and refrain from their use at all sports events) — on top of the no-coaching-from-the-stands rule above. Consequence: a parent guilty of improper conduct at any game or practice is asked to leave and may be suspended from the following game; repeat violations draw a multi-game or season suspension — the same ladder as the coach side (see H1). Board decision (2026-09-02): NCLL will NOT add a literal "stay behind the fence" rule or a snack-shack volunteer expectation to the Majors page — the general conduct standard above (no coaching from the stands, no confronting coaches, respect officials, no drugs/tobacco/alcohol) is sufficient as written.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/majors.html ("Parent Support") + Outline "Sport Parent Code of Conduct" (Resources, published, id `0802c397-d7f4-4cc4-a8f1-20a06a3ea028`). The fence-rule/snack-shack question was a board decision, not a repo/site citation — declined as unnecessary given the existing standard.

### Lineage — how the current policy evolved (context, not a change)

- **G1 draft** — the 2015 policy (`Majors_Draft_Policy_2015_V6`) ran assessment → single
  composite score → distribute a pitcher/catcher pool first → serpentine by age then score →
  re-align for manager kids / siblings / parity. The current process (redraft, hat order,
  coach-child auto-slot by rating, trade window) is a 2024 replacement — same parity intent,
  different mechanics.
- **G7 call-ups** — the 2015 model was a **Minors→Majors** pool of the *top 3 players per
  team*, rotated by the manager. The current "Majors Call-Up" program is **AAA→Majors** with
  ~15 players dual-rostered at draft time. Completely different structure; current wins.
- **A1 / G3 age caps** — the 2015 policy hard-capped **10s at 2 per team** and admitted **9s
  only by unanimous vote** of four officials. The current approach (per `assessments-faq`) is
  assessment-driven with no published cap. If the board ever wants a hard cap back, the 2015
  numbers are the precedent.
- **F6 reporting** — the pre-2018 UIC FAQ **required** the home manager to send the league
  the score and each pitcher's pitch count within 24 hours. The current row (F6) confirms
  NCLL no longer requires this. Evolution, not conflict — but if the board wants league-level
  tracking back, this is where it used to live.
- **H1 training** — 2015 required managers to complete **three** trainings (Positive
  Coaching Alliance + Safety & Logistics + Skills and Drills) and allowed non-return for
  rule/safety violations or "profoundly negative" parent-survey feedback. The current row
  cites PCA only; the other two and the non-return criteria may still be practiced.

### Do not reuse

- The UIC FAQ's pitch-count numbers (75-pitch cap, 61/41/21 rest thresholds) are **pre-2018
  and wrong**. Reg VI (C3) is authoritative: 85 for 11–12, and the 66/51/36/21 rest bands.
