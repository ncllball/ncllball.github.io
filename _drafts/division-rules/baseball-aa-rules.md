## B. Field & equipment

### B1. Base path distance

- **LL Rulebook:** **60 feet — CONFIRMED**, the standard Minor League diamond (same base paths as Majors/AAA; AA/A/Tee Ball all share this field). Cite: LL Rule 1.04 field diagram, Minor League diamond — Tower, 2026-09-03.
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook — matches A Division's field (see baseball-a-rules.md B1).

### B2. Pitching distance & mound type

- **LL Rulebook:** **46 feet — CONFIRMED**, the Minor League mound distance shared by AA/A/Tee Ball. Cite: LL Rule 1.04 field diagram — Tower, 2026-09-03.
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook — 46 feet from home plate, at the MIDDLE rubber at Lower Woodland specifically (a field-specific note, since Lower Woodland has multiple rubber positions for different divisions sharing the field). All pitchers — both players and coaches — throw from this same distance.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Pitching Position," under Pitching Guidelines).

### B3. Ball standard

- **NCLL:** **An official hardball** — unlike A Division's RIF5 safety ball, AA already uses a real hardball. This is a meaningful equipment/safety step up from A worth flagging clearly for families moving up.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Ball," under Other Considerations).

### B4. Bat standard

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03.** LL Rule 1.10 (Minor/Major Divisions): non-wood bats must bear the USABat (USA Baseball) mark; all BPF-1.15 bats prohibited; ≤33 inches, ≤2⅝-inch barrel; wood bats ≥15/16″ (≥7/8″ for bats under 30″). Same citation closes this row across baseball-a-rules.md/baseball-tball-rules.md/baseball-kindy-rules.md B4. **2026 equipment changes, propagated 2026-09-04 (rulebook-verify pass) — baseball-majors-rules.md B4 already carried these, this doc didn't:** pine tar or any other similar adhesive substance is now permitted on the bat handle/grip at all levels (Rule 1.10 Note 2) — reverses the prior prohibition; may not be applied to the barrel/taper or cover the USA Baseball logo/other identifying marks, penalty for violation. Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not (Rule 1.10 A.R. 2). Separately, Rule 1.11(a)(3): the pitcher's undershirt-sleeve rule no longer singles out neoprene — any sleeve a pitcher wears, exposed or not, must be one solid color and not white or gray. **Genuinely relevant at AA, unlike A** — AA has a real player-pitcher portion (see C1), so a coach-relief AA pitcher could actually wear a sleeve under game conditions. Cite: `2026-little-league-baseball.md` "2026 Significant Updates" (lines 59–63) and the full rule text — Rule 1.10 Note 2 (line 1687), A.R. 2 (line 1697), Rule 1.11(a)(3) (line 1711).
- **NCLL:** Practical fitting guidance: bats approximately 26–30 inches, 15–20 oz (a rule of thumb, not a hard rule per the page's own framing). Same "hold with top hand, move across body without dipping" fitting test as A Division. Watch for oversized "cool" bats — limit use to the actual owner.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Bats," under Equipment Checks).

## C. Pitching

### C1. Pitching format — the core AA Division mechanic

- **LL Rulebook:** Regulation VIII's Coach-Pitch local option — same citation as A1 above — applied here as the **primary format** (unlike A Division's machine-pitch-primary approach; both are the same regulation, different local choice) — see NCLL row.
- **NCLL:** **Coach pitch is primary at AA**, a real step up from A's machine pitch. Team (player) pitchers throw first; coaching philosophy is explicit: "Coach your pitchers with the mindset that we WANT the other team to hit the ball. Success is getting through the game without excessive walks." **Pace-of-play trigger:** if a team pitcher issues 3 consecutive walks or hit-batters in an inning, a coach pitcher finishes that inning (the player pitcher may return once they've completed their required 2 innings — see C2). If no team pitchers are available at all, the coach pitches the whole game to their own team. A pitching coach has two jobs: throw a hittable pitch, and help direct the player "pitcher" (who still stands at the position, even when a coach is actually throwing) to the correct spot. All pitchers — player or coach — throw from the same 46-ft rubber (see B2); players pitch standing, coaches may pitch from a knee.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Throwing Strikes," "Coach Pitch," "Coach as Pitcher," "When a Coach Is Pitching," "Knee or No Knee," under Pitching Guidelines). This is the clearest documented A→AA progression in the whole lower-division set: A is machine-primary/coach-backup, AA is player-primary/coach-backup-mid-inning.

### C2. Coach/machine mechanics

- **NCLL:** A player pitcher who hasn't completed their required 2 innings when a coach takes over mid-inning (see C1) is allowed to return once they have. ⚠ The exact "2 innings" requirement referenced here (a per-pitcher cap? a minimum before rotation?) isn't fully spelled out beyond this one reference — worth confirming the full mechanic.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Coach Pitch," under Pitching Guidelines).

### C3. Pitch-count limits & required rest

- **NCLL:** Managers are responsible for recording pitch counts (usually via the scorekeeper) for ALL pitchers on the team throughout the season, and must be prepared to share that data with the Board on request. Refers generally to "the Baseball Pitch Count by League Age guidelines" and "Baseball Rest Day Thresholds" without restating the actual numbers (unlike AAA/Majors, which cite Reg VI directly).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Pitch Counts," listed twice — once under "Pitching Rules Highlights," once under "Pitching Guidelines," with the Guidelines version adding the Board-reporting detail).

### C3a. Catcher gear & safety *(no equivalent numbering above — found 2026-09-03 during the public rebuild, not previously captured in this doc)*

- **NCLL:** A male catcher must wear a hard protective cup. A catcher's helmet must have a dangling throat protector ("dangler"). Catchers must use a specialized catcher's mitt — an infielder's mitt isn't allowed. Anytime a player is in the crouched catching position — including practices, warm-ups, and sidelines — they need at minimum a mask, chest guard, and cup. A catcher may not block access to the plate until the ball is actually in play to them (the plate-access rule is the same underlying safety principle as the malicious-contact rule at higher divisions — see baseball-majors-rules.md D15 — though aa.html frames it as the catcher's own obligation rather than a runner-conduct rule).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Protective Gear for Male Catchers," "Catcher's Helmets," "Catcher's Mitt," "Safety in the Crouch Position," "Plate Access," under Catcher Rules Highlights). A genuinely detailed catcher-safety section this doc's original C-section pass missed entirely — worth checking whether A Division/AAA/Majors have equivalent unstated detail on their own live pages.

### C4. Catcher → pitcher restriction

- **NCLL:** Same rulebook-wide mechanic as every other division — catches 3+ innings ⇒ can't pitch that game. **FIXED 2026-09-03 in the `_Rules/baseball-aa.html` rebuild:** the old page's "Catcher Restrictions" bullet stated the pitcher→catcher direction as "41 or more" pitches — the same stale number Majors/AAA had before their own corrections. The rebuild now reads 21+, matching every other division.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html (superseded — see `_Rules/baseball-aa.html`) ("Catcher Restrictions," "Catcher to Pitcher Transition," "Pitching and Catching Limitation").

### C5. Walks / HBP / balks

- **NCLL:** Standard balls/strikes rules apply. **FIXED 2026-09-03 in the `_Rules/baseball-aa.html` rebuild:** the old page's "No Balks" bullet read "Balks are not called in Tee Ball" — a copy-paste leftover from the Tee Ball page. The substance (no balks at AA) was already correct; the rebuild now says "AA," not "Tee Ball."
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html (superseded — see `_Rules/baseball-aa.html`) ("No Balks," under Pitching Rules Highlights).

## D. Game play

### D1. Innings for a complete game

- **NCLL:** Standard 3-out innings (unlike Tee Ball's 6-batter cycle) — governed structurally by the time limit rather than a stated inning count (see D2).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Innings," under Hitting/Offensive Rule Highlights).

### D2. Time limits

- **NCLL:** **90-minute time limit or 6 innings, whichever comes first.** 30 minutes of shared field time before the game for warm-ups (though see F7 for the separate, more detailed Pre-Game Warm-Ups section, which describes 15 min each / 30 total — the two sections may be describing the same allocation two different ways; worth reconciling).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Time Limit / Inning Limit," under Game Rules).

### D3. Weekday curfew

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for AA Division.

### D4. Mercy / run-ahead rule

- **NCLL:** **Confirmed: NONE.** "There is NO mercy rule in AA Division play. Play your game to the time limit." A genuine, explicitly-stated divergence from every division above AA.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Mercy Rule," under Game Rules).

### D5. Runs-per-half-inning cap

- **NCLL:** **5-run cap per inning** — same structure as A Division (and the same number AAA uses at the higher tier).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Inning Run Rule," under Game Rules).

### D6. Dropped third strike

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03 — this is the LL default at Minor League, not just an NCLL choice.** Rule 6.09(b) (the third-strike-not-caught → batter-becomes-a-runner mechanic) is scoped to "Majors/Intermediate (50-70)/Junior/Senior League" only. At AA/Minor League, a batter is simply out on strike three, caught or not — the rulebook doesn't give Minor League the dropped-third-strike mechanic at all.
- **NCLL:** **Confirmed OFF**, matching the rulebook default exactly — not a local override. "A dropped 3rd strike is an out (no need to throw to 1B)" — explicitly stated, unlike A Division where this isn't addressed at all. **Foul tips do not count as a third strike** — the batter "stays alive"; a caught foul tip, however, does count as a third strike (this distinction is stated for AA specifically, unlike A Division's Foul Tips row which doesn't carve out the caught-foul-tip exception).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Dropped 3rd Strike," under Hitting/Offensive Rule Highlights; "Foul Tips," under same section).

### D7. Stealing / leadoffs

- **NCLL:** No leadoffs ("There are no lead-offs in Little League baseball for players aged 12 and under" — stated as a general LL fact, not AA-specific). **Stealing is season-split:** no stealing at all in the first half of the season (to let catchers/pitchers develop without runner pressure); second half allows stealing only in specific situations — on a passed ball/wild pitch, or on a dropped return throw to the pitcher (1B→2B, 2B→3B). No stealing home is ever permitted, for safety. A "slow down" convention also applies: a team leading by 5+ runs should have runners advance only on a batted ball, not opportunistically.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Leading Off," "Base Runner Behavior," under Base Running Rule Highlights; "Stealing Bases," "'Slow Down' Mode," under Base Running Guidelines). The most detailed, genuinely AA-specific baserunning structure of any lower division found in this pass — worth preserving in full on the public rebuild rather than simplifying.

### D8. Bunting

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for AA Division.

### D9. Batter's-box rule

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for AA Division specifically.

### D10. On-deck / bat safety

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED** — the on-deck position is not permitted below Majors. Cite: LL Rule 1.08 NOTE 1 — Tower, 2026-09-03 (the same rule that flips to "permitted" starting at Intermediate — see baseball-intermediate-rules.md D10).
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook. No on-deck circle or sideline warm-up "— PERIOD." Bats only in players' hands when they're actually up to bat; on-deck batter stays in the dugout.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("No Bats in Hands Unless Up to Bat," under Hitting; "Bat Storage," under Other Considerations).

### D11. Courtesy runner

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for AA Division.

### D12. Infield fly

- **NCLL:** n/a — not mentioned on aa.html.

### D13. Bat throwing

- **NCLL:** Same warning-then-removal mechanic as every other division.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Throwing of Bats," under Hitting/Offensive Rule Highlights).

### D14. Headfirst sliding

- **LL Rulebook:** **Corrected 2026-09-03 (Tower) — AA is NOT rule-less here; Rule 7.08(a)(4) actually applies and backs NCLL's rule directly.** An earlier draft of this row said AA had "no equivalent LL rulebook rule to conflict with." That was wrong: Rule 7.08(a)(4) is scoped "Little League (Major) and below only" — AA (Minor League) is "below Major," so the rule's scope reaches AA. A runner who slides headfirst while advancing is out.
- **NCLL:** **Confirmed OFF, matching the rulebook exactly — not a standalone local invention.** "No headfirst slides are allowed." Sliding technique generally should be practiced/drilled, and "as a general rule, ALWAYS slide into home plate" — the same "must slide at home" framing majors.md D14/D15 flagged as a live-page wording issue for Majors (conflicting with the rulebook's "no must-slide rule" at the higher divisions) doesn't create a conflict here, since NCLL's "no headfirst" rule and Rule 7.08(a)(4) point the same direction — there's no missing rule and no conflict to flag. Still worth watching the "always slide into home plate" wording if a malicious-contact concept is ever formalized for AA.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Slides," under Base Running Rule Highlights; "Sliding," "Home Plate Rule," under Base Running Guidelines).

### D15. Malicious contact

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated as its own rule for AA Division — see D14's "always slide into home plate" note, which functions similarly in spirit but isn't framed as a malicious-contact prohibition the way higher divisions state it.

## E. Roster & lineup

### E1. Batting order

- **NCLL:** **All teams bat a straight lineup of ALL PLAYERS**, regardless of fielding status — no substitutions or pinch hitting. A player injured mid-game and unable to bat is scratched from the lineup (same CBO-style structure as every other lower/mid division).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Batting Order," under Hitting/Offensive Rule Highlights).

### E2. Substitution

- **NCLL:** Follows from E1's CBO structure (free defensive substitution implied) — not stated as its own separate rule on aa.html.

### E3. Minimum / mandatory play

- **NCLL:** "Managers are encouraged to strive for even playing time in the field during each game" — with an explicit acknowledgment that larger rosters may need inning-parity spread across multiple games rather than guaranteed every single game. Softer, goal-oriented framing than a hard minimum-outs number. On the batting side: coaches should aim to get each player **at least two at-bats**, acknowledged as harder in pitching-duel or shorter 4-5-inning games — same target A Division uses.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Even Playing Time," under Fielding/Defense Rule Highlights; "Number of At Bats," under Hitting/Offensive Rule Highlights).

### E4. Minimum players to start

- **NCLL:** A team may field a defense of 8 (borrowing a 9th player from the opponent for defense only — the borrowed player still represents their own team's offense). By mutual manager agreement, a 10th player may be added as a 4th outfielder. Same flexible-fielding-count philosophy as A Division, framed explicitly as an even-playing-time tool for larger rosters.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Flexible Team Size," under Fielding/Defense Rule Highlights).

### E5. Late-arriving players

- **NCLL:** Added to the end of the batting lineup regardless of the team's current position in the order — same mechanic as every other NCLL division.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Late Arrivals," under Hitting/Offensive Rule Highlights).

## F. Game operations

### F1. Dugout assignments

- **NCLL:** Same as every other NCLL division — home = 3rd base dugout, visitor = 1st base dugout.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Dugout Assignment," under Game Rules).

### F2. Umpiring

- **NCLL:** **AA Division provides its own umpires** — a real step up from A Division's single field-umpire structure. The home team provides the Chief Umpire (positioned behind home plate); the visiting team provides the Field Umpire. If an umpire isn't available at game time, the home team provides one, calling from behind the rubber if lacking gear. The Chief Umpire has final say on all calls.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("The Umpire" section). Structurally closer to Majors/AAA's umpiring model than A Division is — worth noting as another A→AA progression point alongside pitching (C1) and ball type (B3).

### F3. Scorekeeping

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated as its own row beyond the pitch-count-recording mention in C3 (managers/scorekeeper record pitch counts). General scorekeeping mechanics not independently confirmed for AA.

### F4. Adult supervision

- **NCLL:** Same league-wide background-check requirement as every division (see H1).

### F5. Game balls

- **NCLL:** League-provided hardball (see B3); no stated per-game provisioning mechanic beyond that (unlike AAA/Majors, which state "home team provides 2 new balls").

### F6. Score & pitch-count reporting

- **NCLL:** Pitch counts are tracked by managers/scorekeeper and shared with the Board on request (see C3) — no stated inter-team reporting requirement beyond that. **Scores ARE kept** (see G8) but with no standings.

### F7. Pre-game warm-ups / field time

- **NCLL:** Pending field availability, each team gets 15 minutes to warm up before first pitch; visiting team warms up first, home team second (staying on the field to start). If 30 minutes isn't available before the scheduled start, teams agree on a mutual shorter split. Field belongs to the umpire at the scheduled start time — late warm-up requests need umpire approval. A team not ready at its scheduled slot forfeits that warm-up.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aa.html ("Pre-Game Warm-Ups," under Game Rules). ⚠ Cross-check flag: this 15-min/30-min-total structure appears to duplicate/overlap with D2's "30 minutes of shared field time" line under Time Limit/Inning Limit — likely describing the same allocation, worth reconciling into one clear statement on rebuild.
