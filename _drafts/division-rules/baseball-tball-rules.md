## A. Division identity

### A1. Age eligibility (league age)

- **LL Rulebook:** **CORRECTED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit, Tower-verified) — the prior "Regulation V" citation was wrong twice over: it mislabeled Regulation III(c) text as Regulation V, and Regulation III(c) isn't an eligibility rule at all (it's local roster age-structure policy).** The actual eligibility rule is Regulation IV(a), Tee Ball Division: "Any candidate with amateur status who will attain the age of 4 years before January 1 and who will not attain the age of 8 before January 1 of the year in question shall be eligible to compete in the Tee Ball [Division] (subject to the local league Board of Directors alignment of this division)." (The rulebook's own text literally reads "Tee Ball Softball Division" here — an apparent copy-paste artifact in the official document, not something added by this doc.) Players league age 4 are eligible ONLY for Tee Ball unless the Charter Committee approves otherwise (Regulation III(c) — a real citation for that specific sub-claim, not for eligibility generally). Cite: Regulation IV(a), `2026-little-league-baseball.md` line 991.
- **D8 Interleague:** n/a.
- **NCLL:** **NCLL's local minimum is league age 5** — stricter than LL's national floor of 4. This is squarely within Reg V's "local league establishes the age structure" authority — a valid board decision tightening the national floor, already made and cited, not open. 4-year-olds are not eligible to register for NCLL Tee Ball. Registration age band: 5–6 (birth dates Sept 1, 2020 – Aug 31, 2022). "6's with a year of T-ball should consider **KCP (Kindy) or Baseball A**" — i.e., a returning 6-year-old is nudged to move up the ladder (T-ball → Kindy → A) rather than repeat T-ball.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Eligibility" section — already tier-badged on the live page: "Little League Rule: Nationally, Little League allows players as young as league age 4... NCLL Local Rule: The NCLL board has voted to set our local minimum at league age 5.") + _Registration/registration-main.html (age-matrix table, footnote 1).

### A2. Purpose / what this division develops

- **LL Rulebook:** Tee Ball's entire design brief is instructional flexibility — "Rules of the game may be varied to accommodate the need for teaching."
- **NCLL:** "This document provides guidelines for conducting games at the Tee Ball level of play. As a developmental league, our primary goal is to prepare players to progress through the Little League system and eventually participate in the Majors." Framed explicitly as non-competitive (see Final Thoughts/H-section).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html (intro paragraph).

### A3. Participation in two divisions

- **NCLL:** n/a — no mechanism found or plausible at this age band.

## B. Field & equipment

### B1. Base path distance

- **LL Rulebook:** **CORRECTED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit) — 60 feet is the default, but Rule 1.04 gives Tee Ball its own shorter option, not just the shared A/AA/Tee Ball 60-ft diamond.** Rule 1.04: "The infield shall be a 60-foot square for Little League (Major) division and below... Tee Ball option: The infield may be a 50-foot square." Diagram No. 1 carries the same option: "Tee Ball base paths may be 50 feet." NCLL uses the 60-ft default (see NCLL row) rather than the 50-ft option, but the option existing is a real rulebook fact, not a Tee-Ball-specific base path NCLL happens to match by coincidence. Cite: Rule 1.04, `2026-little-league-baseball.md` line 1579.
- **NCLL:** Presumed on the 60-ft default (same Minor League diamond A/AA/Tee Ball share) since tball.html doesn't restate a distance and gives no indication NCLL uses the 50-ft option — not independently confirmed.

### B2. Pitching distance & mound type

- **NCLL:** n/a — there is no pitching in Tee Ball. "There is absolutely no pitching in Tee Ball." Confirmed by omission of any mound/distance discussion on tball.html.

### B3. Ball standard

- **NCLL:** The "Safe-T-Ball," a safety ball engineered specifically for Tee Ball — NCLL-provided, not a standard hardball.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Ball," under Other Considerations).

### B4. Bat standard

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03; CORRECTED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit) — Tee Ball bats have their own 26" cap, a separate spec from the 33" Minor/Major standard, not just "the same bats with a different mark."** LL Rule 1.10, Minor/Major Divisions: non-wood bats must bear the USABat (USA Baseball) mark; all BPF-1.15 bats prohibited; ≤33 inches, ≤2⅝-inch barrel; wood bats ≥15/16″ (≥7/8″ for bats under 30″). Separately, Rule 1.10's own Tee Ball paragraph: "Tee Ball: Under the USABat standard, certified Tee Ball bats (26″ and shorter) will feature the USA Baseball mark and text which reads ONLY FOR USE WITH APPROVED TEE BALLS. NOTE 1: Approved Tee Ball bats may also be used for Coach Pitch/Machine Pitch Minor Divisions only with the use of approved Tee Balls." The 33" figure is the Minor/Major cap, not Tee Ball's own — Tee Ball's certified bats top out at 26". Cite: Rule 1.10, `2026-little-league-baseball.md`. Same 33"/26" distinction applies across baseball-aa-rules.md/baseball-a-rules.md/baseball-kindy-rules.md B4. **2026 equipment changes, propagated 2026-09-04 (rulebook-verify pass):** pine tar or any other similar adhesive substance is now permitted on the bat handle/grip at all levels (Rule 1.10 NOTE 2) — reverses the prior prohibition; may not be applied to the barrel/taper or cover the USA Baseball logo, penalty for violation. Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not (Rule 1.10 A.R. 2) — low practical relevance at Tee Ball's youngest ages, but not excluded by the rule text. The Rule 1.11(a)(3) pitcher-sleeve change is n/a here — Tee Ball has no pitcher position of any kind, live or coach/machine. Cite: `2026-little-league-baseball.md` "2026 Significant Updates" (lines 59–63); full text — pine tar NOTE 2 (line 1687), A.R. 2 (line 1697).
- **NCLL:** Practical fitting guidance: bats approximately 24–25 inches long, 11–16 oz. A batter should be able to hold the bat with only their top hand and move it slowly across their body on a level plane without dipping. Watch for oversized "cool-looking" bats — limit use to the actual owner.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Bats," under Equipment Checks).

### B5. Home-run distance / outfield line

- **NCLL:** n/a — not a meaningful concept at this level; not mentioned on tball.html.

### B5a. Gloves *(equipment-check row, no equivalent numbering above)*

- **NCLL:** Gloves should be **9–11 inches**; check that players' names/phone numbers are on them. Specialty gloves (1st base, catcher) are technically usable but discouraged — usually too large for Tee Ball players.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Gloves," under Equipment Checks — same guidance as A/AA, confirmed via live-site check, 2026-09-03, not previously captured in this doc).

### B6. Helmets / protective equipment

- **LL Rulebook:** Same NOCSAE rules as every other division (rulebook-wide).
- **NCLL:** Inspect helmets for cracks or missing pads. If a player has lice concerns about shared helmets, they may purchase their own — subject to the manager's final say on whether player-owned equipment meets safety standards.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Helmets," under Equipment Checks).

## C. Pitching

### C1–C5 (pitching format, mechanics, pitch counts, catcher restrictions, walks/balks)

- **LL Rulebook / D8 Interleague:** **CORRECTED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit) — "no pitching at Tee Ball" overstates the rule; the rulebook offers three legitimate modes and NCLL specifically chose the tee-only one.** Regulation I(a)(3): "The Tee Ball division is an extension of the local league to accommodate participants league ages 4-7 and may utilize the batting tee or the pitched ball (by a coach). The league may opt to deliver a designated number of pitches to all batters and then utilize the tee if necessary." So the national rule permits tee-only, coach-pitch, or a pitch-count-then-tee hybrid — it's a local option, not an absolute ban on pitching. Cite: Regulation I(a)(3), `2026-little-league-baseball.md`. Note the one other adjacent LL default relevant here: strikeouts are locally optional under Tee Ball's "rules may be varied for teaching" authority (CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03) — see NCLL row for which way NCLL chose.
- **NCLL:** **NCLL chose the tee-only option** of the three Regulation I(a)(3) permits — confirmed: no pitching, no walks ("There are no walks in Tee Ball"), no strikeouts ("There are no strikeouts in Tee Ball" — "The player continues to swing the bat until the ball is put in play") — **this is also NCLL exercising the separate strikeouts-optional local choice, choosing "off."** No catcher restriction concept applies, though catching itself has an optional structure (see D10 note below — actually see Notes here): catching gear is provided, and a player OR the offensive team's own coach (the one setting the ball on the tee) may serve as a de facto catcher, at the team's preference.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Pitching," "Catching," "Balls and Strikes," "Walks," "Strikeouts").

## D. Game play

### D1. Innings for a complete game

- **LL Rulebook:** LL's own Tee Ball default: a half-inning ends when the defense records **3 outs OR the entire batting lineup has batted**, whichever comes first — CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03. This default is exactly what "rules may be varied for teaching" gives NCLL room to replace (see NCLL row).
- **NCLL:** **Locally varied, not the LL default.** Time-based, not innings-based in the usual sense — see D2. The batting mechanic itself (see E1) effectively defines "half-innings" as fixed 6-batter cycles, replacing both halves of the LL default (neither "3 outs" nor "full lineup" governs when a half-inning ends at NCLL).
- **Notes:** A clean example of NCLL exercising its Tee Ball local-variation authority explicitly, not just filling a gap the rulebook leaves open — see also D12/D6a for the other two Tee Ball defaults NCLL varies (outs-recorded, strikeouts).

### D2. Time limits

- **NCLL:** 60-minute time limit or 3 innings, whichever comes first. Teams agree on a mutual warm-up length; visiting team warms up first, home team second (staying on the field to start); managers can waive warm-up time entirely to get more game time.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Time Limit/Inning Limit," under Other Considerations).

### D3. Weekday curfew

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for Tee Ball specifically.

### D4. Mercy / run-ahead rule

- **NCLL:** n/a — "There is no inning run rule in Tee Ball. All players who bat will score" (see E1's 6-batters-then-clear-bases mechanic, which caps runs structurally instead of via a mercy threshold).

### D5. Runs-per-half-inning cap

- **NCLL:** n/a in the usual sense — capped structurally at 6 batters per half-inning (see E1), not via a run-count mercy rule.

### D6. Dropped third strike

- **NCLL:** n/a — no strikeouts exist at Tee Ball.

### D7. Stealing / leadoffs

- **NCLL:** **Confirmed off** — "There is absolutely no stealing or leading off in Tee Ball."
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Stealing/leading off," under Base Running).

### D8. Bunting

- **NCLL:** n/a — not applicable to tee hitting.

### D9. Batter's-box rule

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for Tee Ball.

### D10. On-deck / bat safety

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED** — the on-deck position is not permitted below Majors. Cite: LL Rule 1.08 NOTE 1 — Tower, 2026-09-03 (flips to "permitted" starting at Intermediate — see baseball-intermediate-rules.md D10).
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook. No on-deck circle — "there is no warm-up circle in Little League, so your on-deck batter needs to be in the dugout." Bats stay on the fence OUTSIDE the dugout; "absolutely no bats in players' hands unless they are up to bat (no exceptions)."
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Bat Storage," under Other Considerations).

### D11. Courtesy runner

- **NCLL:** n/a — outs aren't recorded and baserunning is structurally simplified (see D12/E1), so the courtesy-runner concept doesn't apply.

### D12. Infield fly

- **LL Rulebook:** n/a as its own concept — but see D1: LL's Tee Ball default DOES contemplate outs counting toward ending a half-inning (3-out threshold). NCLL's local variation (see NCLL row) removes that entirely.
- **NCLL:** **Locally varied.** No outs are recorded at Tee Ball at all. "Hitters and/or base runners who are 'put out' by the defensive team are to remain on base. Fielders making the out should be acknowledged for their good defensive play" — i.e., a "true" out is celebrated as good defense but doesn't remove the runner/batter from play, and doesn't count toward anything the LL default would use it for.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Outs," under Hitting).

### D13. Bat throwing

- **NCLL:** Same warning-then-removal mechanic as every other division — "one warning" on the first bat throw, removed from the game on a repeat, at the managers' discretion.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Throwing of bats," under Hitting).

### D14. Headfirst sliding

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for Tee Ball specifically — not likely to come up given the "single or more" base-advancement structure (see below), but not confirmed either way.

### D15. Malicious contact

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for Tee Ball specifically.

### D16. Advancing on a hit / advancing on an error *(Tee-Ball-specific rows, no equivalent numbering above)*

- **NCLL:** First half of the season: hitters advance to first base only, regardless of how well the ball is hit. Second half of the season, by mutual agreement between managers: a hitter with a standout hit to the outfield may be allowed an extra base. Errors are not recognized at all — "There is no advancing on an error."
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Single or more," "Advancing on an error," under Base Running).

## E. Roster & lineup

### E1. Batting order — the defining Tee Ball mechanic

- **NCLL:** **Teams bat six players per half-inning, not a fixed roster-order or 3-out structure.** The sixth batter "clears the bases" by running from home plate all the way back to home plate; fielders wait until the last base runner crosses home before clearing the field. This is structurally different from every other division's batting-order mechanic (CBO or bat-nine) — it's a fixed-batter-count cycle, not an order-based or outs-based inning.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Who bats," under Hitting).

### E2. Substitution

- **NCLL:** n/a in the usual sense — with no traditional innings/outs structure, defensive substitution isn't governed by re-entry rules the way it is at higher divisions. Not stated explicitly.

### E3. Minimum / mandatory play

- **NCLL:** Structurally guaranteed by the 6-batters-per-half-inning format (E1) rather than a stated minimum-outs/at-bats rule — every player who's in the field rotation plays, and the batting cycle guarantees at-bats.

### E4. Minimum players to start

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for Tee Ball — teams "can place as many players in the field as they would like" (see F, Fielding), so a strict minimum-to-start concept may not apply the way it does at higher divisions.

### E5. Late-arriving players

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for Tee Ball specifically.

## F. Game operations

### F1. Dugout assignments

- **NCLL:** Same as every other NCLL division — home team takes the 3rd base dugout, visiting team the 1st base dugout.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Dugout Assignment," under Other Considerations).

### F2. Umpiring

- **NCLL:** **Confirmed: no umpires at Tee Ball.** "There are no umpires in Tee Ball."
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Umpire" section).

### F3. Scorekeeping

- **NCLL:** n/a — no outs, no mercy rule, and "all players who bat will score" (see D4) — there's effectively nothing to formally score in the competitive sense, consistent with the "non-competitive... no official scorekeeping" framing in Final Thoughts.

### F4. Adult supervision

- **NCLL:** Same league-wide background-check requirement as every division (see H1).

### F5. Game balls

- **NCLL:** League-provided Safe-T-Ball (see B3); no stated per-game provisioning mechanic beyond that.

### F6. Score & pitch-count reporting

- **NCLL:** n/a — no scoring, no pitching (see D4/C-section).

### F7. Pre-game warm-ups / field time

- **NCLL:** Teams agree on a mutual warm-up length before the game; visiting team warms up first, home team second (staying on the field to start play); managers may waive warm-up entirely for more game time. Post-game: teams should pack up and clear the dugout area promptly, with post-game meetings held off-field, away from the dugout, out of consideration for the next teams.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/tball.html ("Time Limit/Inning Limit," "Post-Game Etiquette," under Other Considerations).
