## A. Division identity

### A1. Age eligibility (league age)

- **LL Rulebook:** **CORRECTED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit, Tower-verified) — "Regulation V" was the wrong citation.** Regulation V is "Selection of Players" (draft methods), not age eligibility. Regulation IV(a), Minor League Division: "Any candidate with amateur status who will attain the age of 7 years before September 1, 2026, and who will not attain the age of 13 before August 31, 2026, shall be eligible to compete in the Minor League Baseball Division (subject to the local league Board of Directors alignment of this division)." AAA sits inside this national Minor League band; NCLL's own AAA/AA/A three-way split is the local league's "alignment" of that division, per this same clause. Cite: Regulation IV(a), `2026-little-league-baseball.md` line 995.
- **D8 Interleague:** League age 11, 10, 9, 8, 7 — CONFIRMED. Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Official Rules Specific to AAA" §1, rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** AAA is primarily a 10s-and-11s division. 9s fill AAA roster spots by assessment when Majors doesn't need them first; 8s are rare but not impossible. 10s and 11s who aren't drafted to Majors are AAA's core. (12s aren't eligible for AAA — league-age-12 must be drafted to Majors, see baseball-majors-rules.md A1.)
- **Notes:** Cite: _Registration/registration-main.html ("Baseball AAA" footnote — "primary division for 10's and 11's," 9's fill roster spots by assessment, 8's rare). This narrows D8's wider 7–11 eligibility band to NCLL's actual practice — worth stating both explicitly so a coach doesn't assume NCLL runs 7-year-olds in AAA just because D8 permits it.

### A2. Purpose / what this division develops

- **NCLL:** AAA is NCLL's full-kid-pitch division below Majors — the step where players who aren't yet drafted to Majors get full-rules, full-diamond baseball (same field dimensions as Majors) with a run cap and a shorter clock as the main concessions to age. Teams are formed by a full annual draft (see G1), and a meaningful share of AAA's own roster is simultaneously eligible to be called up to play Majors games (the "Majors Call-Up" program — see A3/G7). It's the direct feeder into Majors.
- **Notes:** Written 2026-09-03 from facts established elsewhere in this doc plus baseball-majors-rules.md's A2, adjusted for AAA. Not a citation — same treatment as baseball-majors-rules.md A2.

### A3. Participation in two divisions — the Majors Call-Up program

- **LL Rulebook:** **REVISED 2026-09-04 — spelled out with the actual Regulation IV(a) conditions, not just Reg I NOTE 2's headline.** A local league may allow an age-eligible player to participate in two regular season divisions in the same season — but only for Major Division and above (Reg I NOTE 2: "Major/Intermediate (50-70) Division/Junior/Senior League: A local league may allow players to participate in two divisions"). Three conditions all apply, per Regulation IV(a) itself: "Major/Intermediate (50-70) Division/Junior/Senior League: An age-appropriate player, in the Major Division and above, AND who is otherwise eligible under all conditions, would be eligible for selection to a team in two regular season divisions in the same league. A local Little League's Board of Directors determines age structure and may limit player selection and participation to one regular season team/division." That's (1) age-appropriate for both divisions, (2) otherwise eligible under all standard conditions, and (3) the Board must affirmatively allow it — the Board sets age structure and may instead cap a player to one division/team even if they'd otherwise qualify for two. **This allowance does not extend to Tee Ball or Minor League.** Reg I NOTE 2's other half: "Tee Ball/Minor Division: Players shall not participate in more than one division." AAA is a Minor League sub-level, so this exclusion is exactly why NCLL's call-up mechanism (see NCLL row) has to work under a different provision (Regulation V(c) — see below), not this one. For tournament team membership specifically: "A player who is otherwise eligible under all conditions could be eligible for selection to multiple tournament teams; however, a player may only be selected to and participate on one (1) tournament team at a time." Tournament Condition 3 spells out the elimination exception: once a team is eliminated, a player from it "may be added to the affidavit of a second team" if they meet all eligibility requirements, there's affidavit space, and they're not replacing another eligible/available player already on it. Cite: Regulation I NOTE 2, `2026-little-league-baseball.md` line 665; Regulation IV(a), lines 1015 and 1017; Tournament Condition 3, line 3729 (same citation as baseball-majors-rules.md A3). **Separately, Regulation V(c)** ("Alternate method of operation") is the actual governing provision for AAA's own call-up program below, not this dual-roster mechanism: "To aid leagues that are having a difficult time getting enough players for their regular season teams... A pool of players from existing regular season teams can be created with players that are willing to participate in extra games during the regular season when teams face a shortage of rostered players for a regular season game within their respective division or one division below, who are age appropriate and have been assessed capable. Pool players may return to their respective division and all other guidelines must be followed as outlined. EXAMPLE: Minors to Major Division, Major Division to Intermediate (50-70) Division, etc." Governing conditions: (1) "The league's player agent will create and run the pool. The league's player agent will use the pool to assign players to teams that are short of players on a rotating basis"; (2) "Managers and/or coaches will not have the right to randomly pick and choose players from the pool"; (3) "such player will not be permitted to pitch in that game"; (4) "Pool players that are called and show up at the game site must play at least nine (9) consecutive defensive outs and bat once." Cite: Regulation V(c), `2026-little-league-baseball.md` lines 1199–1213.
- **NCLL:** Roughly 15 AAA players per season are designated "Majors Call-Up" at draft time — this is NCLL's implementation of the Regulation V(c) player pool for the AAA-to-Majors direction, the exact direction named in V(c)'s own worked example ("Minors to Major Division"). **Their roster stays solely AAA — they are not dual-rostered onto a Majors team**, consistent with Reg I NOTE 2/Reg IV(a) NOTE 2 excluding Minor League from the dual-roster allowance above; they are called up to individual Majors games as GameChanger availability and need dictate, then return to AAA. Per Reg V(c)'s governing conditions, a Call-Up player may not pitch in a game for the Majors team, and must play at least nine consecutive defensive outs and bat once in any game they're called up for. Cite: Communications/majorcallups.html (program announcement) read together with Regulation V(c) (governing mechanism).
- **Notes:** **RESOLVED 2026-09-04 (citation correction, Regulation V(c))** — this row previously flagged the Majors Call-Up program as an unresolved conflict with Reg I NOTE 2/Reg IV(a) NOTE 2's Minor League dual-roster prohibition. It isn't a conflict: Regulation V(c) is a distinct, purpose-built exception for exactly this scenario (an AAA/Minor League player playing up to fill a Majors shortage), and NCLL's program is that exception in practice, not an instance of the dual-roster rule the LL Rulebook row above still correctly says doesn't apply to Minor League players. **REVISED again 2026-09-04** — the NCLL row's earlier "practice with both their AAA team and a Majors team" phrasing was dual-roster language left over from before the V(c) resolution; corrected to reflect that a Call-Up player's roster stays solely AAA, consistent with Reg V(c)'s own model (a pool player borrowed for one game, not rostered on the borrowing team). Cite: Communications/majorcallups.html (the family notification letter — "your player will officially be on a AAA roster this season... one of 15 to earn the designation of 'Majors Call-Up'") read together with Regulation V(c). Same underlying program as baseball-majors-rules.md A3/G7; this row states it from the AAA roster-of-record side, which the Majors doc doesn't. See C3 for the pitch-count-aggregation consequence dual-rostering raises for Call-Up players.

- **NCLL:** Per D8.

### B4. Bat standard

- **LL Rulebook:** RESOLVED — AAA falls under LL Rule 1.10's "Minor/Major Divisions" bat standard, same as Majors: "It shall not be more than 33 inches in length; nor more than 2-5/8 inches in diameter... Wood bats taped or fitted with a sleeve may not exceed 16 inches from the small end." Plus the general Rule 1.10 requirement: non-wood bats must bear the USABat logo, all BPF-1.15 bats are prohibited, and BBCOR is not permitted at this level (BBCOR is Intermediate/Junior only). So AAA = USABat-stamped, ≤33", ≤2⅝" barrel — identical standard to Majors, not a separate AAA rule. Same pine-tar/thumb-protector/sleeve/jewelry rules as Majors too (rulebook-wide) — see baseball-majors-rules.md B4 for that detail. **Inlined explicitly 2026-09-04 (rulebook-verify pass) — this doc already correctly flagged the cross-reference before the verify pass, unlike baseball-a-rules.md/baseball-aa-rules.md which said nothing; quoting the actual text here too for consistency with those two docs' now-inlined citations:** pine tar or any other similar adhesive substance is now permitted on the bat handle/grip at all levels (Rule 1.10 Note 2) — reverses the prior prohibition; may not be applied to the barrel/taper or cover the USA Baseball logo/other identifying marks, penalty for violation. Thumb protectors are permitted; choke-knobs and choke-up assists are not (Rule 1.10 A.R. 2). Rule 1.11(a)(3): the pitcher's undershirt-sleeve rule no longer singles out neoprene — any sleeve a pitcher wears, exposed or not, must be one solid color and not white or gray; relevant here since AAA has a full kid-pitch mechanic. Cite: `2026-little-league-baseball.md` "2026 Significant Updates" (lines 59–63) and the full rule text — Rule 1.10 Note 2 (line 1687), A.R. 2 (line 1697), Rule 1.11(a)(3) (line 1711).
- **D8 Interleague:** Not addressed — CONFIRMED. No AAA-specific bat-length rule beyond USABat/LL Rule 1.10 exists in the D8 AAA supplement; its only bat-adjacent rule is the dugout-safety one (see D10).
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook. Programs/Baseball/aaa.html's "27-31 inches... 15-20 oz" range is coach-facing fitting advice within the rulebook's 33"/2⅝" ceiling, not a separate binding local rule.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aaa.html ("Bats" under Equipment checks) + LL Rule 1.10. **Closed 2026-09-03** — not a board decision; the rulebook already answers it.

## C. Pitching

### C1. Pitching format

- **LL Rulebook / D8 Interleague:** Full kid pitch, same as Majors. Per `_division-deltas.md`, unchanged for AAA.
- **NCLL:** Per D8 — full kid pitch.

### C3. Pitch-count limits & required rest

- **LL Rulebook:** Same league-age-based Reg VI chart as Majors (a pitcher's limit is set by their own league age, not their division) — see baseball-majors-rules.md C3 for the full chart. **Reporting baseline RESOLVED** — Reg VI(e)–(g) requires the league to designate the scorekeeper or another game official as the official pitch-count recorder; that recorder must provide the current count to either manager on request; and the recorder should notify the UIC when a pitcher hits the max. This is a rulebook requirement, not something NCLL invents from scratch.
- **D8 Interleague:** "May not throw more than 50 pitches in a game prior to May 1st" — CONFIRMED, identical to Majors. D8 adds nothing on the reporting mechanism itself — that's purely Reg VI(e)–(g) plus NCLL's own tooling choice. Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball §7 (+ LL Regulation VI), rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** ⚠ Narrowed — Reg VI(e)–(g) settles *that* a recorder must exist and report on request; what's still open is purely NCLL's local workflow (GameChanger vs. paper affidavit, who the recorder is, whether it's submitted to the Player Agent, retention) and whether AAA uses the same manager-side violation-penalty schedule (warning / 1-game / season suspension) majors.html documents for Majors. Programs/Baseball/aaa.html (legacy) doesn't state a mechanism at all — gap.
- **Notes:** » NCLL DECISION (narrowed): the rulebook baseline is settled; only AAA's specific recording workflow and violation-penalty schedule remain open — pull from Outline/past-season practice or flag for the board/registrar. **Added 2026-09-03:** Reg VI limits are per player per day across ALL teams — a Majors Call-Up player (see A3) needs their pitch count aggregated across their AAA and Majors games on the same day, not tracked independently per team. No mechanism for this exists anywhere documented; same open gap as baseball-majors-rules.md C3, not duplicated as a separate open-decision item here.

### C4. Catcher → pitcher restriction

- **LL Rulebook / D8 Interleague:** Same as Majors — catch 4+ innings ⇒ can't pitch that day; pitch 21+ ⇒ can't move to catcher. This is a league-wide rulebook mechanic, not division-specific. **ADDED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit, Tower-verified) — same finish-the-batter exception as majors:** Reg VI(a) EXCEPTION: reaching the 20-pitch limit mid-batter preserves catcher eligibility until that batter reaches base, is retired, the half-inning/game ends, or the pitcher is pulled first. Cite: Regulation VI(a), `2026-little-league-baseball.md` line 1225 (same national text as baseball-majors-rules.md C4).
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook — a pitcher who reaches 21 or more pitches may not catch the remainder of the game. **FIXED 2026-09-03 in the `_Rules/baseball-aaa.html` rebuild:** the old page's "Catching" bullet said "41 or more," the same stale number Majors had before its own correction.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aaa.html (superseded — see `_Rules/baseball-aaa.html`).

### C5. Walks / HBP / balks

- **LL Rulebook:** No balk is called in the Little League (Major) Division and below — CONFIRMED, so AAA is well inside "no balk" territory (that band starts at Intermediate 50/70). An illegal pitch with runners on is a dead ball/ball awarded to the batter, no advance unless forced. Cite: LL Rules 2.00 (BALK / ILLEGAL PITCH) + 8.05 PENALTY (Major/Minor). Neither D8 supplement (Majors or AAA) mentions balks — this is purely rulebook-governed.
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook — no balks at AAA.
- **Notes:** Programs/Baseball/aaa.html's legacy "Intentional Walks" bullet ("must be completed with thrown pitches, count toward pitch count") reads as a genuine AAA-specific clarification, not standard rulebook text — worth keeping on the public page even though it's not a division-identity difference.

## D. Game play

### D1. Innings for a complete game

- **LL Rulebook / D8 Interleague:** Per `_division-deltas.md`: 6 innings, same as Majors.
- **NCLL:** Per D8 — 6.

### D2. Time limits

- **D8 Interleague:** CONFIRMED — "No new inning can be started after 2 hours. No new batter after 2 hours and 15 minutes." Reverts to the last complete inning; ties allowed. Doubleheaders: no new batter after 1:50 (game 1) / 2:15 (game 2) — same as Majors. Tighter than Majors' 2:15/2:30. Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Local Rules That May Differ" §1, rev. 2024-03-22 (⚠ 2024 vintage — current published version, no 2026 update issued yet).
- **NCLL:** Per D8 — no new inning after 2:00, no new batter after 2:15, for interleague and in-house games alike.

### D3. Weekday curfew

- **D8 Interleague:** Same as Majors — CONFIRMED. Sunday through Thursday while school is in session: no new inning after 8:30 pm, no new batter after 8:45 pm. Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Local Rules That May Differ" §2, rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** Per D8 — same curfew applied to in-house games too, for consistency with interleague.

### D4. Mercy / run-ahead rule

- **LL Rulebook / D8 Interleague:** Per `_division-deltas.md`: "same as Majors" — 15+ after 3 (2½ home), 10+ after 4 (3½ home), 8+ after 5 (4½ home). **ADDED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit)** — same national opt-out as majors: Rule 4.10(e) NOTE 2, "the local league may adopt the option of not using this rule."
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook/D8 — same tiers as Majors.
- **Notes:** **FIXED 2026-09-03 in the `_Rules/baseball-aaa.html` rebuild:** the old page's "Mercy Rule" section stated a flat "10-run after 4 innings" — the same simplified/stale wording Majors had before its own D4 correction. The rebuild now states the real tiered 15/10/8 thresholds. Cite: Programs/Baseball/aaa.html (superseded — see `_Rules/baseball-aaa.html`).

### D5. Runs-per-half-inning cap

- **LL Rulebook:** CONFIRMED — there is no per-inning run cap anywhere in the LL rulebook at any level. The rulebook's only run-differential rule is the mercy/10-run rule (Rule 4.10(e)), which D8 already replaces with its own 15/10/8 tiers (see D4). AAA's 5-run cap has no rulebook basis at all — it's purely a district interleague decision (see D8 row).
- **D8 Interleague:** CONFIRMED, full mechanism — D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Local Rules That May Differ" §6: "The side retires after 3 outs, when 5 runs score, or when the entire batting order has batted, whichever comes first." Both teams bat the larger roster's full order. **Exception 1:** a home run over the fence/line, or a ground-rule double, counts all runs driven in even past the 5-run cap. **Exception 2:** in the 6th inning and later, there's no 5-run limit, but runs are capped at one time through the batting order. Majors' D8 supplement has no equivalent section at all — D8 wrote a per-inning cap into the younger division's supplement and chose not to for Majors. Rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** **5-run cap per half-inning is in effect**, matching the D8 mechanism above — confirmed live on Programs/Baseball/aaa.html ("Inning Run Rule": "We play with a 5-run rule per inning... Once five runs are scored, the inning is over"), with an explicit exception: the cap does not apply in the final inning ("Exception to Inning Run Rule (6e)... not valid in the final inning of a game"). The live page's single "final inning" exception is a simplified version of D8's fuller "6th inning and later, capped at one time through the order" rule — worth tightening to match D8's actual wording when the page is rebuilt, rather than a flat exemption.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aaa.html ("Inning Run Rule," "Exception to Inning Run Rule") + D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball §6. This is the clearest AAA/Majors divergence in section D — Majors has NO run cap (D5), AAA has a 5-run cap with home-run/ground-rule-double and late-inning exceptions, and it exists solely because D8 chose to write it for the younger division, not because of any rulebook requirement.

### D6. Dropped third strike

- **LL Rulebook:** LL Rule 6.09(b) — a batter becomes a runner on an uncaught third strike when first base is unoccupied, or occupied with two out (same rule baseball-majors-rules.md D6 cites). D8's AAA supplement flatly overrides this rule for AAA (see D8 row).
- **D8 Interleague:** **NOT in effect for AAA** — CONFIRMED. Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Official Rules Specific to AAA" §7: "DROPPED 3RD STRIKE: Not in effect for the AAA division." This is a direct D8 override of LL Rule 6.09(b); Majors' D8 supplement §7 instead keeps 6.09(b) in effect. Rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** Per D8 — dropped third strike is OFF at AAA. A third strike is simply an out, whether caught or not.
- **Notes:** This is the clearest AAA/Majors rules divergence in the pitching/hitting interaction — make sure the public AAA page states this explicitly rather than silently omitting the Majors language, since a coach moving up from AAA to Majors (or vice versa) needs to know this flips.

### D7. Stealing / leadoffs

- **LL Rulebook:** Same Rule 7.13 basis as Majors (no leadoffs, steal once the pitch reaches the batter) — this is a Major/Minor-wide rule per baseball-majors-rules.md D7's own citation ("Rule 7.13 (Major & Minor)").
- **D8 Interleague:** **AAA-specific restriction Majors does NOT have — delayed steals are not allowed.** CONFIRMED — a runner may steal only (a) a straight steal once the ball crosses the plate, (b) on a wild pitch or passed ball, or (c) on an overthrow back to the pitcher. Once the pitcher has the ball back, the play is dead and a runner in a secondary lead must return to their base. Majors has standard stealing with no such restriction. Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Local Rules That May Differ" §5, rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook/D8 — no leadoff, steal only after the ball reaches the batter, and no delayed steals (see D8 row for the three legal triggers). Confirmed live on Programs/Baseball/aaa.html ("Stealing is allowed in the AAA Division... no leading off") — the live page doesn't currently mention the delayed-steal restriction; worth adding when the page is rebuilt.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/aaa.html ("Stealing / Leading Off") + D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball §5. Second of three genuinely AAA-specific rules Majors lacks (with D5's run cap and D12's lenient infield fly standard).

### D11. Courtesy runner

- **LL Rulebook / D8 Interleague:** **CLARIFIED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit) — this is a Local League Option (Rule 7.14(b)), not an automatic national rule**, same as majors: "A local league may permit a 'courtesy runner' for the catcher and/or pitcher of record when there are two (2) outs." AAA Baseball, common rules §7, rev. 2024-03-22 (part of the "COMMON RULES" block that's word-for-word identical between the Majors and AAA supplements).

### D12. Infield fly

- **LL Rulebook:** Same Rule 2.00 "Infield Fly" definition as Majors (league-wide) — runners on 1st & 2nd or bases loaded, fewer than two out, a catchable fair fly is an automatic out.
- **D8 Interleague:** **AAA gets its own, more lenient local version — third of the genuinely AAA-specific rules.** CONFIRMED — D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball, "Local Rules That May Differ" §4: the infield fly rule is in effect (narrower trigger than standard: 1st & 2nd occupied, 0–1 out, fair catchable fly, not a liner/bunt → batter out, ball stays live), **but umpires should invoke it only when the ball "more likely than not" will be caught in the infield with little effort.** Majors' D8 supplement doesn't restate infield fly at all — Majors runs on the plain LL Rule 2.00/6.05(e) standard, no leniency instruction and the broader "1st & 2nd OR bases loaded" trigger. Rev. 2024-03-22.
- **NCLL:** Per D8 — in effect at AAA, with the narrower trigger (1st & 2nd only, not bases-loaded) and the "more likely than not" leniency instruction to umpires. Not found stated on Programs/Baseball/aaa.html at all — a genuine gap on the live page, since this differs enough from the standard rule that a coach/umpire should be told explicitly.
- **Notes:** Cite: D8 Suppl. AAA Baseball §4. This nuance is easy to lose if AAA's doc is ever "cloned down" from Majors without checking — Majors' silence on infield fly means the standard rule; AAA's local rule is genuinely narrower/more lenient, not identical-by-silence. **CORRECTED 2026-09-04 (Codex audit, Tower-verified) — the public page (`_Rules/baseball-aaa.html`) states the standard infield fly rule "applies at Majors," implying it's a Majors-only national rule. It isn't.** Rule 6.05(d) is a national rule with only one exemption: "NOTE 2: The Infield Fly Rule does not apply in Tee Ball." Minor League/AAA is not exempt — D8's narrower/more-lenient version replaces the standard trigger at AAA specifically, it doesn't opt AAA out of infield fly altogether the way the "applies at Majors" phrasing could be read. Cite: Rule 6.05(d), `2026-little-league-baseball.md` line 2823; NOTE 2 (Tee Ball exemption), line 1946.

### D15. Malicious contact
