## A. Division identity

### A1. Age eligibility (league age)

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03.** Regulation V: "Local league must establish the age structure for the Minor League Division. The Minor League may be sub-divided into Minor League Coach Pitch, Minor League Machine Pitch, and/or Minor League Player Pitch divisions." Plus Regulation VIII(a): the program is for players who "by reason of age and other factors do not qualify for selection in the regular Little League"; VIII(d): league ages 7–12 nationally (5–6 permitted after one year of Tee Ball, per Reg V's Tee Ball exception — see baseball-tball-rules.md A1). NCLL's A=7–8/AA=8–9 split is exactly the "local league establishes the age structure" authority in action — a valid board choice, not a documentation gap.
- **D8 Interleague:** n/a.
- **NCLL:** **7–8 is the target band**, with 6-year-olds who've completed a year of T-ball encouraged to register. Registration age band: 7–8 (birth dates Sept 1, 2018 – Aug 31, 2020).
- **Notes:** Cite: _Registration/registration-main.html (age-matrix table, footnote 3 — "Baseball A (7-8) uses a coach-pitch and machine pitch blend with hardball and is for 7's/8's, but 6's with a year of T-ball are encouraged to register.").

### A2. Purpose / what this division develops

- **LL Rulebook:** Minor League exists "to prepare children for eventual selection to a Major Division team" (same Reg I framing majors.md A2 cites) — A Division is the entry point into that ladder.
- **NCLL:** "Following are guidelines on conducting games at the A Division level of play. Please keep in mind we are a development league and our primary goal is to prepare our players to move up the Little League ladder and eventually play in the Majors."
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html (introduction).

### A3. Participation in two divisions

- **NCLL:** n/a — no mechanism found or plausible at this age band.

## B. Field & equipment

### B1. Base path distance

- **LL Rulebook:** **60 feet — CONFIRMED**, the standard Minor League diamond (same base paths as Majors/AAA; A/AA/Tee Ball all share this field). Cite: LL Rule 1.04 field diagram, Minor League diamond — Tower, 2026-09-03.
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook — not independently restated on a.html.

### B2. Pitching distance & mound type

- **LL Rulebook:** **46 feet — CONFIRMED**, the Minor League mound distance shared by A/AA/Tee Ball. Cite: LL Rule 1.04 field diagram — Tower, 2026-09-03.
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook. The pitching machine sits "just in front of the closest pitching rubber to home plate" (i.e., just in front of that 46-ft mark) — adjustments possible depending on the specific machine.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Placement of the Pitching Machine").

### B3. Ball standard

- **NCLL:** **The Official RIF5 safety ball** — a squishy, reduced-impact-force ball designed for young players, not a standard hardball.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Ball Type," under Other Considerations).

### B4. Bat standard

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03.** LL Rule 1.10 (Minor/Major Divisions): non-wood bats must bear the USABat (USA Baseball) mark; all BPF-1.15 bats prohibited; ≤33 inches, ≤2⅝-inch barrel; wood bats ≥15/16″ (≥7/8″ for bats under 30″). Same citation closes this row across baseball-aa-rules.md/baseball-tball-rules.md/baseball-kindy-rules.md B4.
- **NCLL:** Practical fitting guidance: bats approximately 24–27 inches, 13–17 oz. A batter should be able to hold the bat with only their top hand and move it slowly across their body without dipping. Watch for oversized "cool-looking" bats — limit use to the actual owner.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Bats," under Equipment Checks — this is the *second* Bats entry on the page, the fitting-guidance one; the first, under Other Considerations, just states the 2¼" barrel rule).

### B5. Home-run distance / outfield line

- **NCLL:** n/a — not a meaningful concept at this level; not mentioned on a.html.

### B5a. Gloves *(equipment-check row, no equivalent numbering above)*

- **NCLL:** Gloves should be **9–11 inches**; check that players' names/phone numbers are on them. Specialty gloves (1st base, catcher) are technically usable but discouraged — usually too large for A-level players.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Gloves," under Equipment Checks — confirmed via live-site check, 2026-09-03; same guidance as baseball-aa-rules.md, not previously captured in this doc).

### B6. Helmets / protective equipment

- **LL Rulebook:** Same NOCSAE rules as every other division (rulebook-wide).
- **NCLL:** Check helmets for cracks or missing pads. Lice concerns → a player may purchase their own helmet, subject to the manager's final say on player-owned equipment.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Helmets," under Equipment Checks).

## C. Pitching

### C1. Pitching format — the core A Division mechanic

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED via Tower, 2026-09-03 — this is the entire machine spec in the rulebook, no more.** Regulation VIII(d), full text: "A local league may elect to utilize adult pitch ('Minor League Coach Pitch') or machine pitch ('Minor League Machine Pitch') in Minor League games involving players league ages 7-12. Pitching machines, if used, must be in good working order and must be operated by adult managers, coaches, umpires, or any adult approved by the local league." No velocity, distance, or model requirements — NCLL's UPM 45 / speed / arc detail (see NCLL row) is entirely NCLL's own, not rulebook-mandated.
- **NCLL:** **Spring-arm mechanical pitching machines** — NCLL's specific model is the Louisville Slugger UPM 45 "Blue Flame" (Game Master). A coach or parent from the batting team operates the machine via a foot pedal that releases the spring-loaded arm. Only coaches or parents should operate it. Coaches adjust velocity/trajectory by player skill level, aiming for a slight arc landing just behind home plate — avoiding the "rainbow pitch." Home-team manager configures the machine before the game, typically at slower speeds with a moderate trajectory. **Backup, if the machine is broken/unavailable:** a coach or parent pitches to their own team, standing at the same rubber position.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Pitching Machine Operation," "Placement of the Pitching Machine," "Pitching Machine Setup," "Flight of the Ball," "Knee or No Knee," "Extended Rules for Pitching Machine Issues") + the manufacturer manuals/videos linked on the live page. **Cross-check RESOLVED 2026-09-03 (live-site check):** _Registration/registration-main.html's own footnote 3 describes A Division as "a coach-pitch and machine pitch blend" — a.html's live rules, confirmed unchanged from the repo copy, describe machine pitch as the primary/default format with coach-pitch only as a backup when the machine fails. registration-main.html is the outlier here, not a.html — worth a quick copy fix on that page specifically.

### C2. Coach/machine mechanics

- **NCLL:** Batted ball striking the pitching machine stays in play (ball remains live). If a team pitcher (in the coach-pitch backup scenario — see C1) issues 3 consecutive walks or hit-batters in an inning, standard pace-of-play concerns would apply, though a.html doesn't state a specific "coach finishes the inning" rule for A Division the way aa.html does for AA (see baseball-aa-rules.md C2 note) — not directly stated for A.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Batted Ball Off the Pitching Machine").

### C3–C5 (pitch counts, catcher restrictions, walks/balks)

- **NCLL:** n/a — no pitch-count or catcher-pitch-count restriction applies to machine pitch (there's no player pitcher to protect). Catching is provided by the defensive team; coaches ensure the catcher is in a squatting position facing the machine before each pitch (a suggestion, not a hard rule, per the live page's own tier badge on that row).
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Catching," "Catcher Position").

## D. Game play

### D1. Innings for a complete game

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not explicitly stated as an inning count on a.html — governed by the time limit instead (see D2).

### D2. Time limits

- **NCLL:** **75-minute time limit** — no new inning may start after that, whichever comes first. (a.html's own badge notes: "Little League doesn't set a fixed time limit at this level; NCLL sets its own.") For warm-ups before scheduled field time, teams should warm up in the deep outfield.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Time Limit and Inning Limit," under Other Considerations).

### D3. Weekday curfew

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division.

### D4. Mercy / run-ahead rule

- **NCLL:** n/a as a separate mercy threshold — see D5's 5-run inning cap, which functions as the de facto run-differential control at this level instead of a game-ending mercy rule.

### D5. Runs-per-half-inning cap

- **NCLL:** **5-run cap per inning** — once 5 runs score, the inning ends and the batting team returns to the field.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Inning Run Rule," under Other Considerations).

### D6. Dropped third strike

- **NCLL:** n/a — see C-section: there's no traditional strikeout structure the way full kid-pitch divisions have one; "Strikeouts" at A Division are their own row (see D6a below) but don't interact with a dropped-third-strike mechanic since there's no catcher-drop consequence in machine pitch.

### D6a. Strikeouts / courtesy strike *(A-Division-specific row, no equivalent numbering above)*

- **NCLL:** Three swinging strikes = an out; batters must swing at the third strike (no called third strike, consistent with machine pitch having no true "balls"). **Foul tips do not count as a third strike** — the batter stays alive at the plate (confirmed via live-site check, 2026-09-03 — same rule AA states, not previously captured in this doc). **Courtesy strike option (a suggestion, not a rule):** for players needing more development time, consider a 4-strike count for the whole team early in the season, transitioning to 3-strikes as the season progresses; discuss with the opposing manager before each game. Discontinue the courtesy strike once a team significantly outperforms its opponent, or builds a 6+ run lead.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Strikeouts," "Courtesy Strike," "Discontinuing Courtesy Strikes," "Foul Tips").

### D7. Stealing / leadoffs

- **NCLL:** n/a — no stealing or leading off; not addressed as its own row on a.html, though the base-running section's overall framing (single-or-more, base coaches) implies a controlled, no-steal structure consistent with T-ball/AA below-Majors norms. ⚠ Not explicitly stated as its own "no stealing" rule the way tball.html/aa.html state it outright — worth confirming.

### D8. Bunting

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division.

### D9. Batter's-box rule

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division specifically.

### D10. On-deck / bat safety

- **LL Rulebook:** **CONFIRMED** — the on-deck position is not permitted below Majors. Cite: LL Rule 1.08 NOTE 1 — Tower, 2026-09-03 (flips to "permitted" starting at Intermediate — see baseball-intermediate-rules.md D10).
- **NCLL:** Per rulebook. No warm-up circle — the on-deck batter should be in the dugout; bats stay on the fence OUTSIDE the dugout.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Bat Storage," under Other Considerations).

### D11. Courtesy runner

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division.

### D12. Infield fly

- **NCLL:** n/a — not a meaningful concept at this level; not mentioned on a.html.

### D13. Bat throwing

- **NCLL:** Same warning-then-removal mechanic as every other division — one warning, then removal from the game for safety on a repeat, at the umpire's/managers' discretion.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Bat Throwing," under Hitting).

### D14. Headfirst sliding

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division specifically.

### D15. Malicious contact

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division specifically.

### D16. Advancing on base hits / errors *(A-Division-specific rows)*

- **NCLL:** Real base-running is taught here (unlike T-ball) — not every hit is a single; a legitimate double earns two bases, a triple earns three. Get out of the box quickly and look to the base coach. **Advancing on an error:** encouraged to take just one base even when more might be possible, as a courtesy to the opposing manager — limiting this "helps" the defense's development. Break the habit of watching the ball roll; look to the base coach instead.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Single or More," "Advancing on an Error," under Base Running).

## E. Roster & lineup

### E1. Batting order

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated explicitly as a batting-order row on a.html — likely CBO-style (everyone bats), consistent with every other lower division, but not confirmed independently for A.

### E2. Substitution

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated explicitly for A Division.

### E3. Minimum / mandatory play

- **NCLL:** "Aim for equal innings in both the outfield and infield for all team members. At the A Division level, there's no need for position specialization." Framed as a goal ("aim for"), not a hard minimum-outs rule the way higher divisions state one. On the batting side (confirmed via live-site check, 2026-09-03, not previously captured): coaches should aim to get each player **at least two at-bats**, acknowledged as harder to hit in pitching-duel or shorter 4-5-inning games.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Player Rotations," under Fielding; "Number of At Bats," under Hitting).

### E4. Minimum players to start

- **NCLL:** **A team may field 10 defensive players** (one extra beyond the standard 9), with the extra player restricted to the outfield (left, left-center, right, or right-center). It's acceptable for a 10-fielder team to play against a 9-fielder opponent — "this ensures more playing time without significantly affecting competitiveness." This is a *maximum-flexibility* provision, not a minimum-to-start rule — a.html doesn't state a minimum-players-to-start threshold the way higher divisions do.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Number of Players in the Field," under Fielding).

### E5. Late-arriving players

## F. Game operations

### F1. Dugout assignments

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated explicitly on a.html itself, though every other NCLL division uses home=3rd-base/visitor=1st-base — likely the same, not independently confirmed for A.

### F2. Umpiring

- **NCLL:** **The defensive (fielding) team provides a field umpire** — a coach or parent, positioned around 2nd base, with final say on field calls. The coach who is pitching (see C1) is responsible for tracking strikes during play; the coach-pitcher and field umpire should confer on disputed calls. This is structurally different from every division above A, where the home team/league provides a dedicated plate umpire — A Division has no plate umpire at all, just one field umpire from the defense.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Field Umpire," "Coach/Pitcher's Role," under Umpire).

### F3. Scorekeeping

- **NCLL:** ⚠ Not stated for A Division specifically.

### F4. Adult supervision

- **NCLL:** Same league-wide background-check requirement as every division (see H1).

### F5. Game balls

- **NCLL:** League-provided RIF5 safety ball (see B3); no stated per-game provisioning mechanic beyond that.

### F6. Score & pitch-count reporting

- **NCLL:** n/a — no pitch counts at this level (machine pitch). **RESOLVED 2026-09-03 (live-site check):** A Division does **NOT** keep score at all — "Even though we don't keep score, there will always be individual and team moments" (Final Thoughts section). This is a genuine divergence from AA, which explicitly keeps score but has no standings (see baseball-aa-rules.md F6/G8) — A is one step further removed from competitive structure than AA.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html / live ncllball.com A Division page ("Final Thoughts," "Focus on the Positive" — confirmed via crawl4ai live-site check, 2026-09-03).

### F7. Pre-game warm-ups / field time

- **NCLL:** Each team gets 10 minutes to warm up before first pitch; visiting team warms up first, then home team. If 20 minutes isn't available before the scheduled start, teams agree on a shorter warm-up.
- **Notes:** Cite: Programs/Baseball/a.html ("Pre-Game Warm-Ups," under Other Considerations). Notably shorter than AA's 15-min/30-min-total structure and Majors/AAA's 15-min/30-min structure.
